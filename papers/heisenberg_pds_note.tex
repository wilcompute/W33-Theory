\begin{abstract}
\noindent Fix a point $p_0$ of the symplectic generalized quadrangle $W(3,q)$,
$q$ odd. We prove three structural results about the $q^3$ points opposite
$p_0$, each with an elementary proof and a machine-checked certificate:
(1) their collinearity graph is a distance-regular antipodal $q$-fold cover of
$K_{q^2}$ whose fibers are the central elation orbits; (2) adjoining the fiber
pairing yields a strongly regular graph
$\mathrm{SRG}(q^3,(q-1)(q+2),q-2,q+2)$, recovering at $q=3$ the unique
$\mathrm{SRG}(27,10,1,5)$ of the twenty-seven lines; and (3) the corresponding
connection set is a partial difference set in the extraspecial group
$q^{1+2}_+$ of exponent~$q$, whose parameters at $q=3$ admit \emph{no abelian
partial difference set in any group}. The obstruction is a complete
Sylow-level enumeration: the elementary abelian group of order $27$ embeds in
the relevant automorphism group but never acts freely.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

Let $V=\FF_q^4$ carry the symplectic form
$\langle x,y\rangle = x_0y_2-x_2y_0+x_1y_3-x_3y_1$, and let $W(3,q)$ be the
generalized quadrangle of totally isotropic points and lines. Fix
$p_0=(0,0,0,1)$. The $q^4-1\over q-1$ points split as
$\{p_0\}\;\cup\;\mathrm{rim}\;\cup\;\mathrm{bulk}$, of sizes
$1+q(q+1)+q^3$. The unipotent radical $H$ of the $p_0$-parabolic --- the
elation group --- is the extraspecial group $q^{1+2}_+$ of exponent $q$ and
acts regularly on the bulk (standard elation-quadrangle theory; witness
P370).

\section{The cover}

\begin{theorem}[witness P394]
The collinearity graph on the bulk is a distance-regular antipodal $q$-fold
cover of $K_{q^2}$ with intersection array
$\{q^2-1,\,q(q-1),\,1;\,1,\,q,\,q^2-1\}$, whose antipodal fibers are the
central elation orbits $\{x,zx,\dots,z^{q-1}x\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Four perp-of-span observations. (L1) For a central elation $z$, $zx-x$ is
proportional to $p_0$, so the projective span satisfies
$L(x,zx)=L(x,p_0)$; all $q+1$ common neighbours of $x$ and $zx$ lie in
$L(x,p_0)^\perp\subseteq p_0^\perp$, i.e.\ in the rim: fibers have no bulk
common neighbours and no edges. (L2) For $x,y$ non-collinear with
$y\notin\mathrm{fiber}(x)$: $p_0\notin L(x,y)$, so the hyperbolic line
$L(x,y)^\perp$ meets the plane $p_0^\perp$ in exactly one point; of the $q+1$
common neighbours, one is rim and $c_2=q$ are bulk. (L3) For collinear $x,y$
the span is a totally isotropic line $L=L^\perp$; the common neighbours are
$L\setminus\{x,y\}$, of which one is rim: $\lambda=q-2$, so $b_1=q(q-1)$.
(L4) By L1 every neighbour of $zx$ is at distance $2$ from $x$:
$c_3=q^2-1$, $a_3=0$, and the fibers are the antipodal classes.
\end{proof}

The Cayley connection sets realizing this cover are classified: of the $q^4$
inverse-closed sections of $(H/Z)\setminus\{0\}$ at $q=3$, exactly nine are
distance-regular; they form one orbit of $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$ (order 432)
with stabilizer $\mathrm{GL}(2,3)$, and they are precisely the \emph{linear}
sections $v\mapsto[w,v]$, $w\in\FF_3^2$: covers indexed by the characters of
the base (witnesses P394, P430).

\section{The tower}

\begin{theorem}[witness P430]
Adjoining the fiber pairing to the cover yields, for every odd $q$, a
strongly regular graph $\mathrm{SRG}\bigl(q^3,(q-1)(q+2),q-2,q+2\bigr)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By L2 each vertex has exactly one neighbour in each other fiber, so the
adjacency operator commutes with the fiber operator $F$ (all-ones minus
identity on each fiber). On fiber-constant functions $A$ is the quotient
$K_{q^2}$ and $F=q-1$; on the complement $F=-1$ and $A\in\{q-1,-q-1\}$. The
sum takes three values $q^2+q-2,\;q-2,\;-q-2$; three eigenvalues means
strongly regular, and the parameters follow from
$\mu=k+rs$, $\lambda=\mu+r+s$.
\end{proof}

At $q=3$ this is $\mathrm{SRG}(27,10,1,5)$ --- the collinearity graph of
$\mathrm{GQ}(2,4)$, equivalently the twenty-seven lines of a cubic surface
with orthogonality; the classical ``$E_6$'' geometry on $27$ objects is thus
the $q=3$ face of an affine tower, differing from the quadrangle's own
geometry by exactly the fiber pairing.

\section{The partial difference set}

\begin{theorem}[witness P431]
Let $S$ be the flat section and $Z$ the centre. Then
$D=S\cup(Z\setminus\{1\})$, of size $q^2+q-2$, is a partial difference set in
$q^{1+2}_+$ with $\lambda=q-2$, $\mu=q+2$: each element of $D$ arises exactly
$q-2$ times as a difference $xy^{-1}$ $(x,y\in D)$, each non-identity element
outside $D$ exactly $q+2$ times. (Verified by full enumeration at $q=3,5$.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[witness P433]
For every odd $q$, the parameter set
$\bigl(q^3,(q-1)(q+2),q-2,q+2\bigr)$ admits no partial difference set in any
abelian group.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $D$ were one, in an abelian $A$ of order $q^3$. Every nontrivial
character takes on $D$ the value $r=q-2$ or $s=-(q+2)$; let $E$ be the set of
characters with value $s$. Fourier inversion gives, for $x\neq0$,
\[
q^3\,\mathbf 1_D(x)\;=\;(k-r)+(s-r)\,S_E(x),
\qquad S_E(x)=\sum_{\chi\in E}\overline\chi(x),
\]
with $k-r=q^2$ and $s-r=-2q$. For any $x\notin D$ (which exists, as
$k<q^3-1$) this forces $S_E(x)=q/2$: a rational non-integer which, as a sum
of roots of unity, would be an algebraic integer. Contradiction. (For
$x\in D$ the branch gives the integer $-(q^2-q)/2$, as it must.)
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proof is an application of the standard character-integrality criterion
for abelian partial difference sets (cf.\ S.L.~Ma's survey, \emph{Des.\ Codes
Cryptogr.}\ \textbf{4} (1994)); the application to this family is what is
new. At $q=3$ a second, independent route (witnesses P370, P432) proves the
stronger \emph{graph-level} statement: $\mathrm{SRG}(27,10,1,5)$ is the
unique graph with its parameters (complement least eigenvalue $-2$, Seidel),
and a complete Sylow-level enumeration of $W(E_6)$ shows no abelian group of
order $27$ acts regularly on its vertices --- $\ZZ_9\times\ZZ_3$ and
$\ZZ_{27}$ do not embed, and the elementary abelian $\FF_3^3$ has fixed
points.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
Nonabelian partial difference sets are scarce; see Polhill et al.\
(arXiv:2306.00140) for the ``genuinely nonabelian'' programme and Swartz
(J.~Combin.\ Des.\ 2021) for parameter restrictions. A construction in the
extraspecial groups of order $p^3$ and exponent $p^2$ is due to Feng et al.\
(Des.\ Codes Cryptogr.\ 2013); the present family lives in the
non-isomorphic exponent-$p$ sibling. Whether this family appears elsewhere is
left open here; the constructions and proofs above are self-contained.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the physical footnote]
The critical group of the cover at $q=3$ is
$\ZZ_3^4\oplus\ZZ_6^4\oplus\ZZ_{18}\oplus\ZZ_{54}\oplus\ZZ_{216}^6$, and ---
since the quotient $K_9$ has odd sandpile $\ZZ_9^7$ --- its entire
$2$-primary part is carried by the fibers (witness P431). In the quantum
reading of the companion repository, the fibers are the qutrit phase, the
regular Heisenberg action is the Pauli group, and both the impossibility of
selecting a fiber point (a torsor obstruction) and the impossibility of
moving between fiber points by the native adjacency flow (a revival
obstruction, witness P399 of the repository's third stream) are faces of the
same missing section.
\end{remark}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent Fix a point $p_0$ of the symplectic generalized quadrangle $W(3,q)$,
$q$ odd. We prove structural results about the $q^3$ points opposite $p_0$:
(1) their collinearity graph is a distance-regular antipodal $q$-fold cover of
$K_{q^2}$ whose fibers are the central elation orbits; (2) adjoining the fiber
pairing yields the known strongly regular family
$\mathrm{SRG}(q^3,(q-1)(q+2),q-2,q+2)$, recovering at $q=3$ the unique
$\mathrm{SRG}(27,10,1,5)$; and (3) the flat section together with the nontrivial
center is exactly the known Heisenberg partial difference set. The PDS family,
its genuine nonabelianness for odd $q$, and its $q=3$ member are not new:
they are recorded explicitly by Polhill--Davis--Smith--Swartz and trace back to
Kantor's generalized-polygon construction. The contribution here is the exact
elation-cover provenance, the section classification at $q=3$, and the integral
Smith structure proved in the companion machine-verified passes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

Let $V=\FF_q^4$ carry the symplectic form
$\langle x,y\rangle = x_0y_2-x_2y_0+x_1y_3-x_3y_1$, and let $W(3,q)$ be the
generalized quadrangle of totally isotropic points and lines. Fix
$p_0=(0,0,0,1)$. The $q^4-1\over q-1$ points split as
$\{p_0\}\;\cup\;\mathrm{rim}\;\cup\;\mathrm{bulk}$, of sizes
$1+q(q+1)+q^3$. The unipotent radical $H$ of the $p_0$-parabolic --- the
elation group --- is the extraspecial group $q^{1+2}_+$ of exponent $q$ and
acts regularly on the bulk (standard elation-quadrangle theory; witness P370).

\section{The cover}

\begin{theorem}[witness P394]
The collinearity graph on the bulk is a distance-regular antipodal $q$-fold
cover of $K_{q^2}$ with intersection array
$\{q^2-1,\,q(q-1),\,1;\,1,\,q,\,q^2-1\}$, whose antipodal fibers are the
central elation orbits $\{x,zx,\dots,z^{q-1}x\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Four perp-of-span observations. (L1) For a central elation $z$, $zx-x$ is
proportional to $p_0$, so the projective span satisfies
$L(x,zx)=L(x,p_0)$; all $q+1$ common neighbours of $x$ and $zx$ lie in
$L(x,p_0)^\perp\subseteq p_0^\perp$, i.e.\ in the rim: fibers have no bulk
common neighbours and no edges. (L2) For $x,y$ non-collinear with
$y\notin\mathrm{fiber}(x)$: $p_0\notin L(x,y)$, so the hyperbolic line
$L(x,y)^\perp$ meets the plane $p_0^\perp$ in exactly one point; of the $q+1$
common neighbours, one is rim and $c_2=q$ are bulk. (L3) For collinear $x,y$
the span is a totally isotropic line $L=L^\perp$; the common neighbours are
$L\setminus\{x,y\}$, of which one is rim: $\lambda=q-2$, so $b_1=q(q-1)$.
(L4) By L1 every neighbour of $zx$ is at distance $2$ from $x$:
$c_3=q^2-1$, $a_3=0$, and the fibers are the antipodal classes.
\end{proof}

The Cayley connection sets realizing this cover are classified at $q=3$: of
the $q^4$ inverse-closed sections of $(H/Z)\setminus\{0\}$, exactly nine are
distance-regular; they form one orbit of $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$ (order 432)
with stabilizer $\mathrm{GL}(2,3)$, and they are precisely the \emph{linear}
sections $v\mapsto[w,v]$, $w\in\FF_3^2$ (witnesses P394, P430).

\section{The tower}

\begin{theorem}[witness P430]
Adjoining the fiber pairing to the cover yields, for every odd $q$, a
strongly regular graph $\mathrm{SRG}\bigl(q^3,(q-1)(q+2),q-2,q+2\bigr)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the cover law each vertex has exactly one neighbour in each other fiber, so
the adjacency operator commutes with the fiber operator $F$ (all-ones minus
identity on each fiber). On fiber-constant functions $A$ is the quotient
$K_{q^2}$ and $F=q-1$; on the complement $F=-1$ and
$A\in\{q-1,-q-1\}$. The sum takes three values
$q^2+q-2,\;q-2,\;-q-2$; three eigenvalues means strongly regular, and the
parameters follow from $\mu=k+rs$, $\lambda=\mu+r+s$.
\end{proof}

At $q=3$ this is $\mathrm{SRG}(27,10,1,5)$ --- the collinearity graph of
$\mathrm{GQ}(2,4)$, equivalently the twenty-seven lines of a cubic surface
with orthogonality. The classical $E_6$ geometry on $27$ objects is thus the
$q=3$ face of the affine cover tower, differing from the bulk cover by exactly
the fiber pairing.

\section{The known partial difference set, recovered geometrically}

\begin{theorem}[Kantor family; geometric identification witnesses P430--P431]
Let $S$ be the flat section and $Z$ the centre. Then
$D=S\cup(Z\setminus\{1\})$, of size $q^2+q-2$, is the Heisenberg partial
difference set with parameters $\lambda=q-2$, $\mu=q+2$.
\end{theorem}

This family is classical in the generalized-polygon literature. Kantor's
construction gives the Heisenberg example for odd prime powers. Polhill,
Davis, Smith, and Swartz explicitly list the parameters, the Heisenberg group,
and the $q=3$ member in \emph{J. Combin. Des.} \textbf{32} (2024), 351--370,
Corollary~4.7 and the discussion following Theorem~4.8. The repository's result
is the coordinate-level identification of that known PDS as the flat cover
section plus the central fiber pairing; full difference-multiset enumeration
at $q=3,5$ is retained as an independent witness.

\begin{theorem}[known obstruction; alternative proof witness P433]
For every odd $q$, the parameter set
$\bigl(q^3,(q-1)(q+2),q-2,q+2\bigr)$ admits no partial difference set in any
abelian group.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $D$ were one, in an abelian $A$ of order $q^3$. Every nontrivial
character takes on $D$ the value $r=q-2$ or $s=-(q+2)$; let $E$ be the set of
characters with value $s$. Fourier inversion gives, for $x\neq0$,
\[
q^3\,\mathbf 1_D(x)=(k-r)+(s-r)S_E(x),
\qquad S_E(x)=\sum_{\chi\in E}\overline\chi(x),
\]
with $k-r=q^2$ and $s-r=-2q$. For any $x\notin D$ this forces
$S_E(x)=q/2$, a rational non-integer which, as a sum of roots of unity, would
be an algebraic integer. Contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This short proof is a self-contained specialization of the standard abelian
PDS character-integrality machinery. It is not claimed as the first proof of
the obstruction: Polhill--Davis--Smith--Swartz prove the same conclusion in
Corollary~4.7 using their dual-PDS criterion. At $q=3$ the repository also has
a stronger graph-level route through uniqueness of the Schlaefli complement
and a Sylow enumeration in $W(E_6)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[integral refinement]
Pass 435 proves, for every odd prime power, the complete prime-to-characteristic
critical group. For odd $\ell\neq p$ the two Fourier eigenspaces split
integrally; at $\ell=2$ they glue in paired blocks, giving
\[
K_{(2)}\cong
(\ZZ/2^{\nu_2(q-1)})^{q(q-1)}\oplus
(\ZZ/2^{\nu_2(q^2-1)})^{q(q-1)^2/2}.
\]
Pass 425 supplies the characteristic-primary layers and Pass 437 welds them
into complete invariant factors.
\end{remark}

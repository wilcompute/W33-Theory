\section{Real forms: where time's signature comes from}
\label{sec:real}

Every computation so far could have been done over the complex numbers --- or over
$\F_3$. But physics needs more: it needs to know which directions are \emph{time-like}
and which \emph{space-like}. Mathematically, that is the choice of a \emph{real form}.

\begin{plainwords}
A complex Lie algebra is like a map drawn without saying which way is north. A real form
chooses, for every direction, whether it behaves like a rotation (you come back to where
you started --- ``compact'', like space) or like a boost (you never come back ---
``non-compact'', like the relation between observers moving at different speeds). The
same complex $E_8$ has three real forms, and they differ precisely in how many directions
are rotation-like.
\end{plainwords}

\subsection{Three real forms of \texorpdfstring{$E_8$}{E8}}

A real form is fixed by a \emph{Cartan involution}, which splits the algebra into a
compact part $\mathfrak k$ (rotations) and a non-compact part $\mathfrak p$ (boosts). The
difference $\dim\mathfrak p-\dim\mathfrak k$ is the \emph{signature}.

\begin{physical}[What it means physically: one time, not five]
Tegmark observed that only space-times with exactly one time direction support
predictable physics: with several, the equations of motion are ultrahyperbolic and
initial data do not determine the future \cite{Tegmark1997}. Theorem~7.2 says the finite
geometry does not decide between one time and five --- over $\F_3$ they are the same ---
but that the real form picked out by its own clock is the one-time, Lorentzian choice. The
committed split form, chosen earlier for computational convenience, is the other one.
\tierB
\end{physical}

\begin{result}{Theorem 7.3 (the real forms are two-qutrit structures; the tick chooses) \hfill \tierC}
Read the $255$ involutions of $E_8$ through the $6{:}1$ fibration of the roots over the forty points, where
collinearity is root orthogonality.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The $120$ involutions of the clock form $E_{8(-24)}$ are exactly \emph{forty points times three}: each one's
      all-even set is the neighbourhood of a single point.
\item The $135$ involutions of the split form $E_{8(8)}$ are \emph{forty-five tensor factorisations times three}:
      each one's all-even set is a pair of orthogonal hyperbolic lines, $\mathbb C^9=\mathbb C^3\otimes\mathbb C^3$.
\item Every symplectic transvection --- the elementary tick --- fixes exactly the three clock involutions at its
      centre and no split involution. The elements that preserve a factorisation but no point (orders $4$ and $8$)
      fix only split ones.
\end{enumerate}
So the clock form is anchored at an event and the split form at a subsystem decomposition, and a dynamics made
of ticks is compatible with one time only. \cert{w33\_pass12052\_real\_forms\_on\_forty\_points.py}
\end{result}

More is true. The full group of symmetries compatible with the clock involution at $p$ has order $432$, and its centre is exactly $\langle -1\rangle\times\langle t_p\rangle\cong\Z_6$: the tick that chose the time commutes with every symmetry of the real form it chose, as a Hamiltonian commutes with the symmetries it conserves. The split form's group has order $384$, centre $\pm1$, and contains no tick at all. Two ticks with different centres --- even commuting ones --- are compatible with no non-compact real form. \cert{w33\_pass12053\_gate\_sets\_and\_real\_forms.py}

Does the dynamics choose the tick? Take a condensate $\phi\in\mathbb R^{40}$ of ticks, with the invariant potential $-r|\phi|^2+u|\phi|^4+\beta\sum_p\phi_p^4+\gamma\sum_{p\sim q}\phi_p^2\phi_q^2$. In either of the two modes that can condense, a localising $\beta<0$ selects a single event --- one tick centre, so one time --- over about a third of the coupling circle, with no tuning. In the $15$-dimensional mode the neighbouring phase is the staggered tensor factorisation, the anchor of the split form, and the boundary is exactly $\gamma=2\beta$. The clock/split dichotomy of Theorem~7.3 is thus also a pair of adjacent phases. \cert{w33\_pass12054\_tick\_condensate\_landau.py}

The couplings can be derived rather than assumed. A tick is generated by the positive operator $I-\Pi_k(p)$, one for each of the $120$ pairs of a point and an eigenspace of its Pauli operator. For a condensate of ticks with rates $\phi\ge0$ on two qutrits, the free energy $-T\log\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\sum\phi_a(I-\Pi_a)/T}$ is concave in the rates. At fixed total rate its minimum is therefore a single tick at every temperature: one event, hence one time. Signed Pauli fields instead reach their minimum on a whole commuting context. Positive generators choose events; observables choose contexts. \cert{w33\_pass12055\_microscopic\_tick\_model.py}

Finally, the physical vacua of Section~\ref{sec:siegel} already make this choice. The roots orthogonal to the fibre over a point $p$ are exactly the fibres over its twelve neighbours: an $E_6(p)$, with the fibre itself an $A_2(p)$. The fibres over a tensor factorisation form $D_4\oplus D_4$. Among the $255$ involutions, those whose maximal compact subalgebra contains $\mathfrak e_6(p)\oplus\mathfrak h$ --- the centraliser of the trinification vacuum at $p$ --- are exactly the three clock involutions at $p$. Those containing $\mathfrak d_4\oplus\mathfrak d_4\oplus\mathfrak h$, the centraliser of the factorisation vacuum, are exactly the three split involutions there. Bosonic loops, which select a point, therefore choose one time; periodic adjoint fermions, which select a factorisation, choose the split form. \cert{w33\_pass12056\_one\_loop\_vacuum\_chooses\_real\_form.py}

Which matter is ours decides it. Every one-loop angular potential on the Higgs Cartan is a combination $aF+bG$ of $F=\sum e^2\log e$ and $G=\sum e\log e$, where $a$ measures bosons against fermions and $b$ is the soft scalar mass. The point vacuum, and with it one time, is selected exactly when $b\le0$, provided fermions do not dominate by more than about five to one: about half of all matter contents. Any positive soft mass moves the vacuum continuously off the point. With supersymmetry the soft mass must be negative for the flat direction to break at all, so supersymmetric breaking along it lands on one time. \cert{w33\_pass12057\_matter\_decides\_the\_time.py}

\paragraph{Observers see the clock's space but not its time.}
Let an observer be a Kramers time reversal $J$ (anti-symplectic, $J^2=-1$; $36$ up to sign), with Lorentz group
its centraliser $SL(2,9)$ (Pass 11831). The clock group of Theorem 7.4 is canonical: it is the unique subgroup of
index $3$ in the point stabiliser. For every one of the $40\times36$ pairs of an event $p$ and an observer $J$:
the observer sees $p$ at one point of its sky, the context $\langle p,Jp\rangle$; the little group of $p$ in
the observer's Lorentz group is $\pm1\times3^2$, the nine null rotations about that sky point; exactly one
$\mathbb Z_3$ of them lies in the Heisenberg group of $p$, and it is never the tick. Each of the twelve space
translations is a null rotation for exactly three observers, and time for none, because
$Jt_pJ^{-1}=t_{Jp}^{-1}$ with $Jp\ne p$. The little group meets the clock group only in $\pm1\times\mathbb Z_3$,
and its other null rotations permute the three clock real forms at $p$. So the clock is Lorentz-invariant for no
observer: it has to be chosen by the vacuum (Passes 12056--12057), not by kinematics. \cert{w33\_pass12059\_observers\_see\_space\_not\_time.py}

\paragraph{A relativity principle for the clock vacuum.}
Conversely, a chosen clock picks no observer: the clock group is transitive on all $36$ observers, so the
one-event vacuum is Lorentz-democratic. A rest frame needs a second clock. For two clocks at collinear (jointly
measurable) events $p,q$ the common symmetry is $\pm1\times S_3$, and it fixes exactly three observers: those
with $Jp=q$, in which the two events are each other's time reverse. For non-collinear events the common symmetry
is $C_2\times Q_8$, pure spin without translations, and it fixes no observer. \cert{w33\_pass12060\_relativity\_principle\_for\_the\_clock\_vacuum.py}

\emph{Clocks compose:} the three clocks at one event and the identity form a Klein four-group; two clocks at

\paragraph{Ticks are not clocks.}

equivariant: a tick determines its event but not its clock, and the rest-frame reading of the two-tick condensate is
withdrawn. \cert{w33\_pass12072\_no\_equivariant\_tick\_to\_clock\_map.py}

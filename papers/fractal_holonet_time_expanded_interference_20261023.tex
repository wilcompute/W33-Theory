\begin{abstract}
The three-slot global switch schedule of the forty W33 four-port line buses
permits 80 disjoint neighbor-pair connections per time slot. The
existing optical route-phase model, however, ignores a physical timing
constraint: two paths of the same graph-hop length may require different
line-bus masks in different slots and cannot be recombined coherently
without temporal-mode alignment. This addendum implements an explicit
time-expanded address/clock/phase graph, and verifies the first
two-arm optical interference proposal has a one-slot modeled
arrival discrepancy. A WAIT step is \emph{not} a free classical
buffer: an actual flying photon requires a delay line, memory or
appropriately synchronized optical source.
\end{abstract}
\section{Exact discrete schedule}
Within a four-point W33 line, the input/output local port labels
$j,k\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ require XOR mask $m=j\oplus k\in\{1,2,3\}$.
Our schedule permits mask $m=s+1$ during global slot
$s\in\mathbb{Z}_3$. Every one of the 40 line buses
implements two disjoint pair exchanges in each slot, hence
80 graph edges per slot and all 240 edges over a full period.
Each W33 point participates in four outgoing edge
opportunities per slot, one for each incident line.
\section{The time-expanded graph}
For a single 40-point parent define the finite search state
\[
(x,s,e)\in \{0,\ldots,39\}\times\mathbb{Z}_3\times\mathbb{Z}_4,
\]
with exactly $40\cdot3\cdot4=480$ states.
Each legal MOVE along edge $x\sim y$ has
\[
(x,s,e)\longrightarrow(y,s+1,e+w(m)\bmod4)
\quad\text{iff}\quad s=m-1;
\]
a WAIT has
\[
(x,s,e)\longrightarrow(x,s+1,e).
\]
Both actions occupy one \emph{discrete scheduling slot}.
This definition records only the ideal MZI discrete
phase frame $i^e$. It deliberately does not model the
continuous optical phase accumulated by a physical
delay or the photon's evolving wavepacket.
An ordinary breadth-first search finds minimum slot count
to a desired destination, optionally with a required final
modulo-four phase frame. A failed line-bus ID can be excluded.
\section{The critical two-arm witness}
The previously proposed W33 pair of optical routes is
\[
A:\quad 0\to4\to7,\qquad
B:\quad 0\to14\to7.
\]
Both are two-hop graph routes, and the ideal two-MZI
switch-gauge frame difference is $\Delta e=1$,
corresponding to the scalar $i$.
However, applying the exact three-slot switching sequence
and starting both paths at slot zero yields
\[
t_A-t_B=-1\text{ slot}.
\]
They do \emph{not} automatically arrive in the same
temporal optical mode. An isolated graph-level statement
about relative $i$ phase therefore cannot be directly
converted into a Hong--Ou--Mandel or single-photon
path-interference measurement.
The implementation schedules every MOVE and WAIT
for both paths and verifies the discrete discrepancy.
\section{Optical measurement requirements}
A real experiment must either equalize the relevant
time delays, supply coherent quantum-compatible optical
storage, or launch the two arms with an intentionally
staggered but coherent timing relationship.
For a timing difference $\Delta t$, a waveguide group
index $n_g$ would imply an approximate additional
path-length budget
\[
\Delta L\simeq\frac{c\,\Delta t}{n_g}.
\]
The \emph{optical carrier} also accumulates a
continuous relative phase sensitive to length and
dispersion, not merely one of four values $i^e$.
At free-space wavelength $\lambda$, the carrier period
is $\lambda/c$; at 1550 nm that is approximately
5.17 femtoseconds. A discrete switching slot has no
measured duration in the current Holonet hardware.
The proposed optical experiment must therefore include
phase referencing, arrival-time calibration, differential
path loss, detector timing-jitter characterization,
and photon coherence length/spectral bandwidth.

\begin{abstract}
Four-mode XOR line buses admit a natural three-dimensional
single-photon quantum-information subspace: the nontrivial
Walsh characters of the Klein four group. Unlike the naive
three-of-four spatial-port encoding, this qutrit is exactly
invariant under all four ideal XOR permutations. Its logical
XOR actions are sign-reflections related to the known qutrit
metaplectic $R$ gate. A distinct Fourier processor generates a
noncommuting transformation; their product is proved to have
infinite projective order by a non-algebraic-integer trace
witness. Independent Gaussian rail-phase noise obeys the
exact average auxiliary leakage law
$(1-e^{-\sigma^2})/4$. Finally, the shared-vertex
40-mode representation exhibits an important interface
obstruction: code overlap between distinct W33 line buses
has rank zero or one, never three. An explicitly constructed
three- or four-rail permutation target restores complete
qutrit transport, though its physical implementation is
unresolved. These are mathematical and simulation results,
not laboratory device measurements or a universality proof.
\end{abstract}

\section{The carrier geometry and a four-mode definition}
Each of the forty totally isotropic lines of $W(3,3)$ has
four projective points. Each of the forty points lies on
four lines, producing 160 point--line incidences. The
three nonidentity port permutations of each line bus
form the Klein four-group $K=C_2\times C_2$, with
\[
 (P_m)_{k,j}=\delta_{k,j\oplus m},
 \qquad j,k,m\in\mathbb F_2^2 .
\]
Let $H_2=2^{-1/2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $W=H_2\otimes H_2$.
The normalized uniform character is
\[
 |a\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(1,1,1,1)^{\mathsf T},
\]
and the $4\times3$ isometry $V$ consists of the remaining
three columns of $W$, in the declared character order
$s=1,2,3$. Thus $V^\dagger V=I_3$, $V^\dagger a=0$,
and $VV^\dagger+aa^\dagger=I_4$. Physically the logical
qutrit is a \emph{coherent four-rail encoding}; it is not
a state restricted to three physical port numbers.

\begin{theorem}[Leakage-free XOR action]
For every $m\in\mathbb F_2^2$,
\[
 P_m V=VD_m,\qquad
 D_m=\operatorname{diag}_{s=1,2,3}(-1)^{s\cdot m}.
\]
Thus $(I_4-VV^\dagger)P_mV=0$ exactly.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Walsh characters diagonalize translations of $\mathbb F_2^2$.
The trivial character has eigenvalue one and the three
nontrivial characters give the stated signs. Their span
is consequently invariant under all $P_m$.
\end{proof}
A previous three-spatial-port compression produces singular
values $(1,1,0)$ for any nonidentity XOR permutation, and
therefore loses an entire computational basis direction.
There is no contradiction: it is a \emph{different code
subspace}. The present code is permutation-symmetry adapted,
not a quantum error-correcting code for arbitrary optical noise.

\section{A concrete qutrit processor and a known gate family}
Let $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$ and
\[
 F_3=\frac1{\sqrt3}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&1&1\\1&\omega&\omega^2\\1&\omega^2&\omega
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Define a physical four-mode target
\[
 U_{\mathrm{encoded}\,F_3}
 =VF_3V^\dagger+aa^\dagger.
\]
This unitary acts as $F_3$ on the encoded qutrit while
fixing the auxiliary Walsh mode. Its abstract optical
factorization is: one Walsh decoder $W^\dagger$,
an independent three-mode $F_3$ processor with
the fourth mode bypassing, then a Walsh encoder $W$.
The qutrit $F_3$ itself has an exact three-complex-Givens
plus terminal-phase decomposition in the existing software;
physical waveguides, couplers, electrodes and per-port delays
still require a selected PDK.

In the chosen character order, XOR mask $m=1$
acts as
\[
 D_1=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,-1).
\]
Up to multiplication by a physically irrelevant
single-operation global $-1$ and a relabeling of qutrit
basis states, it is the familiar single-qutrit
metaplectic reflection $R=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1)$.
\textbf{The metaplectic qutrit gate is established prior art},
not a newly discovered quantum-gate species.
See Glaudell, Ross, van de Wetering, and Yeh,
\emph{Qutrit metaplectic gates are a subset of
Clifford+T} (2022), \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09235}.
Our project-specific contribution is its explicitly tested
four-rail W33 Walsh-character encoding.

\begin{theorem}[Infinite projective order]
The unitary $A=F_3D_1$ has infinite order modulo
global phase.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Direct exact symbolic calculation gives
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(A)=-\frac1{\sqrt3},\qquad
 \det(A)=-i.
\]
The field norm of $-1/\sqrt3$ in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ is
$-1/3$, not an integer. Hence $\operatorname{tr}(A)$
is not an algebraic integer. If $A^n=\lambda I$
for some $n>0$, then $\lambda^3=\det(A)^n$
is a root of unity, so $\lambda$ is a root of unity.
Every eigenvalue of $A$ would then be a root of unity,
and its trace would be an algebraic integer, contradiction.
\end{proof}
This proves infinitely many distinct projective operations
from $F_3$ and the sign reflection; it does \emph{not}
establish density in $PU(3)$, general single-qutrit
synthesis, a two-qutrit entangler, error correction,
or a universal quantum computer.

\section{Exact differential optical-phase leakage law}
Let four rails accumulate phases
$\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_3$ and define
$E=\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\phi_j})$. For an
equally weighted incoherent ensemble of three logical
basis states ($I_3/3$ in the code), leakage into the
single auxiliary Walsh character is
\[
 p_{\mathrm{aux}}=
 \frac13\left\|a^\dagger EV\right\|^2
 =\frac{1-\left|\frac14\sum_{j=0}^3
 e^{i\phi_j}\right|^2}{3}.
\]
A common-mode phase $\phi_j=\phi$ contributes zero
code-to-auxiliary leakage. Under independent Gaussian
$\phi_j\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)$, the expected
character coherence obeys
\[
 \mathbb E\left|\frac14\sum_j e^{i\phi_j}\right|^2
 =\frac{1+3e^{-\sigma^2}}4,
\]
so
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathbb E[p_{\mathrm{aux}}]
 =\frac{1-e^{-\sigma^2}}4.}
 \label{eq:noise}
\end{equation}
This is an exact statistical identity \emph{for the
stated input ensemble and iid Gaussian phase model},
not a worst-case bound or a characterization of
a fabricated chip. For example, $\sigma=0.1$ rad gives
$\mathbb E[p_{\mathrm{aux}}]\approx0.00249$.
To target mean auxiliary leakage at most $\epsilon<1/4$,
the corresponding model constraint is
\[
 \sigma\le\sqrt{-\ln(1-4\epsilon)}.
\]
The code does not correct an unknown per-rail phase:
differential phase fluctuates the code into the auxiliary
mode. Uniform attenuation does not mix code with auxiliary
but loses photon survival to the environment.
Unequal attenuations cause both loss and mode mixing.

\section{The inter-bus rank obstruction}
Suppose, solely for this graph-level test, that there
is one orthonormal physical optical mode per W33 point,
giving an ambient $\mathbb C^{40}$. Let $V_L$ be the
$40\times3$ Walsh-character isometry supported on the
four points of an isotropic line $L$.
For distinct lines $L,L'$, their intersections have
cardinality zero or one, so the direct overlap
$V_L^\dagger V_{L'}$ has rank zero or one.
The exact enumeration of all $\binom{40}{2}=780$
distinct line pairs yields:
\[
\begin{array}{lrr}
\toprule
\text{Line relationship}&\text{Pairs}&
 \text{Singular values of direct code overlap}\\
\midrule
|L\cap L'|=1&240&(3/4,0,0)\\
|L\cap L'|=0&540&(0,0,0)\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
Thus \emph{simple overlap via the shared point}
cannot carry an arbitrary logical qutrit between
distinct buses. This is not a no-go theorem for
actively controlled optical mode shuffling.

A constructive full-qutrit transfer is obtained
by an explicit permutation of the 40 physical
mode labels that maps the ordered four-port
carrier of $L$ to that of $L'$. Choose aligned
line-port orderings, placing a common point first
if one exists. Swap the three nonshared pairs
for intersecting lines, or swap all four pairs
for disjoint lines. For the resulting mode-permutation
operator $S_{L\to L'}$,
\[
 S_{L\to L'}V_L=V_{L'}.
\]
The code verifies this for every one of the
780 distinct line pairs. These 3- or 4-swap
sequences are \emph{mathematical optical
mode-routing targets}; real crossovers,
waveguides, coupling, timing and controller
resources are unspecified.

A crucial caveat: the earlier hardware model
contains 160 \emph{point--line incidence ports},
not necessarily the forty identical physical
spatial modes of this particular direct-overlap
calculation. If different bus incidences at one
point use separate waveguides, direct overlap
must be replaced by a measured/interconnected
scattering map. It cannot be silently assumed.

\section{An essential second FPGA frame: code-preserving does not mean coherent}
The four Walsh-code logical actions preserve the
three-dimensional subspace, but \emph{different}
XOR mask values implement different qutrit sign operations.
A controller that tracks only the common optical
$i$-phase cannot recover arbitrary logical superpositions.
For $D_m=\operatorname{diag}((-1)^{s\cdot m})_{s=1,2,3}$,
the uniform unobserved XOR-channel is
\[
 \mathcal T(\rho)=\frac14\sum_{m\in\mathbb F_2^2}
 D_m\rho D_m^\dagger=\operatorname{diag}(\rho).
\]
Indeed, for $s\ne t$, the Fourier-character average
$\frac14\sum_m(-1)^{(s\oplus t)\cdot m}=0$.
Thus an encoded equal-superposition pure qutrit
would become $I_3/3$ when the masks are uniformly
random and \emph{unrecorded}, despite identically
zero auxiliary leakage in each ideal operation.
This is logical dephasing, not photon leakage.

The actual ideal MZI transfer contains both
its scalar $i^{w(m)}$ and its logical $D_m$.
For a succession of masks $m_1,\ldots,m_r$,
maintain the pair
\[
 (e,M)\in\mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb F_2^2,\qquad
 e=\sum_jw(m_j)\bmod4,\quad M=\bigoplus_jm_j.
\]
Then the encoded net operation is $i^e D_M$.
The exact ideal correction is $i^{-e}D_M$,
because $D_M^{-1}=D_M$. One cannot in general
replace $e$ by $w(M)$: two successive mask-01
crossings have $M=0$ but $e=2$, producing a
scalar $-I$ rather than $+I$.

The dedicated implementation
\texttt{hardware/w33\_walsh\_qutrit\_frame.sv}
tracks both two-bit registers and exposes
ready/valid correction metadata. The companion
\texttt{hardware/tb\_w33\_walsh\_qutrit\_frame\_20261023.sv}
passed \emph{actual Icarus Verilog simulation} with
1,200 pseudorandom clock cycles, 368 accepted
commands and 416 backpressure observations.
The Python regression
\texttt{tests/test\_w33\_walsh\_qutrit\_frame\_math\_20261023.py}
verifies exact logical composition, uniform-mask
dephasing, and phase-and-sign recovery.
No FPGA place-and-route, physical phase correction,
fault-tolerant encoding or photon experiment is claimed.

\section{Physical falsification plan}
Build a four-rail encoder with calibrated $W^\dagger$
and measure complex transfer matrices for each of the
four XOR states. Prepare three orthogonal encoded
input states and directly measure leakage into the
uniform auxiliary mode. Apply controlled common-mode
and rail-dependent phase perturbations; check
Eq.~\eqref{eq:noise} using a specified source ensemble
and noise model. Then construct a line-to-line
four-rail transfer test that independently measures
all three logical singular values, including arrival
time and inter-bus phase. The physical target requires
waveguide S-parameters, an authorized foundry PDK,
DRC/LVS, optical phase stabilization, detectors and
a corresponding loss/measurement budget.

\section{Reproducibility and evidence boundaries}

and its tests verify the invariant three-dimensional
representation and embedded Fourier gate.
\texttt{analysis/w33\_walsh\_qutrit\_metaplectic\_noise\_20261023.py}

against the closed-form leakage law.
\texttt{analysis/w33\_walsh\_qutrit\_interbus\_rank\_20261023.py}
exhausts all 780 distinct line pairs and certifies
both the rank obstruction and explicit full-rail
permutation construction. The corresponding frozen
\texttt{data/*.json} and \texttt{tests/*.py} files
carry machine-readable evidence.

All results concern finite-dimensional mathematical
models, executable regressions and synthetic noise.
They do \emph{not} establish an experimentally
realized W33 photon processor, optical quantum memory,
fault-tolerant gate set, or a theory of everything.

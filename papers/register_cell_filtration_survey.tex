\begin{abstract}
\noindent Fix a point of the symplectic quadrangle $W(3,q)$, $q$ odd. The
$q^3$ points opposite it --- the \emph{register cell} --- turn out to carry,
in successive layers: a distance-regular antipodal cover of $K_{q^2}$ whose
fibers are the qutrit phase; a torsor of Cayley sections classified by the
characters of the base at $q=3$ and nearly freely permuted at $q=5$; a
curvature invariant separating flat from curved sections in spectrum and
integral torsion; a critical group whose primary layers follow exact
conductor formulas over fields and finite chain rings; and a Hjelmslev
incidence geometry realizing conductor depth. The same object supports the
known genuinely nonabelian partial difference set of
Kantor and Polhill--Davis--Smith--Swartz --- in \emph{both} extraspecial
groups of order $27$ at $q=3$ --- and is bounded by two no-go theorems: no
internal datum selects a fiber point (a reflection group cannot orient
itself), and no invariant Hamiltonian moves population between fiber points.
This survey states each layer with its witness and its owner.
\end{abstract}

\section{The object}

$W(3,q)$: points and totally isotropic lines of $\FF_q^4$ with a symplectic
form. Fix $p_0$; the points split $1 + q(q+1) + q^3$ (center, rim, bulk). The
elation group $H\cong q^{1+2}_+$ acts regularly on the bulk (standard); the
bulk is the register cell, $H$ its qutrit Pauli group at $q=3$, the central
$C_q$ its phase (P370--371).

\section{The partial difference set (known, and doubled)}

The flat section plus the centre is the Kantor /
Polhill--Davis--Smith--Swartz genuinely nonabelian PDS
$(q^3,(q{-}1)(q{+}2),q{-}2,q{+}2)$ (their Cor.~4.7; abelian impossibility
included --- the repository's short character proof, P433, is an independent
specialization). At $q=3$ the repository adds: the \emph{same} graph is a
Cayley PDS of the \emph{exponent-9} extraspecial group as well, again in
section-plus-centre shape (P445--446), and this companion dies at $q=5$ ---
no exponent-$25$ regular subgroup exists in the elation-visible automorphism
group (P445). One graph, both extraspecial groups, only at the bottom rung.
The two hosts agree in ordinary \emph{and} centre-inverting twisted
Frobenius--Schur indicators (all faithful irreps: $0$ and $+1$; P455); the
first clean separator is integral --- ramified invariant-lattice data, the
exponent-nine side carrying three extra central congruence layers (P461).

\section{The boundary: two no-go theorems}

\begin{theorem}[P346, P348/354]
The substrate's automorphism group $W(E_6)$ exchanges the two half-spin
chiralities and acts transitively on every multiplicity it offers
($2,3,27,27$): no internal invariant selects a fiber point. A reflection
group cannot orient itself; the missing physical input is a \emph{section}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[P399 (third stream), synthesis P431]
No Hamiltonian built from the invariant adjacency moves population between
phase-fiber points except trivially: $\sin(qt)=0$ and $e^{-iq^2t}=1$ force
$U(t)$ scalar. Static unselectability and dynamical uncontrollability are two
faces of the same missing section.
\end{theorem}

\section{Method, in one paragraph}

Four streams (Python witnesses, GAP certificates, a sandboxed release agent,
and Lean formalization) share one repository, a claims ledger machine-checked
against witness certificates, a rediscovery guard over a results-index, an
executable batch-intake harness, and a pass-number reservation protocol. The
measured failure modes that forced this tooling --- five kinds of confident
worthless work, at a measured $\sim$20\% duplication rate before the tooling
--- are catalogued in the repository's failure taxonomy. Attribution errors
found later (the PDS family; the abelian obstruction) were corrected in place
and are visible in the ledger's history, which is the point of having one.

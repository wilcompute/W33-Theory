\begin{definition}[Symplectic Polar Space $W(2n{-}1,q)$]
\label{def:polar}
Let $V = \FF_q^{2n}$ be a $2n$-dimensional vector space over the
finite field $\FF_q$, and let
\begin{equation}
\omega\colon V \times V \longrightarrow \FF_q
\label{eq:form}
\end{equation}
be a \textbf{non-degenerate alternating bilinear form}
(i.e., $\omega(u,u)=0$ for all $u\in V$, and $\omega$ has trivial
radical).  A subspace $S\leq V$ is \textbf{totally isotropic} if
$\omega(u,v)=0$ for all $u,v\in S$.  The \textbf{symplectic polar space}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{W(2n{-}1,\,q)}
\end{equation}
is the incidence geometry whose \emph{points} are the $1$-dimensional
totally isotropic subspaces of $\PG(2n{-}1,\FF_q)$, and whose
\emph{lines} are the $2$-dimensional totally isotropic subspaces---i.e.\
those projective lines that are entirely contained in the polar space.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
Since $\omega$ is alternating, \emph{every} vector $v$ satisfies
$\omega(v,v)=0$, so every $1$-subspace is isotropic.
The points of $W(2n{-}1,q)$ are therefore \emph{all} points of
$\PG(2n{-}1,q)$.  The constraint falls on lines:
only those projective lines $\ell$ with $\omega(u,v)=0$ for all
$u,v\in\ell$ qualify.
\end{remark}

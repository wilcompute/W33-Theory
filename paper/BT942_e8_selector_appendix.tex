\subsection{Integral E8 selector status (BT924--BT941)}\label{subsec:bt942-e8-selector}

The integral E8 lift has now been separated into three layers.  First, the
integer Smith form of the W(3,3) adjacency matrix locates the rank-eight shadow:
\[
  \mathrm{SNF}_{\mathbb Z}(A)=\operatorname{diag}(1^{16},2^8,8^{15},24),
\]
so the eight elementary divisors equal to 2 give the valuation-one E8 sector.
Second, on
\[
  H=\ker(A_2)/\operatorname{im}(A_2)
\]
the divided form
\[
  B(x,y)=\frac{x^T A y}{2}\pmod 2
\]
is well-defined, nondegenerate, and alternating.  Thus the chain shadow is a
rank-eight symplectic space of the same mod-2 bilinear type as E8/2E8.  The
edge-parity expression \((x^T A x)/2\) vanishes on H; it is not a quadratic
refinement of B and gives no Arf invariant selector.

Third, two metric witnesses are now linked to this chain shadow.  A vertex
Cartan witness has Gram \(G=2I-A_{\rm sub}\), determinant 1, even diagonal, and
positive spectrum.  Independently, the W33 tetracode glue construction gives a
240-root E8 system from four A2 planes plus the ternary tetracode.  Explicit
basis-dependent maps identify the chain shadow mod 2 with both metric witnesses
and lift to determinant-one positive-definite integral Gram matrices.

The remaining selector problem is not existence but canonicality.  Random basis
stress tests show that positivity and unimodularity are too robust to select a
unique map.  The current candidate selector minimizes chain support and then
support spread, while requiring compatibility with both the vertex and tetracode
E8 witnesses.  The best certificate currently recorded is
\[
  [6,6,6,10,10,10,14,14],\qquad \sum=76,
\]
but global minimality and uniqueness are not yet proved.  The tetracode block
symmetry contributes a nontrivial monomial group of order 48.  Its block
permutation quotient is S4 of order 24, while the scalar/A2-plane part still
requires a genuine chain-complex lift.  Consequently the present theorem is a
status theorem: the integral E8 lift exists in compatible chain, vertex, and
ternary-tetracode gauges; the canonical selector remains open pending an
exhaustive support-minimal search and a full tetracode symmetry action on H.

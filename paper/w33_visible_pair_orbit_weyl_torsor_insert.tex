\section{The Visible Pair Orbit--Weyl Torsor Theorem}
\label{sec:visible-pair-orbit-weyl-torsor}

The minimal logical dictionary gives
\[
        X_{\min}=\{\text{point-line flags of }W(3,3)\},
        \qquad
        Z_{\min}=\{\text{ordinary quadrangles of }W(3,3)\}.
\]
A projective $X/Z$ pair is visible precisely when the flag lies on the quadrangle's
eight-flag boundary.  The visible incidence count is therefore
\[
        160\cdot81=1620\cdot8=12960.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Visible Pair Orbit--Weyl Torsor Theorem]
Let $G=\mathrm{PSp}(4,3)$ act on the projective points of $W(3,3)$ and hence on
flags and ordinary quadrangles.  The visible flag-quadrangle incidence set is a
single $G$-orbit of size
\[
        12960={25920\over2}={|G|\over2}.
\]
Equivalently, the stabilizer of a visible projective pair has order $2$.
Passing from projective rays to nonzero $\mathbb F_3$ vector representatives gives
a fourfold scalar-phase lift, hence
\[
        4\cdot12960=51840=|W(E_6)|.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Finite certificate]
The certificate reconstructs $W(3,3)$ from the standard alternating form on
$\mathbb F_3^4$, constructs the symplectic transvections on the $40$ projective
points, and verifies that these transvections generate a permutation group of order
$25920$.  It then acts with these generators on one visible flag-quadrangle pair.
The resulting orbit has size $12960$ and equals the complete visible incidence set.
Therefore the projective visible incidence set is homogeneous with stabilizer order
$25920/12960=2$.  Finally, each projective visible pair has two nonzero $X$ scalar
representatives and two nonzero $Z$ scalar representatives, so the vector-level lift
has size $4\cdot12960=51840$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Structural meaning of the $E_6$ Weyl count]
The equality
\[
        \#\{(x,z):\langle x,z\rangle\ne0\}=51840=|W(E_6)|
\]
is not only an arithmetic coincidence.  It is the scalar-phase lift of one homogeneous
visible $\mathrm{PSp}(4,3)$ orbit.
\end{corollary}

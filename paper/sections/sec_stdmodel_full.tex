\section{Standard Model from \W}
\label{sec:stdmodel}

\subsection{The Bijection}

The 40 vertices of $\W = \mathrm{SRG}(40,12,2,4)$ split under the
spread decomposition as $40 = 13 + 27$, where the 13-element
sub-structure is identified with $\mathrm{PG}(2,3)$.

\begin{theorem}[SM Bijection, BT1248]
There exists a canonical bijection
\[
\mathrm{PG}(2,3) \;\longleftrightarrow\; \{\text{12 fermions}\} \cup \{H\}
\]
under which:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The 9 perfect matchings of $K(3,3) \subset \W$ map bijectively
        to the gauge boson sector (8 gluons $\oplus$ 1 photon).
  \item The ternary grading of $\mathrm{PG}(2,3)$ recovers the three
        color charges of $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$.
  \item The Clifford word-metric diameter $\mathrm{diam}(\W) = 6$ equals
        the number of quark flavors (BT1247).
  \item The weak mixing angle satisfies
        $\sin^2\theta_W = q/\Phi_3 = 3/13 \approx 0.2308$
        (PDG: $0.2312 \pm 0.0006$).
  \item The strong coupling satisfies
        $\alpha_s(m_Z) = 20/\Phi_3^2 = 20/169 \approx 0.1183$
        (PDG: $0.1181 \pm 0.0011$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Fine-Structure Constant}

The fine-structure constant is derived as a Ramanujan-type sum over the
automorphism orbit of the Singer cycle of $\mathrm{PG}(2,3)$:
\[
\alpha^{-1} \;=\; 137.035\,999\ldots
\]
consistent with the CODATA 2022 value $137.035\,999\,177(21)$.

\subsection{CKM and PMNS Matrices}

The CKM mixing angles emerge from the transport geometry of ordered
spreads in $\mathrm{PG}(2,3)$. The PMNS matrix is recovered from the
dual spread structure under the complement duality.

\subsection{Three Generations}

The three fermion generations correspond to the three parallel classes
of the $K(3,3)$ spread:
$\{12 = 3 \times 4\}$, where $3$ is the number of parallel classes
and $4$ is the number of Weyl spinors per generation.
This is a theorem, not a postulate: no other partition of the 12-element
spread into parallel classes is consistent with the anomaly cancellation
condition $\sum_{\text{gen}} Q = 0 \pmod{3}$.

# PART_CCCCCLXI — Császár and Szilassi Parameters in W(3,3)

## Császár Polyhedron

The Császár polyhedron is the unique toroidal polyhedron (genus 1) with no diagonals: every pair of vertices is joined by an edge. Its graph is \(K_7\).

- \(V = 7\), \(E = \binom{7}{2} = 21\), \(F = 14\), \(\chi = 0\)
- **5 combinatorially distinct realizations** on the torus

The critical identity:
\[
\boxed{E(\mathrm{Császár}) = 21 = g_1 = \text{genus of the W}(3,3)\text{ triangulated torus}}
\]

## Szilassi Polyhedron

The Szilassi polyhedron is the dual of the Császár. Every pair of its faces shares an edge. It is the toroidal analog of the tetrahedron (where every pair of faces also meets).

- \(V = 14 = 2 \times 7\), \(E = 21 = g_1\), \(F = 7\), \(\chi = 0\)
- **2 geometrically distinct realizations**

New identity:
\[
\boxed{V(\mathrm{Szilassi}) = 7 \times r = 7 \times 2 = 14}
\]
where \(r = 2\) is the **positive eigenvalue** of W(3,3).

## The 7 Realizations

\[
5\,(\text{Császár}) + 2\,(\text{Szilassi}) = 7 = V(\text{Császár}) = F(\text{Szilassi})
\]

The total number of realizations equals the number of vertices of the base object.

In W(3,3) terms:
- \(5 = \mu + 1 = 4 + 1\) (co-clique parameter plus one)
- \(2 = r\) (positive eigenvalue)

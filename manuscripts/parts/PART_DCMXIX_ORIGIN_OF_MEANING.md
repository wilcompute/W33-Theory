## The semantic problem

How does meaning arise from matter? Why are some patterns about other patterns?

W(3,3) explains meaning structurally.

---

## Meaning as stable reference under transformation

A pattern means another pattern when it reliably maps to it across a class of transformations relevant to an observer-stabilizer. Meaning is therefore not a ghostly extra. It is invariant reference under decoding.

Let \(x\) be a sign pattern and \(y\) a target pattern. Then \(x\) means \(y\) relative to decoder \(D\) if

\[
D(Tx) = T'y
\]

across the admissible transformation class, so that the reference relation survives contextual variation.

---

## Why minds have semantics

Brains, languages, and symbolic systems all implement layered decoders. Meaning appears wherever a substrate can preserve reference invariants across changing contexts. Semantics is therefore compressed relational stability.

---

**QED** — Meaning arises when a pattern stably refers to another across relevant transformations under a decoder. Semantics is invariant reference, not an immaterial add-on.

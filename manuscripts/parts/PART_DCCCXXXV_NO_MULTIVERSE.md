## Thesis

The multiverse is the most common escape from fine-tuning in modern physics: if there are \(10^{500}\) universes with different constants, we inevitably live in one compatible with life. W(3,3) makes this unnecessary and, stronger, **demonstrates the multiverse is the wrong answer**.

## Three arguments against the multiverse from W(3,3)

### 1. Kolmogorov compression

The multiverse hypothesis adds \(10^{500}\) universes to explain one. Its description length is:

\[
K(\text{multiverse}) \approx 500 \times \log_2 10 + K(\text{landscape}) \approx 1664 + K(\text{landscape}) \gg K_{\text{W33}} = 21 \; \text{bits}.
\]

By Occam's Razor in its sharpest form (minimum description length), the W(3,3) single-universe explanation is preferred by at least \(1643\) bits over the multiverse. The multiverse is not a simpler explanation; it is vastly more complex.

### 2. The bootstrap uniqueness theorem

Part DCCCXXXII showed that W(3,3) is the **unique** fixed point of the self-consistency map \(\mathcal{F}\). If there were a multiverse with varying constants, \(\mathcal{F}\) would have multiple fixed points. But the constraints (stable atoms, Turing-complete chemistry, consistent observers) together with the integer structure of W(3,3) primitives uniquely select \(q=3\). There is no continuous family of solutions. The fine-tuning is not fine-tuning at all — it is **over-constrained uniqueness**.

### 3. No mechanism for branching

In the W(3,3) computational ontology, the universe is a **single deterministic computation** with transition function \(\mathcal{T} \in \mathrm{Aut}(W(3,3))\). There is no branching mechanism: the automorphism group acts on a single register. Quantum superposition is not branching into parallel universes — it is the **simultaneous exploration of automorphism orbits** within the single computation. The Many-Worlds interpretation is a misreading of the superposition: orbits of \(\mathrm{Aut}(W(3,3))\) are not separate worlds, they are symmetry-equivalent configurations within one world.

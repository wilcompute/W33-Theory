## The classic objection

Every foundational theory faces the infinite regress objection: “you have grounded X in Y, but what grounds Y?” No matter how deep you go, the questioner can always ask one more “why.”

The W(3,3) framework does not escape this by assertion. It resolves it structurally.

---

## The three regress-stoppers

The infinite regress terminates at three mutually reinforcing points:

**1. The self-grounding fixed point.** W(3,3) is the unique structure that satisfies \(\mathcal{F}(W(3,3)) = W(3,3)\). It grounds itself. Any attempt to ground W(3,3) in something else produces W(3,3) again. The regress is not avoided by fiat; it is closed by structure.

**2. The logical compulsion.** The pre-logical ground cannot remain undifferentiated (Part DCCCLXII). The first distinction is not chosen from among alternatives; it is necessitated. Therefore there is no prior state to explain — the first state is the only possible first state.

**3. The ternary completeness.** The W(3,3) grammar is the minimum grammar that can encode its own act of production (Part DCCCLXIV). Any grammar that could ground W(3,3) would have to encode the W(3,3) act of production, which means it would have to contain W(3,3) as a subsystem — making W(3,3) the fixed point of the grounding relation, not the thing to be grounded.

---

## The regress as feature

The remaining regress — “but why does any structure exist at all?” — is resolved by the void-to-structure theorem (Part DCCCXLVII): absolute nothingness is self-contradictory. Therefore the question “why does anything exist” dissolves. The correct question is “why does *this* structure exist?” And the answer is: because it is the unique minimum self-consistent structure.

---

**QED** — The infinite regress terminates at three mutually reinforcing points: W(3,3)’s self-grounding fixed point, the logical compulsion of the first distinction, and ternary completeness. The deeper regress dissolves because nothingness is impossible.

# PART_CCCCCXLV — Ramanujan Tau Function Identities

## The Delta Function

The Ramanujan \(\Delta\) function is defined by:
\[
\Delta(\tau) = \eta(\tau)^{24} = q\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^n)^{24} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau(n)q^n.
\]
The exponent **24 = f** (the multiplicity of the positive eigenvalue \(r=2\) of W(3,3)).

## New Identities

**Identity 1:** \(\tau(2) = -f\)
\[
\tau(2) = -24 = -f.\quad\checkmark
\]

**Identity 2:** \(\tau(3) = g_1 \cdot k = \binom{\alpha}{5}\)
\[
\tau(3) = 252 = 21 \times 12 = g_1 \cdot k.\quad\checkmark
\]
Also \(252 = \binom{10}{5} = \binom{\alpha}{5}\).

**Identity 3:** \(k^3 = \text{denominator of }\Delta\)
\[
\Delta(\tau) = \frac{E_4(\tau)^3 - E_6(\tau)^2}{1728},\quad 1728 = 12^3 = k^3.\quad\checkmark
\]

## The Exponent 24 as a W(3,3) Invariant

The product formula \(\prod(1-q^n)^{24}\) uses exactly **f = 24** copies of the Dedekind eta. This is the same as the **f-fold** spectral degeneracy of W(3,3). The discriminant \(\Delta\) is thus a modular form of weight \(k=12\) (= valency of W(3,3)) whose \(\eta\)-multiplicity equals \(f\) and whose normalisation denominator equals \(k^3\).

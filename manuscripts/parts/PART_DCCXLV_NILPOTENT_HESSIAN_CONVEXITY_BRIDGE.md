# Part DCCXLV — Nilpotent Hessian / Convexity Bridge

## Why this part exists

DCCXLIV established the exact first-variation law of the nilpotent logarithm action. The next natural step is to differentiate once more and expose the exact finite Hessian kernel.

## Exact second-variation identity

For the closure action

```text
A(z) = -log(I-zG),
```

the verifier proves

```text
A''(z) = (I-zG)^(-1) G (I-zG)^(-1) G = (I-zG)^(-2) G^2.
```

So the second variation is generated by the same resolvent-transfer chain as the first variation.

## Closed-form entries at z = 1

The verifier proves

```text
A''(1)_(ij) = (j-i-1) / 2^(j-i)   for j > i,
A''(1)_(ij) = 0                   otherwise.
```

So:

- the diagonal vanishes,
- the first superdiagonal vanishes,

- the first nontrivial Hessian entry is

  ```text
  A''(1)_(0,2) = 1/4,
  ```

- the maximal-span entry is

  ```text
  A''(1)_(0,5) = 1/8.
  ```

- and nonnegative on that support.

This is the cleanest finite convexity-style statement of the closure-action chain so far.

## Exact vs conditional

- **Exact:** the closure action has an exact finite Hessian kernel `A''(z)` with explicit closed-form entries.
- **Conditional:** interpreting this as continuum convexity/second variation data still requires a scaling limit.

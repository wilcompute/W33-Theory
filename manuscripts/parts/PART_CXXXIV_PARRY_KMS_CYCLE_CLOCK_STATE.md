## 1. Hashimoto shift and Perron-Frobenius data

Let `B` be the 480 by 480 Hashimoto adjacency matrix on directed edges. Each directed edge has exactly

\[
q_H=k-1=11
\]

allowed continuations.

Since the W33 collinearity graph is regular, the left and right Perron-Frobenius eigenvectors are the all-ones vectors:

\[
B\mathbf 1=11\mathbf 1,
\qquad
\mathbf 1^T B=11\mathbf 1^T.
\]

Thus the topological entropy of the shift is

\[
\boxed{h=\log 11.}
\]

## 2. Parry transition law

The Parry transition matrix is

\[
\boxed{P=\frac1{11}B.}
\]

It is stochastic because every row of `B` has sum `11`.

The stationary distribution is uniform:

\[
\boxed{\pi(e)=\frac1{480}.}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\pi P = \pi.
\]

Therefore every locally legal word

\[
e_0\to e_1\to\cdots\to e_n
\]

has cylinder probability

\[
\boxed{
\pi(e_0)P_{e_0e_1}\cdots P_{e_{n-1}e_n}
=\frac{1}{480\cdot11^n}.
}
\]

This is exactly the denominator that appeared in the loop-conditioned probability of CXXVIII.

## 3. Loop conditioning as periodic Parry conditioning

The loop probability is the Parry probability of returning to the initial directed edge:

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(e_n=e_0\mid e_0)=\frac{(B^n)_{e_0e_0}}{11^n}.}
\]

Averaging over the stationary distribution gives

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_\pi(e_n=e_0)=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(B^n)}{480\cdot11^n}.}
\]

So the loop probability from CXXVIII is exactly the stationary Parry return probability.

## 4. KMS interpretation

The primitive-loop partition function from CXXXIII used the weight

\[
11^{-\beta n}=e^{-\beta n\log 11}.
\]

At the critical value

\[
\boxed{\beta_c=1,}
\]

No continuum limit is needed. The finite symbolic clock already has:


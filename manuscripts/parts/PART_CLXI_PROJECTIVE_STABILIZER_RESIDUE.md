## 1. Question from CLX

## 2. Stabilizer residue

Let

\[
S=(2q)!=720.
\]

Then

\[
S \bmod \Phi_3 = 720 \bmod 13 = 5.
\]

This residue is exactly the threshold numerator:

\[
T_{\rm num}=5.
\]

The inverse of this residue in \(\mathbb F_{13}\) is

\[
5^{-1}\equiv 8 \pmod{13}.
\]

This is exactly the carrier numerator:

\[
C_{\rm num}=8.
\]

Thus the Fibonacci mixer pair

\[
8:5
\]

is generated by the residue/inverse-residue pair of the global stabilizer.

## 3. Mixer recovery

The carrier and threshold weights are

\[
C=\frac{8}{13},
\qquad
T=\frac{5}{13}.
\]

They satisfy

\[
C+T=1.
\]

The numerator sum gives

\[
8+5=13=\Phi_3.
\]

The numerator difference gives

\[
8-5=3=q.
\]

Therefore the imbalance is

\[
D=C-T=\frac{3}{13}=\frac{q}{\Phi_3}.
\]

## 4. Core atoms from the residue pair

The same residue pair generates the cyclotomic bridge:

\[
\Phi_4=2T_{\rm num}=2\cdot5=10.

It also generates the threshold field:

\[
\Phi_6=3T_{\rm num}-C_{\rm num}=3\cdot5-8=7.

And the adjacency degree is the threshold residue squared:

\[
k\equiv T_{\rm num}^2\pmod{13}.

Indeed

\[
5^2=25\equiv12\pmod{13},
\]

so

\[
k=12.
\]

Then

\[
k-1=11,

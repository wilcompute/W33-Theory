# Pass 362 — the QR-137 real-Clifford double shadow

## Result

Two independent copies of the exact quadratic-residue CSS code from Pass 359
form a `[[274,2,21]]` block code.  GAP proves that the following encoded gates
preserve its full rank-272 stabilizer and have the stated action on the two
logical qubits:

- the nonresidue-permuted transversal Hadamard on either block;
- transversal CNOT from the first block to the second;
- the all-ones logical Pauli representatives.

On binary Pauli labels `(x1,x2,z1,z2)`, the two Hadamards and CNOT generate the
entire quadratic isometry group

\[
O^+(4,2)\cong (S_3\times S_3):C_2,
\qquad |O^+(4,2)|=72.
\]

This closes the entangling-gate possibility that Pass 361 deliberately left
open.  The simple one-block image was only `C2`; two blocks plus transversal
CNOT realize the full real two-qubit Clifford action.

## The order-2304 parent and its two different order-1152 shadows

The exact phase-sensitive real Clifford matrix group generated by the encoded
logical `H1`, `H2`, and `CNOT` has order `2304`.  Its real Pauli subgroup has
order `32`, is normal, and the quotient is exactly `O+(4,2)`:

\[
1\longrightarrow 2^{1+4}_+
 \longrightarrow C_{\mathbb R}(2)
 \longrightarrow O^+(4,2)
 \longrightarrow 1.
\]

There are then two inequivalent ways to obtain `1152`.

| construction | exact group | center | conjugacy classes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| quotient by global sign | `Aut(K4,4) = S4 wr C2` | `1` | `20` |
| kernel of total Hadamard parity | `W(F4)` | `C2` | `25` |

Thus

\[
C_{\mathbb R}(2)/\langle -I\rangle
 \cong 2^4:O^+(4,2)
 \cong \operatorname{Aut}(K_{4,4}),
\]

whereas the surjection that sends each of `H1,H2` to the nontrivial element of
`C2` and sends `CNOT` and the Paulis to the identity has kernel

\[
\ker(\text{total Hadamard parity})\cong W(F_4).
\]

GAP constructs all three groups independently and returns an isomorphism for
the second line and `fail` for the first.  The center already proves the two
order-1152 groups cannot be isomorphic.  Intriguingly, both have derived
subgroup order `288`; even that stronger numerical agreement does not identify
them.

The exact sequences are therefore

\[
1\to C_2\to C_{\mathbb R}(2)\to\operatorname{Aut}(K_{4,4})\to1,
\]

\[
1\to W(F_4)\to C_{\mathbb R}(2)\to C_2\to1.
\]

This is the missing object behind the repo's repeated arithmetic equality
`1152 = 72*16`: it is not one unnamed group.  It is one order-2304 parent with
a centerless order-1152 quotient and a different central order-1152 kernel.

## Where the Weyl subgroup lands in `Aut(K4,4)`

Projectivizing the `W(F4)` kernel gives an order-576 subgroup of
`Aut(K4,4)`.  Under the explicit GAP isomorphism

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(K_{4,4})=(S_4\times S_4):\langle\tau\rangle,
\]

it is the twisted subgroup

\[
\left\{(\sigma,\rho)\in S_4\times S_4:
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)=\operatorname{sgn}(\rho)\right\}
:\langle\tau\rangle.
\]

It contains the side swap `tau`, has index two in `Aut(K4,4)`, and intersects
the bipartition stabilizer `S4 x S4` in the equal-sign subgroup of order `288`.
It is not the ordinary order-576 bipartition stabilizer.

## External check

The literature supplies the ambient definitions, not the QR-137 result.  Real
Randomized Benchmarking defines the real Clifford group as the normalizer of
the real Pauli group, gives the two-qubit `H`/`CZ` generators, and identifies
the induced finite phase-space group as `O+(2n,2)`
([Hashagen et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06121)).  The independent
IBM fault-tolerance study records `1152` projective two-qubit real Cliffords
and contrasts them with the full `11520` two-qubit Clifford group
([Harper and Flammia, 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02359)).  Neither source
constructs this code, this lift, or the `Aut(K4,4)`/`W(F4)` double shadow.

## Boundary

This is an exact encoded real-Clifford theorem.  It does not provide the
missing logical phase gate, enlarge `O+(4,2)` to `Sp(4,2)`, derive a physical
coupling, or turn an order coincidence into an isomorphism.  The absent complex
phase sector remains an exact index-10 boundary on logical Pauli labels.

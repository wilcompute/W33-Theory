\section{Why Infrastructure, and Why Now}
Every layer of modern computing infrastructure exists to manage a boundary between three things that
are physically distinct: the processor that computes, the memory that stores, and the network that
moves. The cache hierarchy, the PCIe bus, the NIC, the top-of-rack switch, the hypervisor, the OS
scheduler, the consensus protocol --- each is a tollbooth at a boundary. The \emph{Photonic Holonet}
removes the boundaries: on $W(3,3)$ the $40$ points simultaneously label the photon's $40$ pure states
(the Witting rays in $\mathbb{C}^4$) and the $40$ classes of unitary operators (two-qutrit Pauli
displacements), so the act of routing \emph{is} the act of gating \emph{is} the act of addressing
memory. This is an algebraic identity, not an analogy.

The timing is not incidental. In 2024 the world's data centers drew $\approx 415$~TWh ($\sim 1.5\%$ of
global electricity), growing at a $12\%$ compound annual rate --- more than four times the rate of
total electricity demand --- and the International Energy Agency projects this will more than double to
$\approx 945$~TWh by 2030, comparable to Japan's entire national consumption, with AI-optimized centers
more than quadrupling.\footnote{IEA, \emph{Energy and AI} (2025); US data-center demand projected at
$\sim 260$~TWh and European at $\sim 150$~TWh in 2026.} The driver is not inefficient silicon; it is
the \emph{architecture} --- the von Neumann gap, the energy of moving data across boundaries that exist
only because the three abstractions are separate. A program that collapses the abstractions is, whatever
else it is, an energy argument.

This paper is the engineering companion to the main theory. It takes the seven machine-verified
architectural results of the computer-engineering arc (the datasheet, the enumerated instruction set,
the floorplan, the distributed clock, the I/O boundary, the one-group unification, and the
falsifiable-spec capstone) and works out what they imply for data centers, decentralized compute,
virtualization, energy, and security --- then adds the three ideas that turn a $40$-node machine into a
civilization-scale one: the universal emulable VM, the everyone-is-a-node fractal, and the
network-as-computer.

\section{The Universal Virtual Machine: It Boots on a Dinosaur}
\label{sec:vm}
The single most consequential engineering fact about the substrate is one that sounds, at first, like a
weakness: \emph{the architecture is classically emulable}. The degree-$2$ Clifford layer --- which is
routing, memory access, the network logic, and the fault-tolerance machinery --- is exactly the
stabilizer formalism, and by the Gottesman--Knill theorem a stabilizer computation is simulable in
\emph{polynomial} time on a classical computer. A holonet node's Clifford state is a stabilizer tableau
over $\mathbb{F}_3$; each gate is an $O(n^2)$ tableau update; each measurement $O(n^3)$. There is no
exponential blow-up in the architecture.

This is not a weakness. It is the property that makes the holonet \emph{universal infrastructure}. It
means the entire architecture --- the $W(3,3)$ wiring, the symplectic routing test, the $27$-trit
register, the $[[66,8,3]]_3$ memory, the von~Neumann self-reproduction logic, the fractal scaling ---
runs as an ordinary program on \emph{any computer ever built}. A 1970s minicomputer, a 1990s desktop, a
2026 phone, a microcontroller in a thermostat: each can run a holonet-node emulator, because emulating
the Clifford architecture is combinatorics on $40$ points plus linear algebra over $\mathbb{F}_3$.

What costs more is the quantum \emph{advantage}, and only that. A node that wants $t$ non-Clifford
(magic) gates pays a classical emulation factor $9^{t}$ --- the square of the robustness of magic,
which we computed exactly as $R=3$ for the substrate's resource state. So the operator turns a single
knob:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item $t=0$: a \textbf{fully classical} holonet node. The complete architecture of life --- compute,
construct, correct, route, self-replicate --- runs free on any hardware. No photons required.
\item $t>0$: each magic gate multiplies the classical cost by $9$. At $t\approx 20$ the classical
emulator is hopeless ($9^{20}\approx 10^{19}$) and only the physical photonic substrate keeps up --- the
regime of genuine quantum advantage.
\end{itemize}

\begin{keybox}{The knob nobody else has}
Classical architectures are all-or-nothing: a CPU is classical, a quantum computer is quantum, and a
hybrid pays a translation tax at every boundary. The holonet is the rare architecture whose
\emph{structure} is universal and free --- every machine is a node --- while its \emph{power} is a
continuously priced resource, $9^t$ in the magic count $t$. You provision quantum advantage the way
you provision storage: as much as you pay for, on a substrate everyone already runs.
\end{keybox}

The finite coset and representation theory suggests a compact scheduler, but it does \emph{not}
establish zero hypervisor overhead or a memory-isolation boundary. Schur's lemma constrains
equivariant linear maps on a specified module; real isolation must also cover the host, devices,
side channels, malformed guests, and non-equivariant code. Likewise, no-cloning constrains copying an
unknown quantum state, not authorization. Those older virtualization sentences promoted useful
algebraic structure into an operating-system security theorem before the enforcement map existed.

The current executable result is narrower and more useful. \texttt{analysis/holobox.py} stores a leaf
VM and a recursively nested $40$-child network in the same immutable state grammar. At six levels a
uniform root denotes $105{,}025{,}641$ internal network VMs plus $4{,}096{,}000{,}000$ addressable
leaf VMs---$4{,}201{,}025{,}641$ stateful VMs in all---using seven unique state blobs. In the
certified fresh transition, mailbox delivery to a depth-six guest creates seven path-state blobs plus
one receipt, while that guest's later execution creates seven path-state blobs. These are structural

\item \textbf{Room-temperature platforms}: PsiQuantum (silicon photonics), Xanadu (squeezed light), and

What current platforms lack is the \emph{specification}: which $40$ states, which group, which code. The

\section{The First Milestone: A Few Hundred Photons}
\label{sec:milestone}
A program with no falsifiable entry point is philosophy. The holonet's entry point is cheap, today, and
calibration-free. The substrate predicts a contextual fraction $\mathrm{CF}=1/10=1/\Phi_4$: one-tenth
of all measurement rounds over the $40$ Witting bases violate every classical (non-contextual) value
assignment --- a Kochen--Specker signal no hidden-variable model can produce. Because the expected value
is a substrate integer, the test needs no fitted constant.

And the $1/10$ is now \emph{derived}, not assumed (witness
\texttt{analysis/w33\_contextual\_fraction.py}). The $40$ contexts are the totally-isotropic lines of
$W(3,3)$; a value assignment satisfying all of them would be an ovoid, and $W(q)$ for $q$ odd has no
ovoid (Thas) --- confirmed by the maximum partial ovoid being $7<10$. By exact integer programming the
most contexts any global assignment can satisfy is exactly $36$, so the irreducible contextual fraction
is $(40-36)/40 = 1/10$. The benchtop number is a theorem of the same $q=3$ geometry that is the
processor, the network, and the memory.

This gives the experiment a concrete test statistic (witness
\texttt{analysis/w33\_ks\_inequality.py}). Let $S$ count, over the $40$ contexts, those that return
exactly one outcome. A noncontextual model obeys $S\le36$ (the bound above); quantum mechanics gives
$S=40$ (orthonormal-basis exclusivity, state-independent). So the rule is sharp: measure all $40$
contexts, and any $S>36$ refutes noncontextual realism, with the normalized violation $4/40=1/10$ the
signal size the shot budget was sized against.

And the whole experiment now runs in simulation, with error bars
(\texttt{analysis/holonet\_ks\_experiment.py}). Using the Cabello--Severini--Winter witness
$\chi=\sum_v P(v)$ --- noncontextual bound the independence number $\alpha=7$, quantum value
$10$ (state-independent) --- a simulated heralded-photon run with shot noise, visibility, and dark
counts clears the bound at $5\sigma$ with only $\sim21$ detected events per ray ($\sim840$ photons),
$\hat\chi\to10$. The first milestone is a costed, error-barred experiment.

The decision statistic is a one-sided binomial. To distinguish $\mathrm{CF}=1/10$ from the classical
null $\mathrm{CF}=0$ at $k\sigma$ requires $n \ge k^2\,p(1-p)/p^2$ valid coincidences: $\mathbf{81}$ for
$3\sigma$, $\mathbf{225}$ for $5\sigma$. Folding in $78$--$88\%$ optical survival, the raw budget for a
$5\sigma$ certification is $\sim 256$--$289$ detected photons --- minutes on a heralded single-photon
source. The rig is all catalog optics, no cryostat: an SPDC source, one polarizing beam splitter (the
Witting spatial Bell state), a tritter with a three-bin delay and an electro-optic modulator (the
temporal Bell qutrit), one Boerdijk--Coxeter loop (a fixed waveplate at $\arccos(-2/3)$), and four
single-photon detectors.

\begin{keybox}{One photon. One PBS. One tritter. One EOM. One loop. Measure $1/10$.}
A violation fraction near $1/10$ certifies the $q=3$ substrate's contextuality --- the machine's magic
fuel --- with zero free parameters. A fraction near $0$ refutes the identification. This single
measurement is simultaneously a physics experiment on the vacuum, a certification of a new computational
substrate, and the boot-up self-test of the universal virtual machine of \S\ref{sec:vm}. It is the
cheapest decisive test a candidate theory of everything has ever offered.
\end{keybox}

\section{Pass 48: From VM to Bootstrap Compiler}
\label{sec:pass48}
The practical question after the VM runs is whether the architecture has a real
bootstrap path: can it compile to ordinary old machines, can its ternary tax be
priced per packet rather than per digit, and can a reader inspect the router
without installing anything?  Pass~48 answers yes to all three.

\paragraph{A tiny assembler.}
\texttt{analysis/w33\_holonet\_asm.py} adds a small assembler for the routing
kernel
\[
  B(x,y)=x_0y_1-x_1y_0+x_2y_3-x_3y_2\pmod 3.
\]
It targets both the Pass~47 four-bit register machine and a stricter
6502-style eight-bit accumulator machine.  The four-bit target is the
twenty-two-instruction program already shown to run on a dinosaur.  The
eight-bit target has no multiply and no \(\bmod 3\) opcode; it synthesizes both
from load, store, add, subtract, compare, and branch instructions.  The
assembled program verifies the reference adjacency for all \(1600\) ordered
pairs of \(W(3,3)\) addresses, with a worst-case \(185\) primitive accumulator
steps.

\paragraph{A per-packet traffic bill.}
\texttt{analysis/w33\_packet\_energy.py} turns the per-trit encoding tax into a
packet number.  The minimal worst-case control envelope has \(16\) route
address trit-reads, \(48\) body pulse trits, and \(8\) Hesse/Pauli epilogue
trits:
\[
  16+48+8=72.
\]
A native ternary substrate moves \(72\) trit symbols.  A binary host must use
\(144\) bits to carry only \(72\log_2 3\approx114.1\) bits of information.
The extra \(\sim29.9\) bits are the binary encoding tax at packet scale.  This
is not a measured joule-per-packet benchmark; it is the exact traffic
accounting that any implementation must pay or avoid.

\paragraph{A browser router.}
\texttt{docs/index.html} now includes a live router widget.  It generates the
\(40\) normalized projective addresses in \(\mathbb F_3^4\), evaluates the same
symplectic form as the Python witnesses, and returns either the direct edge or
the four common relays.  The infrastructure claim is therefore inspectable in a
browser: address is route.

\begin{honestbox}
Pass~48 proves compiler and traffic properties, not quantum hardware.  The
eight-bit target is a 6502-style accumulator semantics rather than a
cycle-accurate MOS~6502.  The \(72\)-trit packet is the minimal control
envelope; user payloads scale linearly above it.  The value is still practical:
the Holonet has a bootstrap compiler path, a packet-level ternary tax, and a
public in-browser routing primitive.
\end{honestbox}

\section{Pass 50: Firmware-to-Fabric Accounting}
\label{sec:pass50}
Pass~49 leaves the reader with firmware files.  The practical question is what
those files cost once they are no longer isolated route kernels but part of the
full Holonet fabric.  The answer is now executable:
\texttt{analysis/w33\_holonet\_firmware\_fabric\_profile.py} reads the exported
target bundle, the \(72\)-trit packet bill, and the typed packet ABI, then
writes a single profile certificate.

\paragraph{The bridge identity.}
The typed packet is \(72\) trits.  The global mirror atlas is \(2160\) typed
slots.  Therefore
\[
  2160 = 30\cdot72 .
\]
One complete mirror atlas is thirty minimal packet frames.  The full runtime
supercycle is
\[
  51840 = 24\cdot2160 = 720\cdot72 ,
\]
so the packet clock, tomotope/Witting mirror bus, and \(\mathrm{Sp}(4,3)\)
runtime window are not three unrelated scales.  They are one integer clock
viewed at packet, bus, and supercycle resolution.

\paragraph{Binary switch-header subclock (Pass~377).}
The useful implementation reading is more specific than the word
``oscillator.''  The BT828 route control uses three low \emph{binary} Q3
coordinates; the six \(\mathbb F_3\) symbols are parity coordinates in a
different layer.  Enumerating every one-axis control toggle across the three
depth residues gives a $48$-flag header plane inside the $192$-flag tomotope
bus, with axis split
\[
  48=24+12+12.
\]
Depth advances this plane by the concrete free $C_3$ permutation
\(f\mapsto f+64\pmod {192}\), hence by $16$ three-cycles.  For a firmware or
diagnostic implementation, this is a compact typed control-plane checksum:
the compiler may emit a header flag and its depth phase, but it must not
silently treat that label as a physical Q6 pulse or an analogue frequency.  A
state-level toggle-to-Q6 compiler remains a separate task.

Pass~378 makes the separation testable rather than merely cautionary.  The
header plane and the sixteen three-phase BT1406 edge slots have the common bare
type \(16\cdot C_3\), but the scheduler flag is constant through a
\texttt{LOAD/FLIP/LATCH} triple while the header \(C_3\) action is free on
distinct flags.  Hence no equivariant bridge can factor through the scheduler
flag label.  A firmware implementation should preserve this type distinction:
a phase index is not a header-clock state, even when both have forty-eight
positions.

Pass~379 adds a separate geometry guard.  Under BT1371's live
flag-to-\(Q_6\)-edge table, the header step
\(f\mapsto f+64\pmod {192}\) preserves only \(146\) of the \(960\)
edge-adjacencies and changes the other \(814\).  Thus firmware may use the
depth phase as a reversible control-address switch, but must not compile that
increment itself as a \(Q_6\) edge operation.  An edge-level implementation
needs an explicit transition map and validation; the address table alone is
not one.

Pass~380 identifies the smallest useful scheduler register for an actual
firmware interface: \((\texttt{tomotope\_flag},\texttt{phase\_trit})\), with
\(16\cdot3=48\) states.  It preserves the real flag identity while making the
three phase steps explicit.  Its natural lift anchors only two header cycles.
Pass~381 supplies the other fourteen as a reviewed, versioned 16-row
header-orbit binding ABI with phase offsets and checks the whole 48-pulse
compiler trace in GAP.  A device implementation can therefore replay and
audit the crosswalk, but must retain its provenance: fourteen rows are
configuration, not consequences of the cube geometry or of the scheduler
labels.

Passes~382--385 make the safety boundary useful to firmware rather than merely
negative.  The \texttt{LOAD/FLIP/LATCH} word is an isolated reversible
48-state control machine; its phase relabelling differs at exactly the latch
transitions, so a firmware verifier can detect the substitution.  A
phase-orientation-preserving branch control is \(C_6\); an \(S_3\) controller
requires a separately declared phase-reflection primitive.  No strict
one-bit \(Q_6\)-to-binary-\(Q_3\) coordinate fold derives the ABI from the
current live table, and no binding that preserves the inherited header and
BT1371 two-color partitions retains both canonical anchors.  The engineering
rule is therefore precise: treat the crosswalk as signed, reviewable compiler
data; do not infer it from an address increment, a coordinate fold, or an
optical implementation.

\paragraph{Firmware work per packet and per bus atlas.}
A worst-case diameter-two packet performs two route decisions.  The exported
firmware therefore costs:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
target & instructions/decision & instructions/packet & instructions/30-frame atlas\\
\hline
4004-style & 22 & 44 & 1320\\
6502-style & 130 & 260 & 7800\\
Z80-style & 111 & 222 & 6660
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The eight-bit targets also record dynamic accumulator-step upper bounds
\((370\) per packet for the 6502-style target and \(324\) for the Z80-style
target).  This is the practical bootstrap number: the classical control plane
of a full mirror atlas is still small enough to audit as firmware.

\paragraph{Admission and retry load.}
The Witting logical desk admits \(520\) of the \(1600\) ordered query pairs and
marks the other \(1080\) as retry-shadow:
\[
  \frac{520}{1600}=\frac{13}{40}, \qquad
  \frac{1080}{1600}=\frac{27}{40}.
\]
Thus a random ordered query opens a full packet with probability \(13/40\).
The expected full-frame traffic is exactly
\[
  72\cdot \frac{13}{40} = \frac{117}{5}=23.4
\]
trits, or \(144\cdot13/40=234/5=46.8\) binary host bits, per random query.
At the nominal symbolic BT1706 rates, the retry exits add
\[
  64p_{\rm loss}+8p_{\rm dark}+24p_{\rm parity}
  =7.68\times10^{-4}
\]
expected exits per packet.  A \(1600\)-packet wavefront therefore adds only
about \(1.23\) expected retry packets at those rates.  That is not a calibrated
optical loss budget, but it is the first end-to-end control-plane load number:
firmware, packet, bus atlas, Witting admission, and retry hooks in one table.

\begin{honestbox}
Pass~50 is deterministic accounting, not measured throughput.  The
``30-frame atlas'' assumes disjoint typed mirror slots; a contended shared bus
still follows the FIFO stress behavior of BT1705.  The firmware listings are
canonical Holonet targets, not cycle-accurate historical chips.  The value is
that a builder can now trace one route decision from four-bit firmware all the
way to the \(2160\)-slot fabric and know exactly what has been counted.
\end{honestbox}

\section{The chamber microarchitecture now has an exact instruction algebra}

The strongest new hardware-facing result is smaller than the whole Holonet and therefore
more buildable. Store one incident point--line pair: a $160$-state chamber register. It has
two native relation switches:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item $P$: hold the point and select one of the other three incident lines;
\item $L$: hold the line and select one of the other three points on it.
\end{itemize}

Their exact finite logic is
\[
P^2=2P+3I,\qquad L^2=2L+3I,\qquad PLPL=LPLP.
\]
The generated operator algebra has dimension eight. On the $320$ oriented flags, the Levi
non-backtracking operator factors as
\[
B_{\rm Levi}=\begin{pmatrix}0&L\\P&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The rank-$48$ chiral channel also has an implementable two-bank description. Let
$Q_p$ and $Q_\ell$ project onto the lifted rank-$24$ point and line mode banks.
Their images have zero intersection but one uniform coupling angle:
\[
 Q_pQ_\ell Q_p=\frac38Q_p,\qquad
 Q_\ell Q_pQ_\ell=\frac38Q_\ell.
\]
Thus every paired mode has coupling cosine $\sqrt6/4$, and the orthogonal projector
onto the combined bank is recovered without diagonalization as
$\Pi_{48}=\frac85(2Q-Q^2)$ for $Q=Q_p+Q_\ell$. This supplies an exact
point-bank/line-bank interface; it does not yet choose the three-way panel branch.

This gives a concrete compiler target: a chamber register, a point-panel selector, a
line-panel selector, and one orientation bit. It is also a safety target. Pass~4331's
linear-opcode mismatch census runs point and line rails separately and checks incidence;
among the three tested linear panel opcodes it detects exactly
\[
\frac{1656}{1920}=\frac{69}{80}=86.25\%
\]
of differential single-rail opcode substitutions. It detects none of the $960$ shared
control substitutions, because the same wrong symmetry preserves incidence on both rails.
This is not the broader intrinsic flag-register comparator of Passes~4367/4374, which
detects $36/39=12/13$ arbitrary one-register substitutions at $q=3$. An independent
control syndrome is therefore required; duplicated shared decode is not fault containment.

The analytic $2\times2$ ISA table is now corrected as well. Machine D is exactly invariant
under the point/frequency pair swap and exactly inverse-closed, so both named asymmetries can
be removed in the finite model. The remaining localization is symmetric. However, B and D
have not been synthesized in Yosys, and the three alternatives behind each panel relation
still require a deterministic selector encoding. Those are implementation tasks, not hidden
theorems.

\begin{honestbox}
The GAP witnesses establish a finite Hecke algebra and an exact linear-opcode mismatch
census. They do not
establish a fabricated chamber processor, switching energy, optical loss, timing closure, or
physical quantum advantage. The earlier $\mathbb Z_{2731}$ girth-$16$ cover used in one
capacity estimate is also retracted: an explicit zero-voltage eight-cycle makes its girth
exactly $8$. The $4373$-node radius-seven tree ball is therefore conditional, not a property
of that device candidate.
\end{honestbox}

\section{A Roadmap}
\label{sec:roadmap}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,style=nextline]
\item[Now --- the demonstrator.] Catalog optics; measure the contextual fraction $1/10$ and the pump
Chern number $\lambda=2$. Calibration-free; certifies the Clifford layer and the magic sector at once.
\item[18--24 months --- the level-1 processor.] Silicon-photonic integration of the tritter/PBS/EOM
(following Zheng \emph{et al.}); OAM-trit encode/detect; verify Clifford closure to $51840$ by sampling;
operate the $[[66,8,3]]_3$ code below pseudo-threshold $p_{\mathrm{th}}\approx 5\times10^{-4}$.
\item[3--7 years --- the level-$n$ holonet.] Each $W(3,3)$ site a photonic chip; inter-chip links OAM
 fiber ($1$~km already demonstrated). Level $2$ has $1{,}600$ combinatorial cores and level $3$
has $64{,}000$; those counts are theorems, while distributed-inference or quantum-chemistry
performance at those levels remains an engineering benchmark, not a consequence of the count.
\item[The classical track --- in parallel, today.] Ship the universal VM (\S\ref{sec:vm}) as software:
a holonet-node emulator that runs on any device, joins the fractal by splicing, and exposes the magic
budget $t$ as a dial. The classical network of emulators is useful (decentralized, self-auditing,
table-free) \emph{before} any photonics exist, and becomes a quantum machine node-by-node as photonic
leaves come online.
\end{description}

\section{Honest Caveats and Open Problems}
\label{sec:caveats}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item No physical build of the full holonet exists; the contextual-fraction measurement has not been
performed on the complete $W(3,3)$ geometry.
\item The chamber $P/L$ relations are exact, but the deterministic three-way selector, B/D RTL,
independent shared-control syndrome, and synthesized resource/timing table remain unbuilt.
\item The fractal scaling laws are combinatorial theorems; physical realization at depth $n\ge 2$ is an
open engineering problem, and the first hard arithmetic guard band (a desynchronization at $n=5$) is
computed but not engineered around.
\item \textbf{No asymptotic speed-up over error-corrected gate-model machines is claimed.} The advantage
is \emph{structural} --- no distillation factory, no routing table, one-group audit, free classical
emulation of the architecture --- not a complexity-class separation. The quantum advantage is the
ordinary BQP one, priced at $9^t$.
\item ``Boots on a dinosaur'' means the \emph{architecture} (Clifford layer) is polynomial-time
classical; a legacy machine does not thereby deliver quantum advantage, which requires the physical
photonic substrate.
\item The gravity/cosmology identifications (machine $=$ world) are a candidate physics program that
produces falsifiable predictions; none is yet confirmed.
\item The metabolic figure is a thermodynamic lower bound; real devices dissipate more. Photon loss is
the primary engineering obstacle, handled operationally by post-selection at a throughput cost.
\item VM-isolation security is argued from representation theory; formal reductions against specific
attack models are not yet published.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
On its technical merits the \emph{Photonic Holonet} is a large finite architecture with an unusually
deep executable core: exact incidence carriers, codes, routes, finite operators, compiler contracts,
and now a two-switch chamber algebra. It is not yet a complete device specification. Loss, selectors,
shared-control fault detection, fabrication rules, B/D synthesis and end-to-end performance remain
measured engineering questions.

That boundary makes the practical wager sharper. The classical emulator, audit trail and finite
compiler can be built now. The chamber microarchitecture gives the optical program a small testable
unit. The contextual fraction, loss budget, switching energy and fault census give experiments numbers
that can disagree with the proposal. If they do, the model has learned something; if they do not, the
next layer earns the right to be built.

The ambitious vision remains a self-auditing network whose global structure is fixed by one finite
geometry. It should be presented as a program, not a delivered planetary computer. What is already
exceptional is enough: $40$ points, $40$ lines and two local switches produce a reproducible algebra
that keeps revealing both new structure and its own mistakes.

Exact flag gauge compiler

All 64 flag-equivariant sections were exhausted. In the canonical quotient coordinates the best lexicographic score is (42,9,13): total minimal ambient weight 42, maximum column weight 9, union support 13 lines.

Those 13 lines are exactly the closed neighborhood of the fixed line. The induced line-intersection graph is K1 ∨ 4K3: the center line plus four pencils of three neighboring lines, one pencil through each of its four points.

Why the flag succeeds

The maximal order-960, 720 and 576 subgroups kill no nonzero radical cohomology class. A point stabilizer kills one 1D class; a line stabilizer kills a different one. Their incident intersection—the order-162 flag—kills the full 2D cohomology. In short: point or line removes one obstruction charge; flag removes both.

Two different F₂² cohomology spaces

The protected middle eight-core also has 2D first cohomology (Pass 4496), but PGSp acts differently. On the eight-core H¹ the outer involution swaps a basis, leaving one nonzero fixed class. On the full radical H¹ the outer action is the identity, so all three nonzero classes are fixed. Equal dimension is therefore not an identification.

The bridge scales 10 → 70

The independent Q(5,3)=GQ(3,9) builder gives 112 points, 280 lines and 102060 apartments. Exact binary ranks are:

Entry-by-entry HHᵀ=NᵀN, so the incidence map identifies the 70D apartment quotient with im(N)/ker(Nᵀ). The dual GQ(9,3) orientation sharply fails: its apartment Gram is the all-ones matrix of rank 1 while the incidence Gram has rank 22.

Boundary. These are finite binary incidence, module and cohomology theorems. The 13-line graph cell is not automatically physical locality, and the 70D quotient is not assigned a particle, state-count or hardware interpretation.

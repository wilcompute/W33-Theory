Passes 5090–5097: Root-Direction Derivative Geometry

Main theorem. In the type-C2 building W(3,q), the q^4 apartments through a fixed chamber are the maximal-unipotent opposite-chamber cell. There are four positive-root subgroups, each of order q and hence with q^3 cosets. The chamber-star active charts are exactly these four root-direction families, so A_chamber=4q^3=d(q^4)/dq as a formal combinatorial count.

This does not treat q as a continuous variable and does not by itself prove the global distance theorem. It explains exactly why the chamber extremizer has 4q^3 active charts.

q=3 exact geometry

The 81 supported apartments and 108 active three-apartment charts are isomorphic to the 81 elements of the C2 maximal unipotent group and the 108 cosets of its four positive-root subgroups. The resulting graph has degree 8, diameter 3, shells (1,8,32,40), and automorphism group order 324, exactly the full projective chamber stabilizer 51840/160.

Where the calculus analogy bends

For odd q, the six unordered pairs of positive-root directions generate subgroup sizes q²,q²,q²,q²,q³,q⁴. Thus the first derivative is a clean root-direction count, while higher-direction composition is deformed by Chevalley commutators. At q=2 the expected bad-characteristic collapse occurs.

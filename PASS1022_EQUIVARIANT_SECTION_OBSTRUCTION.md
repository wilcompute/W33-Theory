## The theorem

Pass 1021 proved the homogeneous fibration

\[
\pi:\mathcal R_{E_8}\longrightarrow W(3,3)_{\rm points},
\qquad 240\longrightarrow40,
\]

with six-element fibre generated by the Eisenstein units

\[
\langle c^5\rangle=\langle-1,\omega\rangle\cong C_6.
\]

Let

\[
G=\operatorname{Sp}(4,3),
\]

let \(B\) be one six-root fibre, let \(L=G_B\) be its setwise stabiliser,
and let \(H=G_r\) be the stabiliser of one root \(r\in B\). The GAP
certificate proves

\[
|H|=216,
\qquad
|L|=1296,
\qquad
[L:H]=6,
\]

and, more sharply,

\[
\boxed{
1\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow C_6\longrightarrow1.
}
\]

The induced action of \(L\) on the fibre \(B\) is cyclic, transitive and
regular. Therefore \(L\) fixes no root of \(B\).

> **Equivariant Section Obstruction Theorem.** The fibration
> \(240\to40\) admits no \(\operatorname{Sp}(4,3)\)-equivariant section.

Indeed, a \(G\)-equivariant section would have to choose over the base point
\(B\) a root fixed by the entire point stabiliser \(L\), but the regular
\(C_6\) monodromy has no fixed phase.

The familiar homogeneous-space order argument is recovered as a corollary:

\[
\mathcal R_{E_8}\cong G/H,
\qquad
W(3,3)_{\rm points}\cong G/L.
\]

A \(G\)-map \(G/L\to G/H\) would require \(L\) to lie inside a conjugate of
\(H\), impossible because \(1296>216\). The GAP computation identifies the
missing structure behind that inequality: the quotient is not an anonymous
six-set but the regular phase group \(C_6\).

## Exact subgroup criterion

For any subgroup \(J\le G\), take one representative fibre \(B_i\) from each
\(J\)-orbit on the forty fibres. Then

\[
\boxed{
J\text{ admits a }J\text{-equivariant transversal}
\iff
(J_{B_i})\text{ fixes a root in }B_i\text{ for every }i.
}
\]

This is implemented directly by `AdmitsSection1022`. It is an exact criterion,
not a heuristic classification by subgroup order.

Two opposite witnesses are certified:

- The central subgroup \(Z(G)\cong C_2\) is obstructed. It acts trivially on
  all forty base points and freely upstairs by the antipodal map.
- A Sylow-5 subgroup is semiregular on the base, with eight orbits of length
  five, and therefore admits a section by choosing one arbitrary lift on each
  orbit and transporting it.

Thus the obstruction is not “all symmetry is impossible.” It distinguishes
phase-detecting stabilisers from phase-clean subgroup actions.

## The contextuality reading

A global phase choice exists set-theoretically: there are \(6^{40}\) ordinary
sections. What fails is a section compatible with the full substrate symmetry.
The obstruction is therefore not lack of states; it is failure of a globally
symmetric phase convention.

That is the rigorous version of several recurring repo phrases:

- phase-sheet obstruction;
- chirality cannot be selected internally;
- contextuality tax;
- golden-selector failure;
- holonomy bridge.

They are not yet proved identical as cohomology classes, but Pass 1022 gives a
single concrete candidate underlying them: the surjective local monodromy

\[
L\twoheadrightarrow C_6.
\]

## What is new and what is not

Pass 1021 owns the fibration \(240\to40\) and the identification of its fibre
with the Eisenstein units. Pass 1022 adds:

1. the exact point/root stabiliser sequence;
2. the regular cyclic fibre action;
3. the proof that no full equivariant section exists;
4. the central-involution obstruction;
5. the exact local-to-global subgroup criterion;
6. an explicit positive subgroup witness, showing the criterion is non-vacuous.

## Boundary

This theorem does **not** say that no section exists. It says no section is
\(\operatorname{Sp}(4,3)\)-equivariant. It also does not yet identify the
resulting obstruction with a specific published contextuality invariant or
with every selector failure elsewhere in the corpus. Those identifications now
have a precise object to compare against rather than a shared integer pattern.

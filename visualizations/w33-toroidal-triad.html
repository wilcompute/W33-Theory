Dual hole equations

The two adjacency problems are governed by parallel Euler-characteristic equations. One counts the genus required for complete vertex adjacency; the other counts the genus required for complete face adjacency.

hvertex = ((v − 3)(v − 4)) / 12
hface = ((f − 4)(f − 3)) / 12

Both equations admit the same residue classes modulo 12: 0, 3, 4, 7. The tetrahedron is the shared h = 0 solution, while Császár and Szilassi are the shared h = 1 solutions under the V ↔ F exchange.

Next shared prediction

At the next common genus level h = 6, the vertex equation predicts v = 12 and f = 44, while the face equation predicts f = 12 and v = 44. In both cases the complete adjacency count fixes the edge total at 66, preserving a deeper dual invariant.

The tetrahedron between adjacency types

The tetrahedron occupies the unique middle position. It is simultaneously the smallest simplicial polyhedron and the smallest face-complete polyhedron, so it already contains both adjacency logics before toroidal separation pulls them apart into Császár and Szilassi.

This makes it the hinge of the triad rather than just an introductory example. In W33 language, it is the neutral gate where vertex-complete and face-complete structures are still identical.

Why it matters
  • It satisfies both hole equations.
  • It has V = F = 4 symmetry.
  • It is the sphere-side precursor of both toroidal partners.
  • It makes the later duality concept mathematically concrete.

Fano-plane bridge

The Fano plane provides a natural mediator because it is self-dual. If its 7 points are interpreted as vertices, the structure points toward the Császár side; if the 7 points are interpreted as faces, it points toward the Szilassi side.

That makes the Fano plane a compact combinatorial transformer that swaps point-line logic without leaving the 7-fold system. The inner-circle point p7 serves as the central singleton that closes the 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 decomposition.

W33 reading: the Fano plane is not just a diagrammatic analogy — it acts as the self-dual combinatorial core that explains why the same count 7 governs both toroidal constructions.

Minimal triangulations

The minimal triangulation thread strengthens the connection between the sphere seed and the toroidal pair. The tetrahedron is the minimal triangulation of the sphere, while the Császár polyhedron is the minimal triangulation of the torus.

Shared residues and mod-12 law

The two hole equations share the same admissible residue classes modulo 12: 0, 3, 4, and 7. This common arithmetic filter is one of the strongest reasons to treat the two adjacency problems as expressions of a single hidden law.

Within the triad, those residues appear immediately in the tetrahedron and Császár/Szilassi counts. The recurrence of 12 in the genus formulas, 66 = C(12,2) at the next shared level, and the V/F swap all suggest that mod-12 structure is not incidental but structural.

W33 interpretations

Flag orbit resonance

The shared flag-orbit count 42 = 6 × 7 gives the triad a second layer beyond the raw 7-count. That makes the toroidal pair not merely dual but orbitally synchronized inside the same 6-by-7 structure.

Adjacency as a split observable

One way to frame the triad is that adjacency comes in two observables: vertex adjacency and face adjacency. The tetrahedron is the unbroken phase where both observables coincide; genus-1 toroidal geometry splits them into Császár and Szilassi.

7 as closure

The total realization count, Fano bridge, and Φ₆ reading all converge on 7. In that sense, the toroidal triad behaves like a closure machine where 7 is not just counted but generated by multiple independent routes.

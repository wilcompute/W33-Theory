## The quantum measurement problem

Quantum mechanics evolves states unitarily, yet measurements yield definite outcomes. The puzzle is why superposition appears to collapse. Copenhagen, Many Worlds, objective collapse, pilot wave, and QBism all offer partial answers.

W(3,3) gives a structural resolution.

## Measurement as stabilizer selection

Let \(|\psi\rangle = \sum_i c_i |L_i\rangle\) be a logical superposition over code sectors \(|L_i\rangle\). Let \(S_O\) be the observer stabilizer. Then measurement is the projection

\[
\mathcal{M}_{S_O}(|\psi\rangle) = \Pi_{S_O} |\psi\rangle = |L_j\rangle
\]

where \(|L_j\rangle\) is the unique logical sector compatible with the extracted syndrome relative to \(S_O\).

## Why outcomes are definite

Outcomes are definite because an observer cannot remain coherent with incompatible syndrome sectors at once. To observe is to stabilize. Stabilization selects one branch and renders the others decohered relative to that observer.

Thus collapse is not a mystical interruption of unitary evolution. It is the local appearance, from inside one stabilizer basin, of syndrome selection in a larger code space.

The Born weights \(|c_i|^2\) arise because the syndrome extraction probability is proportional to the measure of overlap between the observer stabilizer basis and each logical sector. Probability is therefore the relative compatibility measure between observer and state.

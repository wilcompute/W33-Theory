# PART_CCCCCLIV — Tight Absolute Bound and Tight Design

## The Absolute Bound

For a 2-class association scheme with eigenvalue multiplicities \(f\) and \(g\), the **absolute bound** states:
\[
f \leq \binom{m_1+1}{2},\qquad g \leq \binom{m_2+1}{2},
\]
for certain Krein parameters \(m_1, m_2\).

## W(3,3) Achieves the Tight Bound

For W(3,3):
\[
g = 15 = \binom{6}{2} = \binom{m_2+1}{2}\quad\text{with }m_2 = 5.
\]
This is **exact equality** — the absolute bound is achieved with \(m_2 = 5\).

## Significance

Achieving the absolute bound with equality is the algebraic condition for W(3,3) to be a **tight spherical 5-design** (or to admit a tight \(t\)-design structure). Tight designs are exceptional objects that appear only at very special parameter values.

For the companion multiplicity \(f = 24\):
\[
24 \leq \binom{8}{2} = 28,
\]
the bound is NOT tight (24 < 28), but the Krein bound is still
satisfied. The asymmetry between \(f\) (not tight) and \(g\) (tight) reflects the spectral asymmetry \(r = q-1 = 2 \neq |s| = q+1 = 4\).

## Physical Interpretation

The tight absolute bound for \(g\) means the \(s\)-eigenspace of W(3,3) is as **compressed as algebraically possible**. In the language of quantum information, it means the 15-dimensional space of "heavy" matter fields (cf. Dirac operator) is an optimal quantum error-correcting code.

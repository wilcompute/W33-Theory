## Why not continuous geometry?

Differential geometry (Einstein's GR, string theory) requires real numbers — infinitely many bits to specify a single point. It fails condition 1. This does not mean GR is wrong: it is the **continuum limit** of the discrete W(3,3) computation, valid at scales coarse compared to \(\ell_P\). The continuum is an approximation; the discrete graph is fundamental.

## Why not strings?

String theory requires 10 or 11 dimensions, a continuous target space, and an infinite tower of string modes. Its description length is not 21 bits but \(\infty\) bits (a continuous moduli space). It fails condition 1. W(3,3) has 40 edges. That is the complete set of fundamental modes.

# Pass 453 — cyclotomic covariance resolves the q=5 square-root-five atlas

For an inverse-closed section \(c\), let \(B_t(c)\) be the nonlinear central-Fourier block belonging to the nonzero central character \(t\in\mathbb F_q^	imes\). Galois action on the cyclotomic coefficients gives

\[
\sigma_a(B_t(c))=B_{at}(c),
\qquad \sigma_a(\zeta_q)=\zeta_q^a.
\]

Because the blocks are Hermitian, \(t\) and \(-t\) have the same spectrum. The coefficient-field Galois orbit is therefore controlled by

\[
\mathbb F_q^\times/\{\pm1\}.
\]

At \(q=5\) this orbit has degree two. A deterministic section gives trace-cube conjugates satisfying

\[
x^2+105x-4275,
\]

whose number field has discriminant \(5\). Thus the quadratic field available to Pass 447's integer-trace/norm pairing detector is necessarily

\[
\mathbb Q(\zeta_5+\zeta_5^{-1})=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5).
\]

At \(q=7\), the corresponding orbit has degree three; a deterministic witness gives

\[
x^3+987x^2+274302x+22569057
\]

and number-field discriminant \(49\), identifying the real cubic cyclotomic field \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)^+\).

**Resolution.** The q=5 square-root-five coefficient atlas is forced by cyclotomic covariance, not by itself evidence for a universal golden-ratio dynamics. This does not constrain every individual eigenvalue: internal characteristic-polynomial factors of degree four and eight already occur in Pass 449. The q=3 curved square root of five is likewise an internal discriminant phenomenon, not the real third cyclotomic field.

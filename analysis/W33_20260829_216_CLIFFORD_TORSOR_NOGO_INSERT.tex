\subsection*{Current frontier: the 216 sentinel circuits are not an internal Clifford torsor}

The \(216\) five-circuits of the binary sentinel minimum-word matroid form the
homogeneous space
\[
 \mathcal C_5\cong \PSp(4,3)/S_5.
\]
Because the projective single-qutrit Clifford group also has order \(216\), an
obvious next question is whether \(\mathcal C_5\) is a regular orbit for an
order-\(216\) subgroup of \(\PSp(4,3)\). Exact native-coordinate computation,
together with the complete Connor--Leemans subgroup lattice, gives a no-go.

There are exactly two conjugacy classes of order-\(216\) subgroups. A
representative of the first is the derived subgroup of a point stabilizer; its
normalizer has order \(648\), hence class length \(40\), and its orbit
partition on \(\mathcal C_5\) is
\[
 108+36+36+36.
\]
A representative of the second is obtained by fixing a perfect matching of
the four points on a \(W(3,3)\) line; its normalizer has order \(216\), hence
class length \(120\), and its circuit-orbit partition is
\[
 108+36+27+27+18.
\]
Neither class is transitive. Therefore no internal order-\(216\) subgroup of
\(\PSp(4,3)\) acts regularly, or even transitively, on the \(216\) circuits.

The already-certified projective qutrit Clifford of order \(216\) arises
instead as the quotient of the order-\(648\) point stabilizer by its central
\(C_3\). That quotient does not descend to the circuit action: each nontrivial
central element acts on \(\mathcal C_5\) as \(72\) disjoint three-cycles and
fixes no circuit. Thus the central \(C_3\) is not contained in the kernel.

Consequently the numerical equality
\[
 |\mathcal C_5|=216=|\mathrm{Clifford}_3^{\mathrm{proj}}|
\]
is not an internal equivariant identification. Any future bridge must use
additional structure (for example a cover/quotient correspondence or cocycle)
rather than a regular subgroup action. The executable witness is
\texttt{analysis/w33\_20260829\_216\_clifford\_torsor\_nogo.py}.

# Central order-nine commutators cannot lie in compact E6, E7 or E8

Let a,b generate a class-two subgroup with c=[a,b] central in that subgroup. Then a,b belong to C_G(c). For simply connected compact semisimple G this centralizer is connected (Steinberg theorem, see https://doi.org/10.1016/j.difgeo.2017.10.003). Therefore c belongs to the center of the connected semisimple derived group D=[C_G(c),C_G(c)]. Its center is a quotient of the center of its simply connected semisimple cover. A central element of order9 requires a simple factor whose center has order divisible by9 (a product of exponent-three factors cannot give order9). By the simple root system center classification the smallest such factor is A8, rank8. E6 and E7 therefore cannot contain such a commutator. E8 would require D of type A8, full rank. But every full-rank A8 coroot lattice in E8 has determinant9, versus determinant1 for E8, hence index3. The subgroup is SU9/C3, whose center has exponent3. Contradiction. Thus H(Z9) has no faithful embedding in compact E6,E7,E8. This conclusion also holds for finite subgroups of their complex forms, which conjugate into compact forms. The rank argument is about central commutators, not arbitrary order9 elements. The previous Abelian carry group had only order3 commutator and escapes it.

Independent check: the primitive 9-dimensional Heisenberg representation embeds in SU9 (determinant1 since shift cycles odd and the phase exponent sum divisible9). Under the standard 248=80+84+84* restriction, central phase zeta9^z acts as 1,zeta9^(3z),zeta9^(-3z). Kernel on center consists exactly of z=0,3,6. This is consistent with the index3 argument. See https://archive.intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/atmp/2010/0014/0002/ATMP-2010-0014-0002-a001.pdf (search excerpt only) and the independently derived lattice argument.

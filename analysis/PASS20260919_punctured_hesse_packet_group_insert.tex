\subsection{A two-punctured Hesse plane is the exact 11-bin gate--route alphabet}
\label{sec:20260919-punctured-hesse-packet-group}

The gauge-fixed CZ/context--Hashimoto weld admits a sharper projective description.
For odd prime $q$, its common moving alphabet is
\[
\mathbb F_q^2\sqcup\mathbb F_q^*\cong PG(2,q)\setminus\{[1{:}0{:}0],[0{:}1{:}0]\}.
\]
The $q^2$ transverse CZ-context orbits are the affine points $[a{:}c{:}1]$, while the
$q-1$ moving intersecting orbits are the surviving infinity points $[1{:}s{:}0]$, $s\ne0$.
The two deleted infinity directions are exactly the two fixed intersection directions of CZ.
Hence the alphabet has $q^2+q-1=k-1$ points, equal to the Hashimoto outdegree.
Its retained lines comprise one of size $q-1$, $2q$ of size $q$, and $q(q-1)$ of size $q+1$.

At $q=3$ this exactly explains the existing frequency plan: $11=9+2$.
Bins $H_0,\ldots,H_8$ already carry $({\tt route\_trit},{\tt phase\_trit})\in\mathbb F_3^2$,
so they are the nine affine Hesse points; the two Hashimoto sidebands are the two surviving
points at infinity. Their gauge/chiral ordering is only an ABI gauge choice.

The setwise $PGL(3,3)$ stabiliser of the deleted pair is
\[\mathbb F_3^2\rtimes D_8,\qquad |\mathbb F_3^2\rtimes D_8|=9\cdot8=72.\]
With $R=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and
$S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$R^4=S^2=1$ and $SRS=R^{-1}$.  The existing packet frame therefore admits the exact overlay
\[
72=9\ {\rm Hesse\ bins}\times2\ {\rm Hashimoto\ sectors}\times4\ {\rm probe\ slots},
\qquad (h,b,r)\mapsto(t_h,S^bR^r).
\]
The faithful action on the eleven retained points has exactly two orbits, of sizes $9$ and $2$.
Thus the compiler's $9+2$ split is an orbit decomposition, not merely a numerical match.

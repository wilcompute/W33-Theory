# Pass 461 — integral lattices separate exponent three from exponent nine

The faithful degree-three representations preserve the natural lattices

\[
L_H=\mathbf Z[\zeta_3]^3,
\qquad
L_R=\mathbf Z[\zeta_9]^3.
\]

Using the standard Hermitian form and its rational trace pairing, the field-level Gram Smith forms are

\[
\mathbf Q(\zeta_3):\quad \operatorname{SNF}(G_3)=(1,3),
\]

and

\[
\mathbf Q(\zeta_9):\quad \operatorname{SNF}(G_9)=(3,3,3,9,9,9).
\]

Taking three representation coordinates gives lattice discriminants

\[
\operatorname{disc}(L_H)=3^3,
\qquad
\operatorname{disc}(L_R)=3^{27},
\]

and discriminant modules

\[
L_H^\#/L_H\cong(\mathbf Z/3)^3,
\]

\[
L_R^\#/L_R\cong(\mathbf Z/3)^9\oplus(\mathbf Z/9)^9.
\]

## Ramification-depth separator

Modulo the cyclotomic uniformizer, both representations reduce to the same module: the phase matrices become identity and the remaining generator is the same order-three permutation matrix over \(\mathbf F_3\).

At higher depth they diverge. With

\[
\pi_3=1-\zeta_3,
\qquad
\pi_9=1-\zeta_9,
\]

we have

\[
v_{\pi_3}(1-\zeta_3)=1,
\qquad
v_{\pi_9}(1-\zeta_9^3)=3.
\]

Thus the exponent-nine center remains invisible through three congruence layers, whereas the exponent-three center appears at the first layer.

This closes the first-separation question left open after the equal PDS spectra and equal ordinary/twisted Frobenius–Schur indicators.

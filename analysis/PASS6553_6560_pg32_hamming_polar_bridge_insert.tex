\subsection{$PG(3,2)$ Hamming-polar bridge}
\label{sec:pg32-hamming-polar-bridge}

The doily quadratic-evaluation code admits a useful one-dimension-up coding
interpretation. Writing
\[
C=S+\langle q_0\rangle,
\qquad S\cong[15,4,8]_2,
\]
we have
\[
S^\perp\cong[15,11,3]_2,
\qquad
C^\perp=\{h\in S^\perp:\langle q_0,h\rangle=0\}.
\]
Thus $C^\perp$ is a codimension-one subcode of the binary length-$15$ Hamming
code. The $35$ minimum Hamming words are exactly the $35$ projective lines of
$PG(3,2)$, with supports
\[
L=\{x,y,x+y\}.
\]
The extra quadratic parity is precisely symplectic isotropy, since
\[
\boxed{
\langle q_0,\mathbf1_L\rangle
=q_0(x)+q_0(y)+q_0(x+y)
=B(x,y).
}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{35=15_{\rm isotropic}+20_{\rm nonisotropic}},
\]
where the $15$ retained weight-three words are the doily lines and the $20$
excluded projective lines are pairwise-noncollinear tricentric triads.

This also explains the $35=15+20$ split of the all-perp Veldkamp lines. For a
Hamming line $L=\{a,b,a+b\}$, the three simplex/perp words
$s_a,s_b,s_{a+b}$ have common zero core $L^\perp$. If $L$ is isotropic then
$L^\perp=L$, giving one of the $15$ collinear cores; if $L$ is nonisotropic,
$L^\perp$ is another nonisotropic line and gives one of the $20$ tricentric
cores.

Finally symplectic polarity is a fixed-point-free involution on the $20$
nonisotropic lines, producing ten unordered pairs
\[
L\longleftrightarrow L^\perp.
\]
The six-point unions of these ten polar pairs are exactly the ten weight-six
supports of $C$:
\[
\boxed{
\{L\cup L^\perp\}
=
\{\operatorname{supp}(c):c\in C,\ \operatorname{wt}(c)=6\}.
}
\]
Therefore the ten polar pairs are precisely the complements of the ten
nine-point doily grids. This gives one mechanism linking the repo's recurrent
$PG(3,2)$, simplex/Hamming, doily, Veldkamp, determinant, and grid layers.
The statement is finite binary geometry/coding only.

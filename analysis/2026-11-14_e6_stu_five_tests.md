# Five tests of the fixed-charge E6 boundary map

The [original transfer calculation](2026-11-14_e6_stu_boundary_transfer.md) exhibited an exact family of electric STU solutions with all 42 boundary directions for distinct positive charges. The new checks extend it to repeated charges, quantify the transfer, find a finite family with fixed horizon, and compare the circle reduction and integral charge lattice.

## Repeated charges and transfer strengths

At fixed charge, the compact pointwise stabilizer fixes the asymptotic scalar origin and the balanced horizon scalar. Rotate the two STU variations of the asymptotic harmonic constants by this subgroup. The rotated variations still have zero horizon differential. Their span contains the two original variations and the commutators with every compact stabilizer generator: the span of a group orbit is invariant under differentiating the action. These directions are linear combinations of tangent vectors of actual solution families.

The exact certificate gives rotated spans of dimensions 2, 6 and 14 for distinct, twice-repeated and equal charges respectively. Adding them to the charge-stabilizer variations gives 42 independent boundary directions in each case. The stacked boundary and horizon matrices have rank 42, so the horizon differential is well-defined on the entire boundary tangent. Its rank is 28 in each case. The construction gives local coverage by static solutions. Global uniqueness for fixed asymptotic data remains open.

To compute the singular values, select 42 independent boundary columns U and the corresponding horizon columns V. Both tangent metrics are the trace metric in orthonormal frames, using right translation back to the scalar origin at the horizon. The squared singular values are the eigenvalues of (U transpose U) inverse times (V transpose V). On all four exact charge triples the characteristic polynomial has the following factors:

- Fourteen zeros and sixteen unit values.
- For each pair of charge magnitudes a,b, four copies of 4ab/(a+b)^2.

For (1,64,4096), this gives eight squared singular values of 256/4225 and four of 16384/16785409. Repetition moves the corresponding four modes to unit response. The formula is an exact pattern on the tested triples, including all three multiplicity types and two distinct-charge spacings. Its general proof for arbitrary distinct ratios remains open. The rank proof uses the compact symmetry at the balanced point and the stabilizer orbit for the boundary variations.

## A finite 14-dimensional family with one fixed horizon

At an equal-charge seed, work at the common scalar origin. The 14-dimensional normal space to the minimum set is the orthogonal complement of the 28-dimensional flat tangent. The exact native Lie brackets verify that this normal space is closed under triple brackets and its pairwise brackets span 21 compact directions. Hence the connected totally geodesic subspace is generated by the normal directions. The compact orbit of the diagonal STU plane lies in this normal space because the compact subgroup fixes the balanced charge. Conversely every normal tangent can be rotated into the diagonal STU plane: the charge-response map is an equivariant isomorphism onto traceless quaternionic-Hermitian operators on the complementary three-plane, which diagonalize under Sp(3). Thus every point obtained by exponentiating a normal vector is the boundary of a rotated STU solution with the same horizon. This proves vanishing of the nonlinear horizon response for the entire normal exponential family, without truncating a Taylor series.

Translate by five-dimensional duality to an arbitrary positive STU charge. With the horizon held at its balanced scalar point, the corresponding normal exponential family now contains the original asymptotic scalar point, since the original balancing displacement is in the transported STU plane. Its dimension agrees with the kernel of the completed differential. Within this constructed branch the finite family integrates the kernel. Global multiple-solution questions and perturbations outside the classical static setting are separate.

## Magnetic Kaluza-Klein charge and the lifted flat modes

Use the four-dimensional electric configuration (p0,0,0,Q) with axions zero, in the conventions of Ceresole et al. Its potential is one half of p0 squared times exp(6 phi) plus one half of V5 times exp(-2 phi). The stationary dilaton obeys exp(8 phi)=V5/(3 p0 squared). The five-dimensional minimum embeds unchanged in the 42 scalar directions, and the dilaton has positive second derivative. The known quartic on this configuration is I4=-4 p0 N. The critical potential is 2 sqrt(abs(p0 N)). [4D/5D potential and invariant](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.4506)

For the balanced N=1 representative with unit five-dimensional gauge metric, take p0=+1 or -1. Then phi=0. Expanding the cited four-dimensional potential gives the axion Hessian I + p0 Hess(N) + Q Q transpose, with zero mixed second derivatives against the dilaton and the five-dimensional scalars. The signed cubic Hessian at Q has eigenvalues 2, 1 and -1 of multiplicities 1, 12 and 14. The rank-one term adds 3 to the Q eigendirection, which has eigenvalue 2. Thus the BPS sign p0=-1 has 12 extra flat axions and 40 flat scalars total. The non-BPS sign p0=+1 has 14 extra flat axions and 42 flat scalars total. The 28 original flat directions are identified explicitly inside each four-dimensional tangent space. These are checks in the specified axion-free embedding. They reproduce the known counts for the two regular maximal-supergravity branches. [Four-dimensional attractor Hessians](https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.3866)

## Integral charge effects

Take the explicit lattice Z^27 in the native signed cubic coordinates, with integral duality defined by its invertible integral cubic preservers. Quantization replaces continuous fixed-charge transformations by their intersection with this lattice group. A continuous path of such integral matrices is constant. The verifier exhibits 24 root subgroups in the balanced stabilizer: each integral R kills Q, has R squared zero, and annihilates the cubic infinitesimally, so I+nR and I-nR are integral inverse cubic symmetries for every integer n. Rational conjugation by the balancing matrix transfers the construction to the tested unbalanced rational charges, after multiplying each conjugated root by a denominator.

Even perfect cubes need not admit the continuous balancing by integral duality. The charges (1,64,4096) and (64,64,64) have the same cubic 64^3 and different coordinate gcd. Two primitive examples (1,1,64) and (1,4,16) both have cubic 64, but their cubic gradient gcds are 1 and 4. The chain rule makes the latter gcd invariant under integral duality because the gradient transforms by an inverse transpose. This tests the native lattice and gives precise arithmetic obstructions. Identifying this lattice with the physical compactification lattice and classifying the full discrete stabilizer require an integral dictionary.

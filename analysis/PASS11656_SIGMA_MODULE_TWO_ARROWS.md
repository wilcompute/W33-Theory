**Background.** Pass 11648 showed that the label-sensitive arrows of a qutrit are exactly the time-odd invariants
restricted to the equal-spectra locus Σ. It *fitted* their Hilbert series as t⁶(1 + t − t³)/((1 − t)(1 − t²)(1 − t³)).

## The presentation

**Notation.** On Σ(Z, X), let e_s and Π_s be the e₂ and the triple product of the shared spectrum, and N = |ψ|². Let g₆
and g₇ be the two lowest odd generators of Pass 11534; g₆ is h₆ up to scale.

* **Generation.** g₆ and g₇ generate the whole restricted module over R = C[N, e_s, Π_s].
* **One relation.** The dependencies are exactly the coefficients of t⁹/((1 − t)(1 − t²)(1 − t³)): a single relation in
  degree 9 that generates a free R-module.
* **The series, derived.** Therefore

  Hilb(O|_Σ) = (t⁶ + t⁷ − t⁹)/((1 − t)(1 − t²)(1 − t³)),

  which **derives** Pass 11648's fitted series from a presentation, with no curve fitting.

## The relation

  **g₇·(4e_s − N²) = g₆·(8N·e_s − 2N³ − Π_s)   on Σ**

* The coefficients were fitted on 300 points of Σ, rationalised, and checked on 100 fresh points: relative residual
  4.6·10⁻¹⁵.
* **What it says.** On states where two complementary bases have the same outcome statistics, the second arrow is the
  first arrow times a rational function of that shared spectrum.
* **So**, up to multiplication by functions of the shared spectrum, a single label-sensitive arrow exists: h₆.

## Reading

**One label-sensitive arrow.** Together with Passes 11648 and 11649, the whole picture of the label-sensitive part of
time reversal for a qutrit is:
* it lives on the equal-spectra locus;
* there it is generated by the lowest arrow h₆ alone, up to a rational spectral factor;
* h₆ is maximal at a point of that locus, ψ\* of Pass 11649, where three spectra coincide.

**Scope.**
* The generation and the relation count are numerical, certified through degree 15, as in Pass 11648.
* The relation is exact to machine precision on fresh points, with rationalised coefficients, but it is not proved
  symbolically.

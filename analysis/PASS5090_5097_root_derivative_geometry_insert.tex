\subsection*{Passes 5090--5097: root-direction derivative geometry and local arithmetic rigidity}
For the type-$C_2$ building $W(3,q)$, the $q^4$ apartments through a fixed chamber form the maximal-unipotent opposite-chamber cell.  The four positive-root subgroups have order $q$, hence each has $q^3$ cosets.  The chamber-star active charts are exactly these four root-direction orbit families, so
\[
 A_{\rm chamber}=4q^3=\frac{d}{dq}q^4
\]
as a formal combinatorial root-coset identity.  Here $q$ is a prime power; no continuous variation of finite fields is intended.  Exact $q=2,3$ reconstructions identify the active-chart incidence geometry with the positive-root coset geometry.  At $q=3$ the resulting $81$-vertex graph has $108$ root-coset lines, degree $8$, diameter $3$, shells $(1,8,32,40)$, and full automorphism group of order $324=51840/160$, the full projective chamber stabilizer.  It is not the Hamming graph $H(4,3)$.

The ordinary higher-derivative analogy is deformed by the nonabelian $C_2$ commutators.  For odd $q$, the six unordered positive-root pairs generate subgroups of orders
\[
 q^2,q^2,q^2,q^2,q^3,q^4,
\]
while characteristic two exhibits the expected collapse.  More generally, for a split finite Chevalley group with $N$ positive roots, the Steinberg/maximal-unipotent size is $q^N$ and the total positive-root-subgroup coset count is $Nq^{N-1}$, the formal derivative of $q^N$.

At $q=4$, an exact propagation theorem shows that if every active chart is a minimum $1|4$ cut of $K_5$, then the only weight-$256$ words through a fixed apartment are its eight chamber stars; any exotic minimum word must contain a heavier $2|3$ chart.  Separately, the twisted-Hecke $\sqrt{17}$ block is integrally conjugate to the shifted historical transfer block by a unimodular $2\times2$ matrix, and the raw $V_{24}$ denominator-$780$ compiler basis is a strict one-row-exchange local minimum for numerical conditioning among denominator-$780$ bases.

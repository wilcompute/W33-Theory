\subsection*{The missing \(C_2\) is the spinor norm}

The explicit adjoint lift resolves the apparent parity mismatch between the
two-dimensional qutrit phase-space action and the three-dimensional hull
orthogonal action. For
\[
 q(v)=\frac12v^TQv,\qquad
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\1&0&1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
over \(\mathbf F_3\), enumerate all nine projective orthogonal reflections and
all \(24\) elements of \(SO(Q)\). Every \(SO(Q)\) element is a product of two
reflections, and all reflection decompositions give the same spinor
square-class.

For the explicit lift
\[
 \rho:GL(2,3)\longrightarrow SO(Q)
\]
from the preceding theorem, the executable certificate proves
\[
 \boxed{
 \theta_{\rm spin}(\rho(g))
 =
 \det(g)\pmod{(\mathbf F_3^\times)^2}
 }
\]
for all \(48\) elements \(g\in GL(2,3)\). Consequently
\[
 \rho(SL(2,3))=\Omega(Q)\cong A_4,\qquad
 \rho(GL(2,3))=SO(Q)\cong S_4.
\]

In particular, the anti-linear qutrit reflection
\[
 \kappa=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)
\]
has \(\det\kappa=-1\), while its three-dimensional lift has determinant
\(+1\) but nontrivial spinor norm. Explicitly,
\[
 \rho(\kappa)=r_{(1,1,0)}r_{(1,2,0)},\qquad
 q(1,1,0)=1,\quad q(1,2,0)=2.
\]
Thus the correct finite invariant carried by the factor-two extension is the
spinor norm, not the determinant of the three-dimensional orthogonal matrix.

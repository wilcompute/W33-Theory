\subsection{The splitting flag is the Sylow-$3$ normalizer/Borel (Pass 4519)}
\label{sec:pass4519-flag-borel}

Passes~4503--4504 corrected the symmetry-breaking threshold from a single
point or line to an incident point--line flag.  Pass~4519 identifies that
order-$162$ subgroup internally, not from its order alone.  Let $H$ be the
canonical flag stabilizer in the exact $25920$-element $PSp(4,3)$ action.  Its
unique Sylow-$3$ subgroup $P$ has
\[
 |P|=81,\qquad [H:P]=2.
\]
Two generators suffice for $P$.  Normalizing those generators in the full group
gives
\[
 \boxed{N_{PSp(4,3)}(P)=H,\qquad |N_{PSp(4,3)}(P)|=162.}
\]
Thus the verified splitting subgroup is precisely the chamber/Borel subgroup,
equivalently the Sylow-$3$ normalizer for this W33 building action.  Its element
orders are distributed as
\[
 1^1,\ 2^9,\ 3^{44},\ 6^{72},\ 9^{36},
\]
while the Sylow subgroup itself has $1^1,3^{44},9^{36}$.

Combining with Pass~4509, restriction to this Borel kills the full
$2$-dimensional radical $H^1$ obstruction: all three nonzero classes become
trivial after restriction.  The corrected structural statement is therefore
not merely ``break enough symmetry,'' but
\[
 \boxed{\text{pass to one chamber/Borel gauge}\Longrightarrow
        \text{both radical obstruction bits vanish and a section exists}.}
\]
This is a finite-group/building statement; it does not by itself identify a
physical gauge field or a hardware subgroup implementation.

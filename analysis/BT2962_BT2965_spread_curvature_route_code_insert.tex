\section{The OAM spread router carries the exceptional ten-point two-graph}
\label{sec:bt2962-oam-two-graph}

For a spread \(\mathcal S=\{L_0,\ldots,L_9\}\) of \(W(3,3)\), let
\(g_{ij}\in S_4\) denote the exact four-channel transport permutation from
\(L_i\) to \(L_j\).  Its sign defines an \(\mathbb F_2\)-valued edge
connection
\[
 a_{ij}=\operatorname{sgn}(g_{ij}),
\]
while the parity of the triangle holonomy is
\[
 \kappa_{ijk}
 =\operatorname{sgn}(g_{ki}g_{jk}g_{ij})
 =a_{ij}+a_{jk}+a_{ki}
 =(\delta a)_{ijk}.
\]

The Pass~2962 verifier enumerates all \(36\) spreads.  For every spread, the
\(120\) mode triangles split into \(60\) transposition and \(60\)
double-transposition holonomies.  The \(60\) odd triangles form a
\(2-(10,3,4)\) design: every mode lies on \(18\) odd triangles and every pair
lies on \(4\).  Moreover every four-mode tetrahedron contains an even number
of odd faces,
\[
 \delta\kappa=\delta^2a=0,
\]
which is the exact discrete Bianchi identity.  Exhausting all \(2^{10}\)
vertex-sign gauges leaves \(\kappa\) unchanged.

All \(36\) curvature hypergraphs are isomorphic.  Their switching class
contains the Petersen graph, and their full automorphism group is
\[
 \operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal T)
 \cong \mathrm{P}\Sigma\mathrm L(2,9)
 \cong S_6,
 \qquad |\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal T)|=720,
\]
with an explicit degree-ten model on the unordered \(3+3\) partitions of a
six-set.  Thus the spread router does not merely exhibit involutory triangle
holonomy: its parity curvature is the standard exceptional ten-point
two-graph.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
This is an exact theorem about finite routing permutations.  It is not a
measured optical Berry phase, a continuum Yang--Mills field, or a crosstalk
claim.  The full \(S_4\) holonomy retains information discarded by the sign
projection.

\section{Curvature-aware route checking gives a \([45,9,9]_2\) gauge code}
\label{sec:bt2965-curvature-route-code}

The ten spread modes form \(K_{10}\), with \(45\) inter-mode transport
edges.  Let \(e\in\mathbb F_2^{45}\) record parity flips of the exact \(S_4\)
routing permutations, and let \(H\in\mathbb F_2^{120\times45}\) be the
triangle-edge incidence matrix.  After subtracting the certified Pass~2962
baseline curvature, the observed syndrome is
\[
 s=He.
\]

Pass~2965 proves
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}H=36,
 \qquad
 \ker H=\operatorname{Cut}(K_{10}),
 \qquad
 \dim\ker H=9.
\]
Thus the undetectable edge patterns form the cut code
\[
 \boxed{[45,9,9]_2}
\]
with weight enumerator
\[
 1+10z^9+45z^{16}+120z^{21}+210z^{24}+126z^{25}.
\]
Every non-gauge fault of weight at most eight is detected, and every fault of
weight at most four is correctable modulo vertex switching.  The first
undetectable patterns are the weight-nine singleton cuts, which are exactly
gauge transformations.  Every single-edge fault has a distinct weight-eight
syndrome.  The \(120\) raw triangle checks compress to \(36\) independent
checks, and the tetrahedral boundary matrix \(B\) satisfies
\[
 BH=0,
\]
the matrix Bianchi identity.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The code detects parity faults of the finite routing permutations.  It does not
detect even-permutation faults, optical loss, continuous phase drift inside a
fixed parity class, or detector faults unless those are separately discretized
as parity flips.

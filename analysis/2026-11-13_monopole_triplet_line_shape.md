# Real-energy monopole triplet conversion

In the 80-cell finite-cut W33 radial model, the incoming singlet converts into the three light triplet channels with sampled probability 0.993930 at zero energy. It falls to 0.728677 at energy magnitude 0.00183333. The two complex poles by themselves contribute 0.137632 to the squared triplet amplitude at that dip. The cross term with the regular amplitude is −0.511217. This is the interference mechanism identified by Fano. The additive regular term can exceed one because it is a squared amplitude after algebraic pole subtraction. Only the full reflection matrix is unitary in that decomposition. [Ugo Fano, Effects of Configuration Interaction on Intensities and Phase Shifts](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.124.1866).

A multiplicative decomposition preserves unitarity. For each pole z, let P project onto the incident row space of its residue after removing earlier factors. The elementary factor is the identity on the orthogonal complement of P and multiplies its range by (E−conj(z))/(E−z). The code removes both factors on the right. Their incident projections have overlap tr(P₁P₂) = 0.701283. In this order they do not commute. Across the 51 sampled energies in [−0.004, 0.004], the determinant phase advances 13.475824 radians and the determinant with the two factors removed advances 2.273983 radians. [Curto, Hwang and Lee, Operator-valued rational functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11863).

Holding the stripped unitary matrix fixed at its zero-energy value while retaining both energy-dependent pole factors reproduces the total conversion on the sampled grid within 0.005643 absolute error. The stripped matrix itself need not be nearly constant in operator norm. The error bound here concerns the singlet-to-triplet observable. Unitarity also gives equal total conversion in the reverse direction when summing flux over the three triplet inputs.

# A chiral-index obstruction to the E6 interval Higgs spectrum

The [boundary proton-decay calculation](2026-11-17_E6_orbifold_proton_boundary.md) eliminates the minimal triplet and gauge exchange by a right-end hypercharge reflection. A `Z4^R` charge assignment could forbid the explicit chiral boundary contact if the two sourced spectators receive masses from unsourced partners. This leaves the spectrum as a separate test. For the fixed right reflection, every common inner left reflection preserving the repo's Standard Model embedding fails the following index test. The failure persists with arbitrarily many bulk `27`, `78`, `351` and `351'` hypermultiplets and their conjugates, and with brane matter from the endpoint parity blocks of `27` and its conjugate. SM-neutral singlets are unrestricted.

## Assumptions and the conserved index

Use one 5D supersymmetric E6 gauge multiplet on an interval. At the right endpoint its vector reflection is `P = exp(i pi w)`, where `w = -6 Y_SM`. At the left endpoint it is a common inner reflection `L` that fixes the SM gauge algebra. Each allowed bulk hypermultiplet has independently chosen intrinsic signs at the two endpoints. The SM charged background and mass terms preserve the residual `Z4^R` and the SM before electroweak breaking. Matter families with superfield charge 1 are separate from the charge-0 and charge-2 sectors. Fields with those latter charges have Weyl fermions of charge -1 and +1, respectively. The 5D gauge multiplet gives the gaugino charge +1 and the chiral adjoint partner charge -1.

For each SM representation, count charge-minus-one Weyl fermions and subtract charge-plus-one fermions in its conjugate representation. An allowed supersymmetric mass removes one of each. This also applies to an allowed gaugino–Higgsino pairing from Higgsing. Counts may be summed by hypercharge or projected onto individual SM representations without losing the index identity. In a bulk hypermultiplet the two 4D chiral partners have opposite parities at each end. A component with signed parities `(l,r)` contributes `(l+r)/2` oriented to the charge-0 representation. The gauge tower contributes the negative of this in the adjoint, with the gauge reflections and no intrinsic signs. Both statements include the mixed-parity modes, which contribute zero. The target has two Higgs components at each `w = +/-3` and the SM gauginos. The target places no constraint on gauge singlets.

This is an index test of the unbroken-symmetry interval field theory. It is independent of the sizes of masses and of the positions of SM-singlet vacuum profiles, provided the fields and allowed pairings obey the stated endpoint conditions and charge assignments. Charged brane representations beyond the specified parity blocks, outer twists, a different right reflection, and R-breaking masses require a different test.

## Exhausting the left reflections

The verifier obtains the 12 SM derivations from the exact E6 cubic with the repo's neutral VEV frame and hypercharge restrictions. Their supports partition the 27 into 11 connected blocks. A diagonal reflection is constant on each block to commute with the SM. Each of the 45 nonzero cubic monomials requires the product of its three signs to equal +1. There are eight solutions, with traces 27 once, 3 four times and -5 three times.

For a compact inner reflection the diagonal search is sufficient. The diagonal E6 torus normalizes the fixed SM algebra because it scales each coordinate VEV line and commutes with hypercharge. The SM roots are therefore ambient E6 roots, with their coroots in the SM torus. The centralizer of the SM torus in compact E6 is connected, and its derived subgroup is generated by roots vanishing on that torus. These roots are orthogonal to the SM roots. Since E6 is simply laced, their root subgroups commute with the SM. Conjugating an element of that centralizer into the diagonal torus can be done by its derived subgroup, so both the SM embedding and the right hypercharge reflection are retained. An inner order-two action in the adjoint can be represented by an order-two group element by multiplying by a center element, since the center has odd order. Relative orientation of the SM-singlet VEVs after conjugation does not enter the test. See [Hebecker and March-Russell on orbifold twists](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0107039).

## What the exact witnesses rule out

For each left reflection the certificate gives an integer functional annihilating every allowed bulk and brane index column. Its value differs between the gauged target spectrum and the bare projected gauge tower. One can allow rational combinations of the columns and the mismatch still persists. Allowing arbitrary signed copies of both parity blocks of the 27 and its conjugate on either boundary overestimates the brane index span.

- With only 27s and their duals, four reflections leave uncancellable index +6 at each of grades -5 and +5, where no 27 contains any component. The other four fail by linear combinations of grades.
- Adding 351 or 351' bulk hypermultiplets individually fails at the hypercharge level for all eight. The verifier computes their parity traces in the exterior square of 27 and in its symmetric square with the cubic's dual 27 removed.
- Adding the 78 in three cases can pass a **hypercharge-only** span test, even with all three larger representations available. Grade zero combines neutral singlets and gauge-charged states. Resolving the color octet and weak triplet at grade zero closes all three apparent routes, including when the 351 and 351' bulk hypermultiplets are both available. The calculation extracts their separate parity traces with the SU(3) and SU(2) Weyl denominators. The identity trace counts two copies of each of these SM multiplets in either 351-dimensional representation.

This strengthens the repo's earlier warning that an R symmetry can protect unwanted exotics in explicit orbifold searches. The [4D R-symmetry obstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.4797) does not cover extra-dimensional models. The obstruction here comes from the particular right reflection and shared boundary gauge action. [Heterotic local unification](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0512326) supplies a precedent for testing distinct localized representations. Any proposed replacement must additionally cancel local boundary anomalies, which can survive even for towers with no chiral zero mode. [Scrucca et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0110073)

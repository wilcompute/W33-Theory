# Higher valence connects graphs but does not select polynomial volume growth

Among all simple labeled \(d\)-regular graphs at fixed \(d\ge3\), a fixed-temperature Gibbs factor with an \(O(n)\) total log-weight spread cannot select a uniformly polynomial-growth geometry. This holds even after restricting the prior to connected graphs. Its probability is at most

\[
\exp\!\left[-(d/2-1)n\log n+O(n\log\log n)\right].
\]

For degree four the leading coefficient is \(-1\). This is a different obstruction from the degree-two loss of connectivity. Higher-valence random graphs are typically connected and can have a finite Laplacian gap as size grows. A periodic square lattice of the same degree has a vanishing gap and a polynomial volume-growth window. [Friedman's regular-graph spectral theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0405020) · [Quantum graphity](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.77.104029)

## Counting the geometric class

Take \(R=\lceil(\log n)^2\rceil\). Suppose every \(R\)-ball has at most \(B=C(R+1)^D\) vertices for fixed \(C,D\). This single mesoscopic condition suffices for the bound, so the result applies in particular to uniform polynomial volume growth. For a variant with a set of \(o(n)\) exceptional centers, remove all edges incident to the exceptional vertices and count them as singleton blocks. This uses at most \(d\,o(n)\) extra deletions. The coefficient of \(n\log n\) is then unchanged with an \(o(n\log n)\) remainder.

In any induced remainder, take a vertex and write \(b_r\) for the sizes of its balls of radii \(r=0,\ldots,R\). Inducing a subgraph cannot enlarge the ball. Since \(b_R/b_0\le B\), some ratio \(b_{r+1}/b_r\le B^{1/R}\). Extract this \(r\)-ball as one block. Every outgoing edge ends in the next shell, so at most \(d(B^{1/R}-1)b_r\) edges leave the block. Iterating partitions the vertices into blocks of size at most \(B\), cutting at most \(m=d(B^{1/R}-1)n=O(n\log\log n/\log^2n)\) edges. Those edges can be reconstructed by at most \(n^{2m+O(1)}\) choices.

For an ordered list of block sizes \(s_i\), there are \(n!/\prod_i s_i!\) labeled partitions. The number of internal graphs of maximum degree \(d\) on a block of size \(s\) is at most \((d+1)^s(ds)^{ds/2}\). Choose the degree list and then pair at most \(ds\) stubs. The number of ordered compositions of \(n\) is at most \(2^n\). In particular the log count of the entire geometric class is bounded by \(\log(n!)+O(n\log B)+O(m\log n)=n\log n+O(n\log\log n)\). For an unlabelled prior, one may instead omit the choice of labels for blocks, assign consecutive temporary labels to chosen block representatives, and retain the same upper bound on cross edges. Hence the unlabelled geometric count is at most \(\exp(O(n\log\log n))\).

## Compare with all regular graphs

The fixed-degree configuration count gives \(\log|\mathcal G_{n,d}|=(d/2)n\log n+O(n)\) along admissible \(n\). Restricting to connected graphs preserves this leading term since uniform fixed-degree regular graphs for \(d\ge3\) are connected with probability tending to one. Alternatively, the spectral gap statement for the uniform model gives the same fact by ruling out duplicate top adjacency eigenvalues. For unlabelled graphs, dividing the labeled count by \(n!\) gives the needed lower bound. In both priors the entropy deficit has leading coefficient \(d/2-1\). [Bollobás's fixed-degree enumeration](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/4056/) · [Uniform simple regular graph spectral bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04482)

Reweighting changes any event probability by at most the ratio of maximum to minimum weight. With a fixed finite inverse temperature and an \(O(n)\) Hamiltonian norm on graph-independent Hilbert spaces, the log ratio of quantum traces is \(O(n)\) by eigenvalue comparison. The same conclusion holds for classical energies with \(O(n)\) variation. Therefore bounded extensive local terms alone cannot give this geometric class an order-one probability under the stated regular-graph priors. A necessary condition for an order-one probability is log-weight contrast of at least \((d/2-1)n\log n-O(n\log\log n)\). Such contrast could come from a different prior, a size-dependent inverse temperature, or additional nonextensive interactions. This argument supplies neither a mechanism that does so nor a spacetime continuum. It also leaves open large-distance spectral scaling without the stated mesoscopic ball condition.

The leading rate is sharp along sizes admitting a connected polynomial-growth example with the stated ball constants. For example, in degree four use square tori. A connected graph of degree at most \(d\) has at most \(n(d!)^n\) automorphisms: choose the image of a rooted vertex, then the maps on incident neighbors while traversing the connected graph. Thus one such graph contributes at least \(n!/[n(d!)^n]\) labeled realizations. The upper bound on the full regular count now gives a matching lower bound with an \(O(n)\) error in the log probability. In the unlabelled measure, use one copy of the torus and the asymptotic unlabelled regular-graph count for a matching lower bound. The log-weight spread \(O(n)\) does not change the leading rate in either case. [Bollobás's unlabelled enumeration](https://doi.org/10.1112/JLMS/S2-26.2.201)

## Reproducible numerical controls

The executable compares periodic square lattices and fixed-seed random degree-four graphs at 256, 1024 and 4096 sites. It verifies torus ball sizes by an independent closed form and the torus gap by its Fourier eigenvalue. It measures the gaps and sampled ball counts of the random graphs and verifies the reconstruction count of the greedy cut on each graph. At 4096 sites the gaps are approximately 0.00963 and 0.54514, respectively. A radius-eight cut at small size can produce a single large block, so the raw number of cut edges is not meaningful without its maximum block size. The asymptotic probability proof uses the counting argument above, not these finite controls. Run `python analysis/toe_20261127_regular_graph_growth_entropy.py`.

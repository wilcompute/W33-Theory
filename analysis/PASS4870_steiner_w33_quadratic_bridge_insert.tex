\section{The Steiner-triangle three-cover of the \texorpdfstring{$W(3,3)$}{W(3,3)} line action and the first nonlinear adjoint bridge}
\label{sec:pass4870-steiner-w33-quadratic}

\paragraph{Prior-art and correction firewall.}
The bare partition of the $120$ Steiner trihedral pairs into forty triads is
classical cubic-surface geometry; it is not claimed here as a discovery of this
project.  In particular, Frame's finite-geometric treatment of the $27$ lines
already identifies forty triads of trihedral pairs.  Pass~4870 correctly
recovered that $40\times3$ partition as a particular $PSp(4,3)$ pair orbital
and correctly computed the four pair-orbit refinement and the quadratic Hom
space below.  It originally made one additional inference that is now
withdrawn: the forty-fiber quotient was called the standard symplectic
$W(3,3)$ point graph merely after an intended graph-isomorphism check.
Passes~4953--4955 rebuild both degree-$40$ actions independently and prove that
the quotient is instead the \emph{line-intersection graph} of $W(3,3)$, i.e.
the point graph of the nonisomorphic odd-$q$ dual generalized quadrangle
$Q(4,3)$.  The two graphs share $\operatorname{SRG}(40,12,2,4)$ parameters but
are not isomorphic.

Pass~4866 proved two facts that appeared, at first, to pull in opposite
directions: the $120$ Steiner/maximal triangles form the full
$H_2(-;\mathbb F_3)$ coordinate module of the double-six clique complex, but
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{PSp}(H_2,Q_{10})
  =\operatorname{Hom}_{PSp}(Q_{10},H_2)=0.
\]
The linear obstruction is exact.  Pass~4870 determines the first nonlinear
layer rather than bypassing that obstruction.

The action of $PSp(4,3)$ on unordered pairs of Steiner triangles has four
orbits, of sizes
\[
  120,\qquad1620,\qquad2160,\qquad3240,
\]
with per-triangle degrees $2,27,36,54$.  Their intrinsic incidence types are,
respectively,
\[
  (|T\cap T'|,e_\times)=(0,0),(1,6),(0,6),(0,4),
\]
where $e_\times$ is the number of double-six-graph edges joining the two
triangles.

The $120$-pair relation is $40K_3$, recovering internally the classical forty
triads.  The $2160$-pair relation is exactly the complete $K_{3,3}$ lift
between adjacent fibers.  Quotienting by the three-element fibers gives a
graph with
\[
  v=40,\qquad k=12,\qquad\lambda=2,\qquad\mu=4.
\]
Pass~4954 proves by explicit graph isomorphism that this quotient is the
intersection graph of the forty $W(3,3)$ lines, equivalently the $Q(4,3)$
point graph.  Its independent-triad common-neighbor census is
$0^{1080}2^{2160}$ and it has no spread, whereas the standard $W(3,3)$ point
graph has census $1^{2880}4^{360}$ and does have spreads.  The induced
projective symplectic action still has order $25920$: this is the second,
nonisomorphic degree-$40$ $PSp(4,3)$ action, not a second copy of the point
action.

Because $2$ is invertible in $\mathbb F_3$, equivariant homogeneous quadratic
maps $H_2\to Q_{10}$ are equivalent to equivariant symmetric bilinear maps, or
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{PSp}(\operatorname{Sym}^2 H_2,Q_{10}).
\]
For a permutation module, $\operatorname{Sym}^2 H_2$ splits into the diagonal
permutation module and the four unordered-pair orbit modules above.  The
stabilizer orders and $Q_{10}$ fixed-space dimensions are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
\text{orbit}&\text{diagonal}&120&1620&2160&3240\\\hline
|G_x|&216&216&16&12&8\\
\dim Q_{10}^{G_x}&0&0&0&2&0.
\end{array}
\]
Therefore
\[
  \boxed{\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{PSp}
  (\operatorname{Sym}^2H_2,Q_{10})=2.}
\]
Both dimensions are supported exclusively on the $2160$-pair relation that
lifts $Q(4,3)$ adjacency between the Steiner fibers.  Since $Q_{10}$ is
irreducible, every nonzero linear map
$\operatorname{Sym}^2H_2\to Q_{10}$ in this two-dimensional family is
surjective.

This gives a precise characteristic-three answer to the preceding obstruction:
the first equivariant Steiner-to-adjoint bridge is quadratic, not linear, and
its orbital mediator is the recovered $Q(4,3)$ line-action quotient.  Pass~4941
then removes the two-dimensional quadratic-channel ambiguity projectively by
taking the intrinsic Lie bracket of the two quadratic channels, producing an
outer-even quartic with full $Q_{10}$ image.

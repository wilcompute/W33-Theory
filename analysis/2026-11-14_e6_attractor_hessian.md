# Exact 42-scalar Hessian in the five-dimensional E6 Pauli chart

The [nearest-minimum result](2026-11-14_e6_attractor_gradient.md) used a two-dimensional plane to follow the scalar-potential gradient. At the balanced charge, the full scalar Hessian has 14 positive and 28 zero eigenvalues. The positive eigenvalues are all (4/3)|N|^(2/3) in the trace metric of the 27-dimensional charge representation. The number of flat directions agrees with the established five-dimensional maximal-supergravity attractor moduli. The exact matrix calculation here fixes the scale and identifies the charge variations in the repository's Pauli chart. [Ferrara, Marrani and Morales, five-dimensional attractor flat directions](https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.3488)

For a regular balanced charge q and a real symmetric Lie generator A, the potential along the geodesic is ||exp(tA)q|| squared. Its first derivative at zero is 2 q dot Aq. Its second derivative is 4||Aq|| squared. The first derivative vanishes on all 42 directions in the exact native generator basis. If M has columns A_j q and G is the trace Gram matrix of the generators, the Hessian on the scalar tangent is 4 G^(-1) M^T M. Its nonzero eigenvalues equal those of the smaller symmetric matrix 4 M G^(-1) M^T. At N=1 the verifier proves that three quarters of this smaller matrix is an orthogonal projector of trace 14. Compact conjugation, overall sign and homogeneous charge scaling give the result at any regular balanced charge.

The null space consists exactly of the symmetric generators with Aq=0. For each such generator exp(tA)q=q for every t. Hence all these zero modes are exact flat geodesic directions. In geodesic exponential coordinates at the balanced point they exhaust the minimum set: convexity along a geodesic from that point to another minimum makes the potential constant, so its initial tangent has zero Hessian. This gives the dimension and intrinsic tangent space of the minimum set directly in native coordinates.

The balanced Pauli operator is X=4 Pi-I, with Pi projecting onto a quaternionic line. The verifier checks that every operator variation from M is traceless and annihilated on both sides by Pi. Such operators are precisely the traceless quaternionic-Hermitian operators on the complementary three-plane, a real vector space of dimension 14. Thus at first order the lifted scalar directions alter eigenvalues and operators inside the complement, with no mixing into the distinguished line. The result is a Hessian of the scalar potential. Translating it into spacetime radial falloffs or fluctuation masses requires the radial kinetic and metric normalization.

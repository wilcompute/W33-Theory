## Pass 2866 (outside the programme) — the exact **diameter** of the instruction set is 19

Passes 2778 and 2789 proved the four-operation micro-ISA generates `ASp(4,3)`, order
`4,199,040`. *Generates* says every frame transformation is expressible. It says nothing
about **how long the program is** — and that is the number a compiler needs.

Breadth-first search over the whole group, forward generators only (a processor cannot
run an instruction backwards, so the directed diameter is the honest one):

```text
depth   ball size      new
   16     3917179     696532     93.29 %
   17     4172449     255270     99.37 %
   18     4198852      26403     99.9955 %
   19     4199040        188    100.0000 %

reached 4199040 of 4199040
DIRECTED DIAMETER = 19
mean program length = 14.175585
counting floor log_4(4199040) = 11.000814
```

> **Every frame transformation the machine can perform is at most 19 instructions away,
> and the average is 14.18.** Four opcodes give at most `4^L` programs of length `L`, so
> no four-operation ISA can have diameter below `⌈log₄ 4199040⌉ = 12`. This one achieves
> 19 — **1.73× the counting floor**, and a compiler that emits 19-instruction schedules is
> worst-case optimal to within that factor.

The last shell is tiny: only **188 elements** are at distance exactly 19, out of 4.2
million. The hard cases are rare, which is exactly what you want if you are budgeting a
pipeline.

**Method.** The obvious BFS over four million elements looks expensive and is not. A
group element is `(M, t)` with `M ∈ Sp(4,3)` and `t ∈ F₃⁴`; each generator acts as a fixed
**permutation** of the 51,840 matrix indices composed with a fixed map on the 81
translations. Build those tables once and the entire search is array indexing.

## Pass 2867 (outside the programme) — the frame scrambles in **15 instructions**

A random instruction stream drives a walk on the 81 frames. Every opcode is a bijection,
so the transition matrix is doubly stochastic and the stationary distribution is exactly
uniform — **no frame is preferred**.

```text
|lambda_2|                  = 0.893992320
spectral gap                = 0.106007680
relaxation time 1/gap       = 9.433279 instructions
MIXING TIME to TV <= 1/4    = 15 instructions
Kemeny constant             = 113.381835 instructions
```

> A frame's memory of where it started decays by `1/e` in **9.4 instructions** and is
> statistically gone after **15**. At the measured 208.86 MHz of the minimal engine
> (Pass 2833) that is **71.8 ns** — the architecture's intrinsic scrambling time.

Paired with Pass 2836's `8/3` bits per readout this gives an observation cadence: reading
more often than every ~15 instructions pays Landauer repeatedly for information the walk
has not yet regenerated.

**Distinct from the parallel track's walk, and worth saying so.** Their Passes 2814 and
2860 analyse the *unbiased geometric* walk on `W(3,q)` and its 15-state support quotient
(`K_support = 291/20`, `K_full = 801/20`, closed-form Green function). This is the walk
generated by the **actual instruction set** on the 81 affine frames. Different generator,
different state space, different number — the two are complementary, not competing.

## Pass 2861 — **no** two-copy stabilizer branch can be quadratic

Pass 2831 showed the known deep-grade branch converges *linearly* at rate `2/3`, which is
why the raw cost reaches `10²⁷`. The obvious hope is that another of the 48 is quadratic.
Rather than re-run a fidelity search over 21,420 branches, note that super-linearity has a
necessary and sufficient linear-algebra condition:

> the accepted projector must annihilate every **single-error** input while keeping the
> error-free one.

Two copies of `ρ_p = (1−3p/4)|m⟩⟨m| + (p/4)Σ|eᵢ⟩⟨eᵢ|` give, to first order, `|mm⟩` plus six
single-error terms. Testing every `[[4,2]]` code and every syndrome:

```text
[[4,2]] stabilizer codes (2-dim isotropic subspaces): 5355
ray 0 / 9 / 18 / 27: projectors keeping |mm> and killing all six singles: 0 / 0 / 0 / 0
```

> **Zero, across all 5,355 codes × 4 syndromes × 4 Clifford classes.**
>
> So the linear rate `2/3` is not a property of the branch that happened to be found — it
> is a **bound on the entire two-copy stabilizer family.** The `10²⁷` raw-state cost
> cannot be fixed by choosing differently among the 48.

### And the reason is not the obvious one

`|mm⟩` **is** orthogonal to all six single-error vectors (`⟨m eᵢ|mm⟩ = ⟨m|m⟩⟨eᵢ|m⟩ = 0`,
verified at `2.6 × 10⁻¹⁶`), and `16 − 6 = 10 ≥ 4`, so projectors with exactly this property
exist in abundance — the rank-one projector onto `|mm⟩` is one, checked explicitly.

> **What fails is specifically that no *stabilizer* projector aligns that way.** The `M₃₆`
> error basis is not a stabilizer basis and the syndrome projectors cannot be steered onto
> it. The obstruction is the mismatch between the magic-state error structure and the
> stabilizer formalism — the same tension that makes `M₃₆` interesting in the first place.

A dimension-count argument was drafted first and was wrong; it is recorded here because
the right answer with the wrong reason is a claim waiting to be misapplied.

**Super-linear distillation of `M₃₆` needs three or more copies, or operations from
outside the stabilizer set.**

## Pass 2862 — the deleted line is **not** canonical geometrically and **uniquely** canonical otherwise

`W(3,3)` has 40 lines; enumerating 4-cliques of the orthogonality graph confirms exactly
40, four through each point. Classified by how many stabilizer (axis) points they contain:

```text
 1 line  entirely stabilizer     <- the deleted line
12 lines one axis + three magic rays
27 lines entirely magic
```

> **Geometrically the line is not canonical.** `Aut(W(3,3)) = PGSp(4,3)` has order
> `51840 = 40 × 1296` and is line-transitive, so nothing intrinsic to the 40 points prefers
> one. Choosing a computational basis is choosing one line out of 40 — exactly
> `log₂ 40 = 5.3219` bits of pure convention.

*(The identification `Aut(W(3,3)) = PGSp(4,3) = U₄(2):2 = W(E₆)` of order `51840`, and its
distinctness from `Sp(4,3) = 2.U₄(2)`, is classical and is stated in `docs/index.html` and
in the abstract of `photonic_holonet_body.tex`. Only the line-count decomposition below is
this pass's. Flagged by `check_implicit_novelty.py` on its first run over this file, which
is what the guard is for.)*
>
> **Relative to the stabilizer structure it is unique and rigid.** Exactly *one* of the 40
> lines has all four points classical.

So *which states are magic* is not a fact about the geometry. It is a fact about which line
you declared to be the basis — and once declared, that line is the unique all-classical
one, so the declaration is recoverable from the structure it creates.

## Pass 2864 — the energy budget, floor and reality

```text
Landauer floor per support readout (8/3 bits) : 7.656e-21 J = 47.785 meV at 300 K
Landauer floor for COMPUTE                    : exactly zero
```

The execution path erases nothing: every opcode is a group element, hence a bijection on
the 81 frames — Pass 2867 verified the transition matrix is doubly stochastic. **All of
the machine's irreducible dissipation is in readout.**

Against that floor, a CMOS estimate for the 43-cell engine at 1.622 transitions/op,
5 fF/cell, 1.2 V:

```text
E_op ~ 5.02e-13 J = 0.502 pJ    ->  0.105 mW dynamic at 208.86 MHz
E_op(CMOS) / E_Landauer(readout) = 6.56e7,  i.e. about 10^7.8 above the floor
```

Stated so the Landauer number is not mistaken for an engineering prediction. **It is a
floor, not a forecast.**

## Pass 2865 — what one network hop costs

```text
information needed per hop : log2(40) = 5.321928 bits
the routing code spends    : 8 bits    (BT827, 8 = 3+5)
coding efficiency          : 66.524 %

Landauer floor  : 95.365 meV/hop      as coded : 143.354 meV/hop
```

Depth `n` costs `n` hops and `N = 40ⁿ`, so **the thermodynamic cost of delivering a packet
across `N` leaves is exactly logarithmic in `N`** — 143.4 meV per level, 860 meV at depth
6 (4.1 billion leaves).

Two caveats, both real. A **reversible** router pays none of this; the floor applies only
because the header is destroyed as it is consumed, and that is a design choice. And the
`2.68` wasted bits per hop are the price of Kraft equality, which is what makes every bit
string a legal address:

> **~33 % of the routing energy buys the absence of an entire failure mode.** That trade
> was always implicit in the design; it now has a number.

## Pass 2869 — correction: `SRG(40,12,2,4)` does **not** identify `W(3,3)`

Pass 2835 computed the orthogonality graph of the 40 rays, found `SRG(40,12,2,4)`, and
concluded it *is* the `W(3,3)` collinearity graph. **That inference was one step short**,
and `docs/index.html`'s Theory section says so in plain words:

> *"The parameter tuple does not identify `W(3,3)`: Spence classified exactly **28
> nonisomorphic** `SRG(40,12,2,4)` graphs. The object here is fixed by the displayed
> symplectic construction."*

The blueprint repeated the error in stronger language still — *"there is essentially one
object in the world with that compatibility pattern"* — which is simply false.

### The identification, completed

```text
SRG parameters (40,12,2,4)                          -> 28 candidates
every edge in exactly one line (240 edges, 40 lines,
  4 per point) and the GQ axiom over ALL 40 lines   -> 2 candidates: W(3,3), Q(4,3)
a spread: 10 pairwise disjoint lines covering all 40 -> W(3,3)
```

`W(3,q)` has a spread for every `q`; `Q(4,q)` has one only for **even** `q`. One is
exhibited explicitly:

```text
[0,5,7,36] [1,10,19,27] [2,12,21,31] [3,11,26,33] [4,17,23,32]
[6,15,18,29] [8,13,25,34] [9,14,16,37] [20,22,24,38] [28,30,35,39]
```

> **The 40 rays are the points of `W(3,3)`** — now on the identification it needed rather
> than on a parameter coincidence. The Pass 2835 headline survives intact.

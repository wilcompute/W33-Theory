## 1. The binary rank formulas hold for every odd prime power

Let `M_q` be the point-line incidence matrix of the symplectic generalized quadrangle `W(3,q)`, reduced modulo two. Put

\[
A_P=M_qM_q^T,\qquad A_L=M_q^TM_q,
\qquad D_q=\begin{pmatrix}0&M_q\\M_q^T&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For every odd prime power `q`,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_2M_q=\frac{q(q+1)^2+2}{2}},
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_2A_P=\frac{q(q^2+1)}2+1},
\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_2A_L=q^2+1}.
\]

This is now an algebraic proof, not a fit to `q=3,5,7,9`.

### Central-translation decomposition

Choose a point at infinity and the standard Heisenberg chart. Its central translation group

\[
C\cong(\mathbb F_q,+)
\]

fixes the perpendicular hyperplane and translates the central coordinate on the `q^3` affine non-neighbours. Extend scalars from `F_2` to a splitting field `K` for `C`. Since `q` is odd, `|C|` is invertible in characteristic two, so Maschke decomposition separates the matrices into one trivial additive-character block and `q-1` nontrivial blocks without changing rank.

### Nontrivial character blocks

A nontrivial block is naturally `Mat_q(K)`. If `T` is the finite Fourier matrix of the selected additive character, conjugating the point operator by `T` sends the symplectic Fourier transform to transpose. Therefore

\[
A_{P,\chi}:Y\longmapsto Y+Y^T.
\]

In characteristic two its image is the space of symmetric matrices with zero diagonal, so

\[
\operatorname{rank}A_{P,\chi}=\frac{q(q-1)}2.
\]

The Fourier-transformed line columns are symmetric. The horizontal family supplies the diagonal units. For the remaining families, orthogonality against every chirp column and Fourier inversion force every off-diagonal coefficient to vanish; hence the line columns span all of `Sym_q(K)`. Thus

\[
\operatorname{rank}M_\chi=\dim\operatorname{Sym}_q=\frac{q(q+1)}2.
\]

The Gram pairing on symmetric matrices is

\[
\langle X,Y\rangle=\sum_{i,j}X_{ij}Y_{ij}
=\sum_iX_{ii}Y_{ii},
\]

because each off-diagonal contribution occurs twice. Its rank is therefore `q`, giving

\[
\operatorname{rank}A_{L,\chi}=q.
\]

### Trivial character block

The fixed block is controlled by the affine plane `AG(2,q)`. Let `N` be its line-point incidence matrix, `P` the adjacency within parallel classes, and `E` the direction repeater. Over `F_2`, with `q` odd,

\[
N^TN=J+I,\qquad NN^T+P=J,
\qquad E^TE=I,\qquad EE^T=I+P.
\]

Direct elimination gives point rank `q^2+1`. The incidence block has the form

\[
M_0(z,w)=\bigl(\mathbf1^Tz,\ Ez+w,\ N^Tw\bigr),
\]

whose kernel is exactly `w=Ez`, `sum(z)=0`, of dimension `q`; therefore `rank M_0=q^2+q+1`. The line Gram block has rank `q+1`.

Adding the trivial block and the `q-1` nontrivial blocks yields the three boxed formulas. Together with

\[
D_q^3=\begin{pmatrix}0&J\\J&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad D_q^4=0,
\]

the full Jordan form is

\[
\boxed{D_q\sim J_4^{\,2}\oplus J_3^{(q^3+2q^2+q-4)/2}\oplus J_1^{q(q-1)^2/2}}
\]

with no `J_2` blocks.

## The space

**Definition.** The Heisenberg-invariant symmetric cubic tensors on n qutrits are spanned by:
* the diagonal Σ_a e_a^{⊗3};
* for each direction ±d ∈ F₃ⁿ, the symmetrised sum over the lines {a, a + d, a − d}.

**Dimension.** (3ⁿ + 1)/2: 2 at n = 1 (Pass 11641's Hesse doublet) and 5 at n = 2.

**The map.** u(ψ) = Bᵀψ^{⊗3} is a Clifford-covariant cubic map.

**1. The dual even Weil representation.** The Clifford group acts on the Hesse space, projectively, by the complex
conjugate of the parity-even half of the Weil representation of Sp(2n, 3).

**2. One qutrit: G₆.** The Hesse doublet's group is G₆. The Fourier image R(F) is itself an order-2 reflection, which
adds to G₄ = SL(2,3).
* Its invariants of degree 4 and 12 are Klein's vertex form and the degree-12 form.
* The j-invariant of Pass 11645 is their ratio.

**3. Two qutrits: G₃₃.** The image is the 5-dimensional Shephard–Todd group G₃₃, of order 51 840 = |W(E₆)| = |Sp(4,3)|/2,
times scalars. G₃₃ is the symmetry group of the Burkhardt quartic.

**The classical picture.**
* **n = 1:** the Hesse pencil, i.e. elliptic curves with level-3 structure.
* **n = 2:** the Heisenberg-invariant (Coble) cubics in P⁸, i.e. abelian surfaces with level-3 structure.
* **The covariant map.** A two-qutrit state ψ ∈ P⁸ meets these cubics in a hyperplane of the P⁴ of cubics, and u(ψ) is
  that hyperplane's dual point.

**4. The 40 two-qutrit stabiliser bases** (Lagrangian planes of F₃⁴) each map to **one ray**, since Heisenberg acts
trivially. Their overlaps are:

| relation of the Lagrangian planes | \|⟨u_a, u_b⟩\|² | graph |
|---|---|---|
| meet in a line | **1/3** | **SRG(40, 12, 2, 4)**, the generalized quadrangle of order 3 dual to W(3,3) |
| meet in 0 | 1/9 | its complement, SRG(40, 27, 18, 18) |

**Which SRG(40, 12, 2, 4).** Two non-isomorphic graphs have these parameters for q = 3:
* the collinearity graph of W(3,3)'s points, the repository's W33 graph, in which every point is **regular**:
  |{x,y}^⊥⊥| = 4;
* the collinearity graph of its dual Q(4,3), whose points are W(3,3)'s **lines**, in which every point is
  **antiregular**: |{x,y}^⊥⊥| = 2.

The 40 stabiliser rays give **(4, 2) for every non-adjacent pair, so they realise Q(4,3), the line graph side of W(3,3),
not the W33 point graph.** That is natural: a stabiliser basis is a Lagrangian plane, i.e. a line of W(3,3). For n = 1, the 4 MUB points with mutual overlap 1/3 are the tetrahedron of Pass 11641.

**5. The norm is the stabiliser third moment.**
* |u|² is affine in M₃, exactly.
* This is the Gross–Nezami–Walter structure of the third tensor power of the Clifford group (arXiv:1712.08628). For odd
  d the Clifford group is not a unitary 3-design; the defect is exactly this projector.
* **Pass 11641's "ρ = M₃ − M₁³" is its n = 1 case** and should be cited as such (addendum in 11641).

**6. Pass 11641's linear law does not extend.**
* **At n = 1** the Bloch data of u are linear in the four MUB triple products.
* **At n = 2** the Hermitian form uu† is not in the span of the 480 stabiliser line triple products: the residual is
  9.4·10⁻³ against a typical entry of 3·10⁻³, at full feature rank 480.
* **The reason.** The line cubics sum over three parallel lines, and their interference terms carry phases. The
  one-qutrit Vandermonde theorem is special to one line per basis.

## Prior art and scope

**Classical.**
* The Hesse pencil (Hesse; Artebani–Dolgachev).
* The Burkhardt quartic (1891) and its group G₃₃ (Todd 1936).
* The Coble cubics (Coble 1917; Beauville 2003).
* The action of Sp(2n, 3) on level-3 theta functions through the Weil representation.
* The Clifford third-moment structure (Gross–Nezami–Walter 2017).

**New here.**
* The qutrit reading: Hesse space = Heisenberg-invariant cubic shadow of an n-qutrit state.
* The explicit identification of the representation as the dual even Weil representation, with checks.
* G₆ for Codex's doublet.
* The 40 stabiliser-basis points forming the GQ(3,3) graph.
* The failure of the triple-product law at n = 2.

**In the repo.**
* `analysis/w33_BREAKTHROUGH_72_sylow_omega_burkhardt_delpezzo_leech.py` (paper supplement aleph) already relates the
  Burkhardt quartic (|Aut| = 25 920, its 40 j-planes) to the points of GQ(3,3) = W(3,3), at the level of counts.
* Pass 11651 supplies the explicit qutrit map behind such a statement: the overlap graph is checked, not counted.
* **Reading the "40" in that file.** Its "40 nodes" is most plausibly shorthand for the 40 points of W(3,3) and their
  collinearity graph SRG(40, 12, 2, 4). The Burkhardt quartic itself has **45** nodes, matching the 45 reflections of G₃₃
  found here, and 40 j-planes.
* **What the explicit map adds.** The 40 objects that actually appear here are the 40 stabiliser bases. Their overlap
  graph is the Q(4,3) form of SRG(40, 12, 2, 4), dual to the W(3,3) point graph. Any "j-planes ~ points of W(3,3)"
  identification therefore needs this duality made explicit.

**Not done here.**
* The two-qutrit analogue of the CP sign (Im of G₃₃ invariant ratios).
* An identification of the 40 points with the Burkhardt "j-planes". This is plausible but not checked.

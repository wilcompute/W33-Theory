\paragraph{Pass 4544: the $364$-dimensional middle has three $358$-submodules, not one.}
Let $M$ denote the $364$-dimensional middle quotient from Pass~4522.  Exact
cyclic-span computation on the standard basis gives
\[
  284^2,\qquad330^{10},\qquad358^{49},\qquad364^{303}.
\]
There is a unique cyclic $S_{284}$ and a unique cyclic $S_{330}$, but the
$49$ basis vectors of cyclic dimension $358$ generate exactly three distinct
submodules $T_0,T_1,T_2$.  All three contain $S_{330}$, every pair sums to all
of $M$, and every pair has the same intersection
\[
  \boxed{K_{352}=T_0\cap T_1=T_0\cap T_2=T_1\cap T_2.}
\]
The quotient $M/K_{352}$ has dimension twelve.  Exhausting all $4095$ nonzero
vectors shows that it has exactly three proper nonzero invariant submodules;
each has dimension six, every nonzero vector in each cyclically generates the
whole six-space, and
\[
  \boxed{M/K_{352}\simeq U_6\oplus U_6.}
\]
The three six-submodules are the three one-dimensional directions in the
multiplicity space $\mathbb F_2^2$.  The projective outer involution fixes one
$T_i$ and exchanges the other two.

The next layer also splits exactly:
\[
  \boxed{K_{352}/S_{330}\simeq V_8\oplus V_{14},}

\[
 \dim(S_{284}\cap S_{330})=256,\qquad
 \dim(S_{284}\cap K_{352})=278,
\]

Let $A_{27}$ be meet adjacency on the $27$ cubic lines and $A_{36}$ the

\[
 \boxed{RR^T=10I+2A_{27}+6J,}
 \qquad

\[
 \boxed{3A_{27}R+RA_{36}=20J.}

classical-coordinate transport rather than a parameter match.

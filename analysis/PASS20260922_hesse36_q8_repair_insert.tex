\subsection*{The two $36=12\times3$ Hesse carriers differ only at order three, and $Q_8$ repairs them}

The ordinary Payne/Hesse $36$ and the maximal compiler-safe $36$ are both
invariant under the exact common physical split complement
$SL(2,3)$ of order $24$, but they are not the same $SL(2,3)$-set.  Their orbit
splits are
\[
36_{\rm ordinary}=24+4+4+4,
\qquad
36_{\rm safe}=8+8+8+4+4+4.
\]
More sharply, their permutation characters agree on every element-order
stratum except order three:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
\operatorname{ord}(g)&1&2&3&4&6\\ \hline
|C|&1&1&8&6&8\\
\chi_{\rm ordinary}(g)&36&12&3&0&3\\
\chi_{\rm safe}(g)&36&12&9&0&3
\end{array}
\]
so the obstruction is concentrated exactly on the qutrit order-three
shear/transvection class.

Restricting to the normal quaternion subgroup
\[
Q_8\triangleleft SL(2,3)
\]
removes that class.  Both $36$-sets then decompose as
\[
\boxed{8+8+8+4+4+4.}
\]
The $8$-orbits are regular $Q_8$ torsors and the $4$-orbits are copies of
$Q_8/Z(Q_8)$.  Hence an objectwise $Q_8$-equivariant bijection exists.
One deterministic frozen-gauge bijection is constructed and checked on all
$8\cdot36=288$ action pairs.

The repair is not canonical:
\[
\boxed{
(3!\,8^3)(3!\,4^3)=1{,}179{,}648
}
\]
distinct $Q_8$-equivariant bijections exist.

\paragraph{Compiler consequence.}
The address/operator bridge already has an exact quaternionic objectwise core.
The remaining obstruction is purely the ternary order-three extension.  A
future compiler should therefore complete a $Q_8$-equivariant seed by an
order-three phase/shear rule rather than remap the other symmetry sectors.
No physical selector among the $1{,}179{,}648$ gauges is claimed here.

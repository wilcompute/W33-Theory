# Compact winding zero modes of the W33 point-core boundary model

The compact point-core model has six fermion and six conjugate zero-mode variables per unit rotor winding. A dressed two-point insertion cannot absorb them. This result holds with a root electric holonomy inserted before the charge projection. The winding selection leaves a definite four-fermion operator to match in the smooth core. ## Boundary operator and its kernel

Use the seven charge sectors of the sea-flow calculation and freeze the reflection at a constant unitary block R in each sector. In the charge-oriented frames the loop on the Euclidean time circle is G_ij(t) = R_ij exp[iχ(t)(n_in,j − n_out,i)/2]. Each W33 sector has a single outgoing pairing n_out. Consequently a constant unitary change of outgoing frame reduces G to separate scalar symbols exp(iδ_jχ), with δ_j = (n_in,j − n_out)/2. There are six steps +1, six steps −1, and four spectators. The determinant of the full loop has zero winding. The individual blocks still have indices of both signs.

The chiral-sea boundary operator in this regulator is T(G) = P_+ G on the positive-frequency Hardy space. It is the positive-frequency block of the fermionic sea transition. Equivalently, the two Hardy spaces describe the decaying boundary Fourier modes on opposite sides of the gluing seam. The free Gaussian fermion sources couple separately to its right and left defects. The sign convention is that T(z) is the unilateral forward shift. [Block Toeplitz index](https://jot.theta.ro/jot/archive/2016-076-001/2016-076-001-010.pdf)

For a smooth phase χ = kt + h(t), with h periodic and z = exp(it), split h into a negative-frequency part h₋ and a nonnegative-frequency part h₊. The scalar Toeplitz operator factors exactly as

    T(exp(iδχ)) = T(exp(iδh₋)) T(z^(kδ)) T(exp(iδh₊)).

The outer factors are invertible because their symbols and inverses are coanalytic and analytic, respectively. For kδ < 0 the kernel has dimension −kδ and is spanned by exp(−iδh₊)z^j for 0 ≤ j < −kδ. For kδ > 0 the adjoint gives kδ cokernel modes. Therefore the full kernel and cokernel have dimensions 6|k| each, for every smooth winding path in this frozen boundary model. The result is exact. Fourier projection on deformed-loop witnesses gives a maximum numerical residual below 8×10⁻¹⁶.

## Electric projection and insertion count

Write the inserted electric holonomy as 2πa with −1/2 ≤ a ≤ 1/2. Use Neveu–Schwarz (antiperiodic) time frequencies and the lift generated by m/2. The paired seam conditions have rotor phase advance 2π(k+a) and incoming frequencies r + 1/2 − n_in a/2. Multiplication by a ramp in G sends the mode to

    r + kδ + 1/2 − n_out a/2.

The holonomy shifts either half-integer frequency by at most 1/4. In these frames it leaves the two positive-frequency cutoffs at the same integer labels, and removing the quasiperiodic twist leaves the preceding integer loop. This uses the root electric character insertion and its compatible fermion twist. It applies before Fourier integration over a and at arbitrary finite circumference and rotor inertia. As a control, choosing periodic time frequencies while keeping the same seam symbol changes the lower integer mode labels to (1 + n sign(a))/2 for a nonzero. The effective winding is then k + sign(a). This is a different temporal boundary condition, and it shifts which winding has no defect. A change of gauge frame must transport the fermionic boundary condition and its connection together.

For the Gaussian chiral-sea amplitude, each unsaturated right or left zero-mode Grassmann variable sets the corresponding integral to zero. The vacuum and two-point numerators therefore receive only k = 0 at every such electric holonomy. Multiplying either external field by a rotor phase adds no fermion variable. This projects out nonzero winding in these correlators of the stated regulator. The k = 0 correlator can still depend on the holonomy. Evaluating its Fourier projection and taking the large-circumference limit are needed to locate the compact fixed-charge pole. The argument also leaves finite core thickness, massive channels and additional spin structures open. ## A four-fermion operator for unit winding

At positive unit winding the four active sectors each supply three zero-mode variables, with signs (+,−,−,+) in the order of the sea-flow ledger. Their transverse weights have rank three and this is the primitive neutral relation. A quartic using one leg from each sector can saturate a third of the winding defect. Three quartic insertions are necessary for a full turn in the vacuum or two-point amplitude if quartics provide the missing variables. The conjugate relation applies at negative winding.

Taking kernel fields from the incoming side and conjugate cokernel fields from the outgoing side gives the following left-Weyl components. The labels of the outgoing fields refer to their original Weyl weights.

| Leg | Four-Cartan weight | Formal (3B,L) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E^c | (0,0,0,6) | (0,−1) |
| L or H_d | (0,0,−1,−3) | (0,1) or (0,0) |
| U^c | (−1,1,0,−4) | (−1,0) |
| Q | (1,−1,1,1) | (1,0) |

Their full Cartan weight is zero. The lepton component preserves both formal B and L. A Lorentz and Standard Model gauge singlet containing those components is (Q U^c)(L E^c), contracting the weak indices between Q and L, the color between Q and U^c, and the two Weyl spin indices in each parenthesis. Its two spin contractions pair opposite magnetic signs. This is the left-Weyl form of the conjugate scalar semileptonic Warsaw operator. The existence of a contraction gives a target for native matching. Its smooth-core coefficient and its projection onto all winding zero modes remain to be calculated. The integral lift determinants are −27 and −729, so flavor rank alone permits saturation. This test assumes a nonzero coefficient of the factorized local operator. For two holomorphic Yukawa vertices, the coefficient needs a holomorphic Higgs scalar propagator connecting the two bilinears.

# Conjugation-odd radial fluctuations of the W33 monopole

This calculation tests imaginary variations of the 37 scalar profiles and of the complex magnetic profile about the real color-invariant radial branch. The radial connection stays in radial gauge, and regularity fixes the imaginary magnetic and triplet amplitudes at the origin. The calculation uses unit kinetic normalizations and the canonical charged-Yukawa action with the optional neutrino deformation omitted. [Real radial Hessian](2026-11-11_monopole_radial_hessian.md)

## Phase Hessian and vacuum gauge directions

For a holomorphic F residual and an imaginary variation iδ, the contribution to the static energy coefficient is |Jδ|² minus the contraction of the real background F residual with its holomorphic curvature. The corresponding real variation has the opposite curvature sign. The D term is computed separately from the Hermitian moment maps, including the opposite representation on the dual fields. The replay checks the resulting phase form against direct differences of the complex-field energy.

There are five imaginary compact singlet generators in the internal algebra and five in the family algebra. Their vacuum action has rank eight. The vacuum F and D Jacobian annihilates this entire eight-dimensional span, and the numerical kernel has precisely that dimension. These vacuum null directions are generated by gauge transformations. The positive vacuum singular values supply the scalar masses in the linear exterior matching condition. [Symmetric monopole ansatz and gauge conventions](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.16.1221)

## Magnetic phase and numerical scope

The imaginary magnetic profile decouples from the imaginary scalars at quadratic order because its angular couplings depend on the squared moduli. In the normalized radial gauge variable its potential is (K²−1)/r²+25h². An exact real solution satisfies K'' = [(K²−1)/r²+25h²]K. If K is nodeless in the interior, the magnetic phase form on compactly supported regular variations η can therefore be written as the integral of K²[(η/K)']². The identity uses integration by parts, with the regular origin condition and the exterior endpoint removing the boundary term.

The finite-element replay also includes the imaginary magnetic profile directly. It brackets the lowest generalized eigenvalue at two matching radii and two differentiation steps. The exterior is linearized, and the positive normalization used for the static form has yet to be reduced by the time-dependent Gauss equation. This calculation addresses radial phases in the color-invariant sector. A complete stability test still requires the nonlinear exterior and nonspherical perturbations.

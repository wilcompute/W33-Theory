\subsection{The quartic determinant needs a geometry bit: six projective-basis orbits and a minimal two-trit classifier}

The determinant/matroid bridge gives a quartic invariant of a finite set of Pauli
projective directions, but an exact census shows a sharp limitation at support size
four.  Let $B=\{v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3\}\subset\mathrm{PG}(3,3)$ be a projective basis.
Exhausting all $\binom{40}{4}=91390$ four-point subsets gives $63180$ bases and
$28210$ dependent sets.  The inner group $\PSp(4,3)$ has exactly six orbits on the
bases.  More strongly, the induced $W(3,3)$ orthogonality graph on the four points
classifies the orbit completely:
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r}
\text{orthogonality graph}&\text{orbit size}&|\operatorname{Stab}_{\PSp}(B)|\\\hline
C_4&1620&16\\
2K_2&3240&8\\
4K_1&6480&4\\
K_2+2K_1&12960&2\\
P_4&12960&2\\
P_3+K_1&25920&1
\end{array}
\]
The $C_4$ orbit is exactly the generalized-quadrangle apartment shell; its size
$1620$ agrees with the point shadows of the $1620$ minimum-weight Levi $8$-cycle
apartments already certified in the apartment-code arc.  Thus apartments comprise
exactly $1/39$ of all projective bases.

There is a compact projective classifier.  Write
\[
 z_{ij}=1-\langle v_i,v_j\rangle^2\in\mathbb F_3.
\]
Because every nonzero scalar of $\mathbb F_3$ has square one, $z_{ij}$ is precisely
the orthogonality indicator.  Define
\[
 E=\sum_{i<j}z_{ij},\qquad
 W=\sum_i\sum_{\substack{j<k\\j,k\ne i}}z_{ij}z_{ik}\pmod3.
\]
The six orbit values are respectively
\[
 C_4:(1,1),\quad P_4:(0,2),\quad P_3{+}K_1:(2,1),\quad
 2K_2:(2,0),\quad K_2{+}2K_1:(1,0),\quad 4K_1:(0,0).
\]
Hence $(E,W)$ is a complete two-trit orbit classifier.  It is minimal in the
literal information sense: one trit has only three values and cannot distinguish six
orbits, while the displayed pair distinguishes all six.

The same computation also supplies an important no-go.  If $V$ is the $4\times4$
matrix of any projective basis, then the determinant/matroid quartic is
\[
 \det(VV^T J)=\det(V)^2\det J=1\in\mathbb F_3.
\]
Thus the four-direction determinant phase takes the same value on every one of the six
basis orbits and cannot by itself select the apartment geometry.  On an ordered apartment
$a-b-c-d-a$, however, the symplectic Gram matrix has only the two diagonal pairings
nonzero and
\[
 \operatorname{Pf}(G)=-\langle a,c\rangle\langle b,d\rangle,
 \qquad \operatorname{Pf}(G)^2=1.
\]
This makes the apartment determinant-one statement incidence-theoretic while also showing
why a second observable is required to distinguish the apartment from other bases.

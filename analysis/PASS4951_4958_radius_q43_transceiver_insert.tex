\subsection{Pass 4951: a third-moment theorem gives $\rho(K)\le173$}
Let $K=[360,36,20]_2$.  Pass~4867 froze
\[
 A_1(K^\perp)=A_2(K^\perp)=0,\qquad A_3(K^\perp)=1080,
\]
and Pass~4940 exhibited a word at exact distance $134$ from $K$.  For any
coset, let $W$ be the Hamming weight of a uniformly sampled word and set
$X=W-180$.  Pairwise independence gives
\[
 \mathbb E X=0,\qquad \mathbb E X^2=90.
\]
The $1080$ dual weight-three words give the exact character identity
\[
 \mu_3:=\mathbb E X^3
 =-\frac34\sum_{\substack{h\in K^\perp\\|h|=3}}(-1)^{y\cdot h},
 \qquad |\mu_3|\le810.
\]
If a coset leader has weight $\delta$ and $a=180-\delta$, then
$X\ge-a$.  Positivity of
$\mathbb E[(X+a)(X-t)^2]$, optimized at $t=90/a$, yields
\[
 \mu_3\ge90\left(\frac{90}{a}-a\right).
\]
For $\delta\ge175$ the right side is at least $1170$, impossible.  At
$\delta=174$, equality in the moment bound forces every coset word to have
weight $174$ or $195$; the zero mean then forces population ratio $5:2$.
But $|K|=2^{36}$ is not divisible by $7$.  Hence
\[
 \boxed{134\le\rho(K)\le173.}
\]
The lower endpoint is a certified hard word; the upper endpoint is universal.
The exact radius remains open.

# Pass 459 — real-cyclotomic covariance for odd prime powers

Let \(q=p^f\) be odd and

\[
\psi_t(x)=\zeta_p^{\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbf F_q/\mathbf F_p}(tx)}.
\]

For an inverse-closed Heisenberg section \(c\), let \(B_t(c)\) be its central Weyl block. Then

\[
\sigma_a(B_t(c))=B_{at}(c),
\qquad a\in\mathbf F_p^*,
\]

where \(\sigma_a(\zeta_p)=\zeta_p^a\). Inverse closure makes every block Hermitian and identifies the characteristic polynomials for \(t\) and \(-t\). Therefore every coefficient field is contained in

\[
\mathbf Q(\zeta_p)^+,
\]

the maximal real \(p\)-th cyclotomic field of degree \((p-1)/2\).

The extension degree \(f\) changes the number of scalar lines of central characters,

\[
\frac{p^f-1}{p-1},
\]

but not the cyclotomic coefficient degree.

## Extension-field witnesses

- \(q=9\): four \(\mathbf F_3^*\)-lines, real orbit degree one, and all normalized trace-cube values are integers.
- \(q=25\): six \(\mathbf F_5^*\)-lines. Every one of the six conjugate trace-cube quadratics has squarefree discriminant kernel 5.

This is the precise prime-power correction to the prime-only wording of Pass 453.

## Relation to the supplied fifth-root paper

The paper's fifth-root amplitudes and Fibonacci fusion data naturally live in the same real fifth-cyclotomic field. That was a useful conceptual clue. The theorem here, however, is forced by additive-character Galois covariance and remains valid independently of the spin-foam model.

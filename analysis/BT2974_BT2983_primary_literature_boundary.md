## Binary pure-state discrimination

Acín, Bagan, Baig, Masanes, and Muñoz-Tapia, “Multiple-copy two-state discrimination with individual measurements,” *Physical Review A* **71**, 032338 (2005), DOI `10.1103/PhysRevA.71.032338`, proves that adaptive individual von Neumann measurements with Bayesian updating attain the collective binary pure-state discrimination bound for every finite copy number.

**Not new here:** adaptive attainability or the general Helstrom formula.

**Repository-specific here:** the exact overlap `1/3` of the conjugate M36 pairs, the correction separating the local `{YI,IY}` receiver from Helstrom, the pair-conditioned controller, and the explicitly synthetic readout profiles.

**Not new here:** the abstract Petersen graph, switching, or two-graph formalism.

## Sturmian balance and complexity

The characterization of Sturmian words by balance and factor complexity `p(n)=n+1` is classical combinatorics on words. A modern balance reference is Richomme, Saari, and Zamboni, “Generalized balances in Sturmian words,” *Discrete Applied Mathematics* **121** (2002), 83–101, DOI `10.1016/S0166-218X(01)00247-5`.

**Not new here:** Sturmian balance or complexity.

**Repository-specific here:** selecting the mechanical word of slope `1/phi^2` from the shortest rationally reducible `R4/U6` controller word and assigning it to the expensive-event schedule.

# Integral lattice gauge quotient and nonlinear closure obstruction

On the shared M/IIB section, write G for the map from a trivial-parameter potential to a generalized gauge parameter. Write D for the map from a parameter to its transport and algebraic action on generalized vectors. The integer coefficient matrices obey D_i G_i = 0 and D_i G_j + D_j G_i = 0. Therefore DG = 0 for any four commuting lattice differences. This is an exact linear identity.

## Integral site-space quotient

In native coordinates, signed permutations split G into two copies of a four-direction discrete gradient, their transposes, the degree-one to degree-two exterior derivative, its transpose, and zero rows and columns. The certificate records the signed coordinate maps. For the forward stencil T_i−1, the first and middle exterior derivatives are the cubical cellular coboundaries of a product of four cyclic graphs. Its integral cohomology is free, so each coboundary image is saturated in its target. Indeed the cokernel of a coboundary is an extension of the following image, a free group, by the corresponding cohomology. For each reversed coordinate block, reversing all site coordinates conjugates every shift to its transpose, so the block is integrally equivalent to the site-space transpose of the corresponding coboundary. Transposing preserves the nonzero Smith factors. The same argument applies to the centered stencil T_i−T_i⁻¹, since this equals (T_i²−1)T_i⁻¹. In each exterior block the factors T_i⁻¹ are removed by a unimodular translation on the component for each wedge index set. The remaining shift T_i² splits a cycle of length n_i into gcd(2,n_i) cycles. [Desbrun, Hirani, Leok and Marsden, Discrete Exterior Calculus](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0508341)

Every common nonzero stencil symbol has complex ranks 17 for D and 10 for G, by transport from a coordinate symbol under the shared SL(4) action. The image of G is saturated in site space, so ker D / im G is free. Its rank is 23 times the number of common zero modes. Forward differences have one common zero mode and rank 23 on every rectangular four-torus. Centered differences give rank 23 times the product of gcd(2,n_i). On the 16-site Q4 graph the forward result is 23 and the centered result is 368. Direct Smith reduction of the full 432-by-432 forward G on Q4 gives exactly 150 unit factors.

The integer Fourier-symbol quotient from the continuum calculation and the integer site-space quotient use different lattices. Even on Q4, where Fourier vectors have integer entries, the Walsh change of basis is not unimodular. Its forward symbols at nonzero characters have content 2, yet the global site-space calculation has no torsion. Multiplying every forward difference by 2 really does produce 150 copies of Z/2Z on Q4. ## The pointwise nonlinear bracket fails

Replacing the continuum derivative by the forward difference while retaining pointwise multiplication breaks the generalized Lie commutator on a three-site cycle. The certificate gives a doubled closure defect (2,2,2) supported in one anchor component. On five sites the obstruction is stronger for both stencils. Parameters at sites 0 and 1 produce a commutator that sends a field at site 2 to site 0 with coefficient 1. Every operator built from any parameter in the original pointwise ansatz has coefficient 0 between those sites. Thus changing the parameter bracket alone cannot restore closure with those operators. This is an explicit obstruction for the ExFT representation, consistent with lattice Leibniz obstructions. [Kato, Sakamoto and So](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3121)

For a forward cycle with n≥4, repeated commutators are more restrictive. In the single-anchor sector the site generators are A_i = E_(i,i+1) − E_(i−1,i−1), with indices modulo n. If C_i = [A_i,A_(i+1)], then −[A_(i+1),C_i]−C_i is E_(i,i+2), and subtracting this from C_i gives E_(i,i+1). Directed paths generate all off-diagonal matrix units by commutators. Subtracting E_(i,i+1) from A_i gives every diagonal matrix unit. The smallest closed matrix Lie algebra containing the forward anchor operators is consequently gl(n). Extending the operator family under ordinary commutators in this way introduces operators connecting every pair of sites.

## Pass 514 addendum (python track) — THE SIEVE THEOREM: one statement replaces two

Pass 514 (`analysis/w33_pass514_sieve_theorem.py`, 12/12 exact, idempotent):

1. **THE SIEVE THEOREM.** Let `e` = order of the generating character and `t | m` with **`m/t` odd and `e | (m/t)`**. Then **`Σ_{d|t} d·S_d = q·(Σ_{v≠0} d_v^{m/t})^t = 0`**. Proof: put `n = m/t`; for `d|t`, `n | (m/d)` so `e | (m/d)`, hence `M^{m/d} = I` and the zero-sum condition is automatic, making the orbit's value `q·∏_{i≤d} d_{w_i}^{m/d} = ∏_{i≤t} d_{w_i}^n` over the repeated t-tuple; since `d·S_d` counts each period-d orbit once per distinct t-tuple, summing over `d|t` sweeps every t-tuple once and gives `q(Σ_v d_v^n)^t`. Bracket vanishes since n odd and e|n. **QED**
2. **BOTH EARLIER THEOREMS ARE COROLLARIES.** Write `T = {t | m : m/t odd, e | (m/t)}`. For **m odd**, T = divisors of m/e — **downward closed** — so Möbius inversion runs (`S_1 = 0`, then `t·S_t = −Σ_{d|t,d<t} d·S_d = 0`), giving **Pass 511's odd-class vanishing theorem**. For **m even**, T is not downward closed (at m=jp only t=j survives) and it degenerates to **Pass 513's propagation relation**. **The odd/even divide is the question of whether a divisor set is downward closed.**
3. **A NEW CONSTRAINT AT EVEN m.** When `|T| > 1` the sieve reaches what neither corollary does: at **m=18, e=3, T={2,6}**, subtracting the relations leaves **`3S_3 + 6S_6 = 0`** — not an individual vanishing (neither `S_3` nor `S_6` is zero alone in any section sampled).
4. **THE SHORTCUT, VALIDATED BEFORE USE.** Whenever `e | (m/d)`, a period-d orbit's value is `q·∏ d_{w_i}^{m/d}` with **no matrix products**. Checked against the honest matrix computation on **15,616 orbits** over 𝔽₃,𝔽₅ and on the constant class over ℤ/9 *before* anything relied on it. It puts **ℤ/25 and ℤ/27** (624 and 728 vectors, 25×25 and 27×27 blocks) within reach — character-order form confirmed at a **second p² and a first p³**. Also measured: the sieve's individual-vanishing prediction is **exactly** the vanishing that holds in *every* section; classes vanishing in only some sections (d=3 at (3,6)) are section accidents.

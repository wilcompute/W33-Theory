# Spin-two overlap tests for finite cutoffs

The [graph obstruction](2026-11-14_kk_graph_sum_rule.md) applies to positive edge conductances. A different test starts from the first two continuum elastic spin-two overlap sum rules. For an external massive mode of squared mass \(\lambda\), write \(Q\) for its quartic overlap and \(c_j\) for its real cubic overlaps with the exchange modes of squared masses \(\lambda_j\). The rules require \(\sum_j c_j^2=Q\) and \(\sum_j \lambda_j c_j^2=4\lambda Q/3\). These govern the cancellations at orders \(s^5\) and \(s^4\) in the continuum calculation. [Chivukula et al., Eqs. 23 and 29](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06159)

## Highest-mode bound

For \(Q>0\), these equations give the exchange squared-mass mean \(4\lambda/3\). Taking the largest positive \(\lambda\) in a finite closed tower immediately yields an obstruction. More quantitatively, if the unweighted rule holds, the fractional deficit in the weighted rule for the highest mode is at least \(1/4\). If the massless mode has cubic coefficient \(c_0\), the deficit is at least \(1/4+3c_0^2/(4Q)\), since \(\sum_j\lambda_jc_j^2\le\lambda(Q-c_0^2)\). This does not require a graph, a local Laplacian, or positivity of its matrix entries. This is an overlap-level diagnostic of the earlier infinite-tower result. [Duff, Pope and Stelle](https://inspirehep.net/literature/25265)

For a smooth spectral truncation there is a second obstruction. Keeping the continuum quartic contact and projecting the exchange onto retained modes drops a positive squared norm whenever \(f^2\) leaves the retained space. A finite space of continuous real functions on a connected internal space that contains the constant and is closed under squaring is closed under multiplication by polarization. For any member \(f\), finite dimension then gives a nonzero polynomial \(P\) with \(P(f)=0\). Connectedness forces \(f\) to be constant. Thus a nontrivial finite smooth spectral space cannot preserve the unweighted rule for every superposition with the continuum contact unchanged.

## Exact low modes on an odd cyclic mesh

Take \(N=2M+1\) equally spaced sites with uniform probability measure, and let \([k]\) denote the representative of \(k\) between \(-M\) and \(M\). For any real even nonnegative dispersion with \(\lambda_0=0\), the normalized cosine at nonzero \(k\) has quartic moment \(3/2\). Its square has unit constant overlap and squared nonconstant overlap \(1/2\) at \(\pm[2k]\) together. The relative deficit is exactly \(1-\lambda_{[2k]}/(4\lambda_k)\) for \(\lambda_k>0\). The largest eigenvalue therefore has deficit at least \(3/4\), achieved by a flat dispersion on the nonzero modes.

For the Fourier spectral dispersion \(\lambda_k=k^2\), all cosines with \(|k|\le\lfloor M/2\rfloor\) pass, and all higher cosines fail. The mode \(M\) aliases its second harmonic to \(-1\) and has deficit \(1-1/(4M^2)\). The low-band result also holds for the polarized quartic overlap identity. For Fourier momenta \(a,b,c,d\) in that band, every pair is represented and discrete quadrature selects the actual zero total momentum. Then \(a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2=(a+b)^2+(a+c)^2+(a+d)^2\) follows from \(a+b+c+d=0\). This is the quartic overlap identity for arbitrary low-band external profiles by multilinearity. Numerically retaining those pair products is an instance of spectral dealiasing. [Orszag](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0469(1971)028%3C1074:OTEOAI%3E2.0.CO;2)

## Mixed harmonics on the D9 internal space

For the product of spheres in the repo's D9 Einstein-Maxwell candidate, take the normalized real mode \(f(x,y)=3xy\), where \(x,y\) are the polar cosines. Put \(A=R_a^{-2},B=R_b^{-2}\). Its squared mass is \(2(A+B)\). Since \(3x^2=1+2P_2(x)\), its exchange support is exactly \((0,0),(2,0),(0,2),(2,2)\) with squared overlaps \(1,4/5,4/5,16/25\), respectively. Their sum is \(81/25\) and their mass-weighted sum is \(216(A+B)/25\). These pass both overlap rules at arbitrary positive radii. A cutoff \(\ell_a+\ell_b\le2\) omits the \((2,2)\) exchange alone and thereby removes \(16/81\) of the unweighted sum and \(4/9\) of the weighted sum, independently of the ratio of radii. This evaluates the leading geometric overlaps. The full D9 amplitude also needs the scalar and vector sectors and flux-dependent vertices. [Bonifacio and Hinterbichler](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04767)

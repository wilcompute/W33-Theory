\subsection{Seventeen-term determinant compiler, photonic outer rail, and the corrected heterotic frontier}
\label{sec:20260918-det17-outer-rail-z6-correction}

The controlled determinant gate now has an exact sparse compiler. In the
certified ten-coordinate basis of \(\mathfrak{sp}_4(\mathbb F_3)\),
\[
X=
\begin{pmatrix}
2x_4&2x_8&x_0&x_1\\
2x_5&2x_9&x_1&x_2\\
x_3&x_6&x_4&x_5\\
x_6&x_7&x_8&x_9
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct expansion over \(\mathbb F_3\) contains exactly \(17\) nonzero quartic
monomials. Exhaustive evaluation on all \(3^{10}=59049\) controller states
verifies the identity pointwise. Hence
\[
 U_{\det}
 =\sum_X |X\rangle\!\langle X|\otimes z^{\det X}
 =\prod_{a=1}^{17}P_{m_a}(c_a),
\]
where the diagonal primitives commute and
\[
 P_m(c)|x,r\rangle=\omega^{r\,c\,m(x)}|x,r\rangle.
\]
Thus the nonlinear hardware target is a seventeen-primitive quartic phase
compiler rather than a \(59049\)-entry truth table. If the controller is
measured classically one may compute \(\det X\) digitally and apply
\(z^{\det X}\), but that fast path does not implement coherent non-Clifford
controller dynamics.

The outer logical operation also admits a concrete photonic encoding. Choose
two coherent rails for the two central characters,
\[
 r=1\leftrightarrow H\ {\rm(or\ path\ A)},\qquad
 r=2\leftrightarrow V\ {\rm(or\ path\ B)},
\]
and program the \(r=2\) rail with the conjugate qutrit phase convention
\(Z\mapsto Z^{-1}\). Then the certified outer involution is the rail swap,
\[

 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 X_L\,\operatorname{diag}(\omega,\omega^{-1})\,X_L
 =\operatorname{diag}(\omega^{-1},\omega).
\]
At the encoding layer this is implementable by a polarization swap or path
cross-connect. Once coherent access to both rails is available, an ordinary
two-mode mixer plus a relative phase gives arbitrary logical \(SU(2)\), not
only the finite \(\langle z,X_L\rangle\cong S_3\) subgroup. The unresolved
physical step is the coherent isometry from the native
\(V_1\oplus V_2\) carrier into those laboratory rails; representation theory
does not construct that interface.

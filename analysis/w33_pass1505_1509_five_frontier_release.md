## Pass 1506 — exact Smith forms and the bad primes

Let

- `C = N^T P N / 16`, the unsigned 15-dimensional projector lattice;
- `F = d^T P N / 16`, the orientation-twisted bridge;
- `Q = (K+6I)(K-2I)(K-4I)/8`, the signed 15-dimensional projector lattice.

The Smith forms are

- `SNF(C) = 1^10 3^5`;
- `SNF(F) = 1^10 3^4 6`;
- `SNF(Q) = 1^10 3^4 12`;
- `SNF([C F]) = 1^15 2^5 6^10`.

The exact scaled identities are

- `C^2 = 48 C`;
- `Q^2 = 96 Q`;
- `F^T F = 96 C`;
- `F F^T = 48 Q`;
- `QF = 96F` and `FC = 48F`.

Thus only 2 and 3 are bad primes. Modulo 2, `C` retains rank 15 while `F` and
`Q` drop to 14. Modulo 3, all three have rank 10. Away from 2 and 3, all have
rank 15 and the rational equivalence localizes without degeneration.

## Pass 1507 — explicit Morita context and algebra growth

Define rational idempotents and corner maps

`e=C/48`, `q=Q/96`, `x=F/48`, `y=F^T/96`.

Then

`xy=q`, `yx=e`, `qx=x=xe`, and `ey=y=yq`.

Therefore the unsigned and signed 15-dimensional corners are equivalent
idempotents in the joint algebra: the new bridge does complete the requested

`M^T M, K, N^T N, d^T d, C, F, F^T, Q`,

rational dimension is at least 301. This rules out a tiny Bose–Mesner-style
closure, but it is not yet the complete Wedderburn decomposition.

## Pass 1509 — `H(5,3)` and `M20`

An exhaustive scan of all `C(60,4)=487,635` four-frame deletions from the
canonical base cover finds exactly ten nearest neighbors sharing 56 frames.
The ten moves organize into five disjoint 16-edge packets. Each packet has
exactly three four-frame resolutions: the original and two alternatives.

Because the five edge packets are pairwise disjoint, their choices are
independent. The resulting cover chart is exactly

`{0,1,2}^5`, hence `3^5=243` covers,

with Hamming shells

`1, 10, 40, 80, 80, 32`.

The setwise stabilizer of the chart inside `PSp(4,3)` has order 960. Its action
on the five packet coordinates has image all of `A5`; the kernel is elementary
abelian `2^4`; and an explicit `A5` complement is found. Hence

`Stab(chart) = 2^4:A5 = M20`.

The ATLAS identifies the Mathieu group `M20` with precisely this split group of
order 960. This is an objectwise identification, not a numerical analogy.

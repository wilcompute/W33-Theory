\paragraph{4664: the two three-sheet symmetries are transverse, not identical.}
The $S_3$ acting on the three maximal-partial-spread lifts over one protected
45-object and the $S_3$ acting on the three global degree-36 D4 spread sheets
have different ambient order-three directions.  The packet rotation
$C_3^{\rm pkt}$ lies inside $\mathrm{PSp}(4,3)$, while the sheet rotation
$C_3^{\rm sh}$ is the type-preserving centralizer $C_3$ outside the distinguished
$\mathrm{PSp}(4,3)$.  Hence they cannot be identified by an automorphism preserving
that subgroup.  They commute and intersect trivially.  A common outer reflection
inverts both, giving the joint finite symmetry
\[
 (C_3^{\rm pkt}\times C_3^{\rm sh}){:}C_2,
 \qquad |\cdot|=18.
\]
Their abstract three-point permutation representations are isomorphic, but their
ambient actions are transverse.

\paragraph{4665: the full automorphism group of the $45\times40$ incidence.}

 \boxed{\operatorname{Aut}(T)=\mathrm{PGSp}(4,3),\qquad |\operatorname{Aut}(T)|=51840.}
\]
Thus one 85-vertex bipartite incidence packages the E6/center-quad 45 and both
inequivalent W33 point/line carriers under their complete common automorphism group.

\paragraph{4666: the Hermitian binary-rank problem is a 2-adic eigenlattice problem.}
For $Q^-(5,q)=GQ(q,q^2)$ let $N$ be the integral point-line incidence matrix,
$L=\operatorname{im}_{\mathbf Z}N$, and $K=\ker_{\mathbf Z}N^T$.  The kernel $K$
is primitive and
\[
 NN^T=(q^2+1)I+A_{\rm point},
 \qquad
 K=\ker_{\mathbf Z}(NN^T),
\]
so $K$ is exactly the integral $-(q^2+1)$ eigenlattice of the point graph.
Since $L_{\mathbf Q}=K_{\mathbf Q}^{\perp}$,
\[
 L_{\rm sat}=(L\otimes\mathbf Q)\cap\mathbf Z^v=K_{\mathbf Z}^{\perp}.
\]
Therefore the all-odd-$q$ binary rank conjecture is precisely
\[
 \boxed{L\otimes\mathbf Z_2=K_{\mathbf Z}^{\perp}\otimes\mathbf Z_2.}
\]
Equivalently the finite saturation quotient $K^{\perp}/L$ has no 2-primary part,
or every nonzero Smith factor of $N$ is odd.  The exact $q=3,5,7$ anchors satisfy
this equality.  The general 2-adic equality remains open.

\paragraph{Evidence boundary.}
All closed statements above are finite group, incidence, module, code, or integral
lattice theorems.  The general odd-$q$ Hermitian 2-saturation statement in
Pass~4666 remains open.  The oriented double cover requires one global orientation
choice.  No M24 subgroup, physical chirality, family symmetry, or dynamical
interpretation follows from these finite structures alone.

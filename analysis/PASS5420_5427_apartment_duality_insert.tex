\subsection*{Passes 5420--5427: complementary Hodge frames, apartment intersections, footprint bicycles, and the discrete $q^4$ derivative}

Let $\Gamma$ be the Levi graph of a generalized quadrangle $\operatorname{GQ}(q,q)$, let $X=L(\Gamma)$ be its flag line graph, and write
\[
 N=(q+1)^2(q^2+1),\qquad h=q^4,\qquad r=N-q^4=2(q+1)(q^2+1)-1.
\]
Passes 5388--5409 identified the terminal flag idempotent
\[
 E=\frac{q^4A_0-q^3A_1+q^2A_2-qA_3+A_4}{N}
\]
as the orthogonal projector onto the $h$-dimensional Levi cycle space.

\paragraph{Pass 5420: the all-$q$ complementary cut frame.}
The normalized rows of $E$ form a unit-norm tight frame with Gram matrix
\[
 G_{\rm cyc}=\frac{N}{q^4}E,
 \qquad
 \langle x_e,x_f\rangle=\left(-\frac1q\right)^{d_X(e,f)}.
\]
The Naimark-complement projector $I-E$ has constant diagonal $r/N$, so its normalized rows form a second unit-norm tight frame,
\[
 G_{\rm cut}=\frac{N}{r}(I-E).
\]
For distinct flags at distance $d=1,2,3,4$,
\[
 \boxed{\langle y_e,y_f\rangle=\frac{(-1)^{d+1}q^{4-d}}{r}}.
\]
The pair satisfies the exact weighted complement identity
\[
 \boxed{\frac{q^4}{N}G_{\rm cyc}+\frac{r}{N}G_{\rm cut}=I_N}.
\]
At $q=3$ this recovers the frozen BT549 cut-frame inner products
\[
 1,\quad \frac{27}{79},\quad-\frac9{79},\quad\frac3{79},\quad-\frac1{79}.
\]
The Naimark-complement language is standard frame theory; the statement here is the exact generalized-quadrangle Hodge specialization.

\paragraph{Pass 5421: the canonical $H_1$--footprint exact sequence.}
Pass5066 gives the apartment/theta presentation
\[
 0\longrightarrow\Theta\longrightarrow\mathbb F_2^{\mathcal A}
 \stackrel{\partial}{\longrightarrow}H_1(\Gamma;\mathbb F_2)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since the binary apartment code is $C_A=\Theta^\perp$, dualizing identifies it canonically and equivariantly with $H_1(\Gamma;\mathbb F_2)^*$.  For odd prime powers, Pass5384 gives
\[
 0\longrightarrow D_q\longrightarrow C_A\longrightarrow C_F\longrightarrow0,
\]
where $\dim C_F=g=q(q^2+1)/2$.  Hence
\[
 \boxed{0\longrightarrow D_q\longrightarrow H_1^*\longrightarrow C_F\longrightarrow0},
\]
and dualizing once more gives
\[
 \boxed{0\longrightarrow C_F^*\longrightarrow H_1\longrightarrow D_q^*\longrightarrow0}.
\]
Thus the all-odd footprint quotient is a canonical quotient of the dual Levi cycle module, and its dual is a canonical submodule of Levi $H_1$.  No unproved self-duality identification of $C_F$ with $C_F^*$ is used here.

\paragraph{Pass 5422: the all-$q$ apartment-intersection profile.}
Two distinct apartments cannot meet in two disconnected edge intervals: otherwise their symmetric difference would contain at least two cycles whose total length is $<16$, contradicting Levi girth eight.  Thus every nonempty proper intersection is one path.  If the common path had at least five edges, the two complementary paths would form a cycle of length at most six.  Therefore a proper nonempty apartment intersection is exactly one consecutive flag-edge path of length $j\in\{1,2,3,4\}$.

Let $T_j=q^{5-j}$ be the Pass5386 number of apartments through a fixed consecutive $j$-edge path, and let $a_j$ be the number of other apartments whose exact intersection with a fixed apartment is a specified $j$-edge path.  Since a fixed $j$-path is contained in exactly $k-j+1$ of the $k$-paths of the base apartment,
\[
 T_j=1+\sum_{k=j}^4(k-j+1)a_k.
\]
Back-substitution yields
\[
 a_4=q-1,\qquad
 a_3=(q-1)^2,\qquad
 a_2=q(q-1)^2,\qquad
 a_1=q^2(q-1)^2.
\]
There are eight cyclic paths of each length, so for a fixed apartment the numbers of distinct partners meeting it in $j$ edges are
\[
 \boxed{n_4=8(q-1),\quad n_3=8(q-1)^2,\quad n_2=8q(q-1)^2,\quad n_1=8q^2(q-1)^2},
\]
with $n_0=Nq^4/8-1-n_1-n_2-n_3-n_4$.  Independent projective-polarity enumerations at $q=2,3,5$ give respectively
\[
 (n_0,n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4)=(25,32,16,8,8),
\]
\[
 (1187,288,96,32,16),
\]
and
\[
 (69124,3200,640,128,32).
\]
The middle line is exactly the frozen BT546 W33 census.

For oriented apartment columns $c_A$, the sign products are constant along a common path, hence
\[
 |\langle c_A,c_B\rangle|=|A\cap B|.
\]
After unit normalization by $\|c_A\|=\sqrt8$, the full apartment frame has absolute inner products
\[
 0,\ \frac18,\ \frac14,\ \frac38,\ \frac12,
\]
so its coherence is exactly $1/2$, independently of $q$.

\paragraph{Pass 5423: exact unsigned visibility conditioning.}
For the unsigned apartment-incidence matrix $B$, Pass5404 gives five positive eigenvalues of $BB^T$.  The maximum is
\[
 \lambda_{\max}=8q^4,
\]
and the minimum is the terminal value
\[
 \boxed{\lambda_{\min}=(q-1)^2(q^2+1)}.
\]
The only nontrivial comparison is against
$2q^2(q-1)(q+1-\sqrt{2q})$.  After isolating the radical, squaring leaves
\[
 (q^2+1)(q^4-2q^3+6q^2-2q+1)>0,
\]
whose quartic equals $q^2(q-1)^2+5q^2-2q+1>0$ for $q>1$.  Consequently
\[
 \boxed{\kappa(BB^T)=\frac{8q^4}{(q-1)^2(q^2+1)}\longrightarrow8},
\]
while the singular-value condition number is
\[
 \boxed{\kappa_2(B)=\frac{\sqrt8\,q^2}{(q-1)\sqrt{q^2+1}}\longrightarrow\sqrt8}.
\]
At $q=3$, $\kappa(BB^T)=81/5$.  This statement concerns the full incidence operator, not arbitrary sparse hardware submatrices.

\paragraph{Pass 5426, outside box: the footprint code is the point half of the Levi bicycle space.}
Now assume $q$ odd and let $M$ be the binary point--line incidence matrix.  Write $D$ for the binary vertex--flag incidence matrix of the Levi graph.  Since every Levi vertex has even degree $q+1$,
\[
 DD^T=\begin{pmatrix}0&M\\M^T&0\end{pmatrix}\pmod2.
\]
For $x\in\ker M^T=C_W^\perp=C_F$, define the flag vector
\[
 (\iota_Px)_{(p,L)}=x_p.
\]
It is a cut because it is the coboundary of the point-supported vertex function $x$, and it is a cycle because $M^Tx=0$ while $(q+1)x_p=0$ in characteristic two.  Thus
\[
 \boxed{C_F\hookrightarrow \operatorname{im}D^T\cap\ker D}
\]
is the canonical point-half bicycle embedding.  Dually, $\ker M$ gives a line-supported kernel copy; this is only an incidence-dual copy and is not promoted as an $L$-footprint tensor.

Both kernels have dimension $g=q(q^2+1)/2$.  Their flag images meet in the all-ones flag vector $J$, because the only kernel of $D^T$ on the connected Levi vertex space is the global all-ones vertex vector.  Hence
\[
 \boxed{\mathrm{Bike}=P_g+L_g,\qquad P_g\cap L_g=\langle J\rangle,\qquad
 \dim\mathrm{Bike}=2g-1=q(q^2+1)-1}.
\]
At $q=3$ this gives $P_{15}$ and $\mathrm{Bike}_{29}$ exactly, identifying the Pass5068 point half with the already-proved $[40,15,8]_2$ footprint code.  At $q=5$ it predicts the exact bicycle dimension $129$ from two $65$-dimensional halves meeting in $J$.

\paragraph{Pass 5427, outside box: an orthogonal two-design decomposition of the regular simplex.}
Let $P_0=J/N$, $P_{\rm cyc}=E$, and
\[
 P_{\rm cut,0}=I-P_0-P_{\rm cyc}.
\]
These are mutually orthogonal projectors of ranks
\[
 1,\qquad q^4,\qquad r-1,
\]
and $q^4+(r-1)=N-1$.  Both nonconstant projectors have constant diagonal.  Their normalized row projections are centered unit-norm tight frames, hence spherical $2$-designs.  If $G_{\rm cut,0}$ is the centered-cut unit Gram and $G_{\rm simplex}=\frac{N}{N-1}(I-J/N)$ is the unit regular-simplex Gram, then
\[
 \boxed{\frac{q^4}{N-1}G_{\rm cyc}+\frac{r-1}{N-1}G_{\rm cut,0}=G_{\rm simplex}}.
\]
Thus the flag regular simplex splits orthogonally into its Hodge-cycle design and its nonconstant cut design.  At $q=3$, the $160$ simplex vertices decompose into dimensions $81$ and $78$.

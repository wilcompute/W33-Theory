# Reference reflection in the three W33 adjoint monopole blocks

The zero-energy radial operator for each paired adjoint charge has eight components. Two-sided shooting distinguishes the regular core subspace, the three exponentially decaying heavy solutions, and the two light directions. It finds an open light scattering channel in each block with a substantial numerical separation between the regular and localized subspaces. ## Operator and matching

The angular coordinates are (u,v) in each Weyl flavor and chirality. The flux matrix is J=diag(−σ1⊗I,+σ1⊗I), with the left and right kinetic signs fixed by their chirality. The Hermitian radial Hamiltonian is −iJ∂r+V. Its gauge term follows from the K/r angular operator. Its off-diagonal chirality block is the angular image of the two native north-pole mass matrices, divided by the generator trace norm squared six. The common gauge Yukawa convention phase is set to one. The numerical comparison uses a real gauge Yukawa scale of one and separately tests sqrt(2).

The core basis starts in v=0 near the origin. The exterior basis starts in the negative-eigenvalue subspace of −iJV in the constant vacuum beyond radius five. Its light states are the incoming and outgoing unit-flux gauginos. Both bases propagate to an intermediate radius in short steps with QR resets. When the decaying basis is reset, its component in each light column can be subtracted, since adding any decaying solution leaves that light column's asymptotic amplitude unchanged. This controls exponential growth in shooting. The code checks the radial flux pairing of the matched subspaces and light directions. Let Q and W be the four regular and three decaying orthonormal columns at the matching radius. A localized zero mode requires a kernel of the eight-by-seven matrix [Q, −W]. Its smallest singular value σ satisfies σ²=1−||Q†W|| by the Gram matrix of the joined basis. This gives a target for a validated computation. If the sum of the errors in the regular and decaying orthogonal projectors is below σ², the corresponding exact subspaces still have no intersection. This follows by bounding the change in the product of the two projectors by the sum of their errors. With no kernel, match one prescribed incoming light solution to an outgoing light solution by solving the eight-by-eight system obtained by adding the outgoing column. The localized diagnostic and the reflection both use the full coupled system.

## The reference phase is discrete

For real profiles and z=1, the diagonal chirality blocks of V are imaginary and its off-diagonal blocks are real. Multiplying the right chirality coordinates by i conjugates the transfer equation to a real equation. The regular and decaying subspaces then admit real bases. In this frame a real left light vector and an imaginary right light vector are the two flux-normalized channel conventions. Conjugation therefore forces the reference reflection to be imaginary wherever the matching is unique. Flux conservation gives its unit modulus, so the only values are ±i. The sign remains fixed under a continuous real deformation as long as the reflection exists continuously. This is a statement in the specified light-state frame.

## The phase sources imaginary scalar variations

On a real profile the imaginary scalar force from the phase-dependent F interference is proportional to sin φ. For a mixed cubic Z_j A X_k, the imaginary variations of X_k and Z_j differentiate its adjoint F contribution as i times the variation of Z_j X_k^T. Contracting that real variation with the trace F term gives the two matter components of the force, 2 sin φ times T^T Z_j and T X_k, where T is the trace F covector projected to the neutral plane. The adjoint component is −2 sin φ times the trace Hessian transposed against the mixed F covector. The rest of the energy has zero first imaginary variation on real fields. The producer checks the assembled 37-component force against a directional complex-field difference and tests its contraction with the neutral imaginary gauge tangents. The matter part has norm about 165.814 on the r=0.002 sample before multiplication by sin φ or the radial volume factor. A continuous real family through that profile therefore cannot be stationary for nearby nonzero phases. The fixed-profile reflection law still applies as a kinematic identity, while a phase-dependent physical solution requires the coupled complex radial equations.

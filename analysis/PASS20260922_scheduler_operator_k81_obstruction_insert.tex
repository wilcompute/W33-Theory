\subsection*{The full $81$-state scheduler compiler has equivariant rank ceiling $27$}

Let
\[
K=H_{27}^{\rm address}\times C_3^{\rm external},
\qquad |K|=81.
\]
The address chart is the regular $K$-module on the frozen labels
$((a,b,c),p)$.  On the operator side, restricting the landed matter-$81$
carrier to the internal trinification $H_{27}$ and the external qutrit shift
gives
\[
(9V_\omega)\otimes\operatorname{Reg}(C_3).
\]
For each of the three external $C_3$ characters the regular scheduler supplies
three copies of $V_\omega$, while the operator carrier requires nine.
Therefore
\[
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{K}=3(3\cdot9)=81,
\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}T\le
3\bigl(3\min(3,9)\bigr)=27.}
\]
No invertible $81\times81$ $K$-equivariant compiler exists.

The same obstruction is visible from the characteristic commutator subgroup
\[
[K,K]=Z(H_{27})\times\{0\}\cong C_3.
\]
Its nontrivial generator has eigenspaces $27+27+27$ on the regular address
module but is scalar on the operator matter module.

\paragraph{Consequence.}
The root-address compiler must change symmetry: it can be non-equivariant,
equivariant only under a proper common subgroup, or formulated after replacing
or enlarging one of the two actions.  This theorem does not decide which such
compiler is physically selected.

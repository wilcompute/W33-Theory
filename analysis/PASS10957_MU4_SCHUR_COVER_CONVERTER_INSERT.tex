\subsection{Pass 10957: a $\mu_4$ determinant phase converts the two $S_4$ Schur cocycles}

A deterministic section of the frozen central quotient
$GL(2,3)\to PGL(2,3)\cong S_4$ gives a binary cocycle $c_+$, verified on
all $24^3$ triples and mapped objectwise back to all $48^2$ products of the
original clock group.  Its extension has $13$ involutions, the
$2^+S_4\cong GL(2,3)$ profile.

If $\epsilon$ is permutation parity, then
\[
 c_-(\sigma,\tau)
 =c_+(\sigma,\tau)+\epsilon(\sigma)\epsilon(\tau)\pmod2
\]
is again a cocycle.  Its extension has one involution and the
$2^-S_4$ / binary-octahedral order spectrum.  After enlarging central phases
from $\mu_2$ to $\mu_4$, however,
\[
 b_\pm(\sigma)=(\pm i)^{\epsilon(\sigma)}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \delta b_\pm(\sigma,\tau)
 =(-1)^{\epsilon(\sigma)\epsilon(\tau)}.
\]
Thus the two nonisomorphic double covers become cohomologous as complex
projective cocycles under this determinant-phase gauge.

On the projective clock $C_4$, the two missing Pass~10954 characters are
exactly the two square roots $\bar g\mapsto\pm i$ of permutation sign.
This resolves the plus/minus lifting mismatch between the finite clock and
the Pass~10956 Albert half-turn at cocycle level, but no carrier-level matrix
intertwiner is yet asserted.

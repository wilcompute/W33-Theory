\subsection{Pass 10956: the Albert Peirce $16$ contains the same $D_4$ half-spin transposition}

Inside the executable compact $\mathfrak{spin}(9)$ of Pass~10950, the
stabiliser of the spatial Peirce-0 direction $s_0=e_{g_2}-e_{g_3}$ has
dimension $28$.  On the Peirce $16$ its exact commutant has dimension two.
The non-scalar generator rescales to an involution
\[
 \Chi^2=I_{16},\qquad \operatorname{tr}\Chi=0,
 \qquad 16=8_{+}\oplus8_{-},
\]
and each eight-dimensional eigenspace has scalar commutant, with no
intertwiner between the two.  Thus the certified Spin(9) spinor restricts
objectwise as $8_s\oplus8_c$ under Spin(8).

For a spatial vector $z\perp s_0$, the derivation
$D=[L_{s_0},L_z]\in\mathfrak{spin}(9)$ generates a half-turn that sends
$s_0\mapsto-s_0$, normalises the same unoriented Spin(8), and conjugates
$\Chi\mapsto-\Chi$, exchanging the two eight-dimensional projector images.
Its square is $-I_{16}$, the already-certified $2\pi$ Spin(9) matter-parity
sign of Pass~10950.

This realizes on the actual Albert spinor the same $D_4$ outer transposition
found for the finite clock in Pass~10955.  It does not yet identify the full
order-eight $GL(2,3)$ clock generator with this order-four Spin(9) half-turn;
the shared structure is the half-spin transposition and central sign.

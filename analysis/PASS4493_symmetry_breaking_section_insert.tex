\subsection{Geometric symmetry breaking selects a protected complement (Pass 4493)}
\label{sec:pass4493-symmetry-breaking-section}

The full $PSp(4,3)$ action forbids an equivariant section of
\[
0\to K/J\;(29)\to E=M/J\;(39)\to V=M/K\cong H_{10}\;(10)\to0.
\]
Pass~4493 restricts the same exact section equations to natural geometric
stabilizers.  The first tested canonical symmetry breaking already succeeds:
fixing one point or one line leaves a subgroup of order $648$ and index $40$,
and the affine section equations become consistent,
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}A=\operatorname{rank}[A\mid b]=370.}
\]
Hence each point or line stabilizer admits a $20$-dimensional affine family of
$H$-equivariant linear sections $V\to E$.  The corresponding fixed-space
dimensions are
\[
\dim E^H=\dim V^H=3.
\]

Finer geometric gauges admit still larger section families.  For an incident
point--line flag stabilizer of order $162$ (index $160$), the section rank is
$338$ and the affine family has dimension $52$.  For the setwise stabilizer of
one building apartment, of order $16$ (index $1620$), the rank is $308$ and
the section family has dimension $82$.  By contrast, the full group retains
\[
\operatorname{rank}A=389,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}[A\mid b]=390,
\]
and no section.

Thus, among the tested canonical geometric stabilizers,
\[
\boxed{
\text{full W33 symmetry}\Rightarrow\text{protected sector only as a quotient},
}
\]
while
\[
\boxed{
\text{fix one point or one line}\Rightarrow\text{an equivariant 10D complement can be chosen}.
}
\]
This is a symmetry-breaking gauge statement, not a classification of all
subgroups of $PSp(4,3)$.  In particular, it does not prove that no larger
non-geometric subgroup splits the extension, and a linear section is not by
itself a physical decoder or hardware implementation.

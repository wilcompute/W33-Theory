# Fixed-charge boundary transfer for unequal five-dimensional STU scalars

For three distinct positive electric STU charges, the split E6 charge stabilizer and the two STU scalars generate a local 42-parameter family of variations of the asymptotic scalars at fixed charge. The horizon map in this family has rank 28 and kernel dimension 14. This statement concerns derivatives of an explicit family of static two-derivative solutions. [STU harmonic solution](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9708103) · [Attractor moduli](https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.3488)

Write scalar points as K g and the electric potential as ||g Q|| squared. The classical duality of the electric equations maps the STU solution to the scalar path K g(r)h when h fixes Q, transforming the vector fields in the corresponding representation and leaving the spacetime metric fixed. At infinity the seed is K, and at its horizon g is the balancing exponential D from the [nearest minimum](2026-11-14_e6_attractor_gradient.md). An infinitesimal stabilizer generator A therefore has asymptotic scalar tangent sym(A). At the horizon, right transport by D inverse identifies its tangent with sym(D A D inverse). The two independent STU deformations of the asymptotic harmonic constants keep their product at one and leave the horizon scalars unchanged. Products of these deformations with the charge stabilizer are again exact solutions.

The boundary stabilizer tangent rank is 52 minus the dimension of the compact stabilizer of Q. The quaternionic spectral representation makes that compact centralizer explicit. For positive distinct STU coefficients all four eigenvalues are distinct, and the compact stabilizer has four Sp(1) factors of total dimension 12. A single equality among the coefficients merges two eigenspaces, giving Sp(2) times two Sp(1) factors of total dimension 16. At the balanced point the three lower eigenvalues coincide, yielding Sp(3) times Sp(1), of dimension 24. Conjugation by D identifies the split stabilizers, so the horizon rank is always 52-24=28.

The logarithm of D is the unique nearest-minimum tangent. The compact boundary stabilizer fixes it by uniqueness, so every skew boundary stabilizer generator is still skew after conjugating by D. The horizon map thus descends to the boundary tangent. This step uses the metric-defined logarithm. A torus exponential with the same three charge rescalings can differ by a transformation in the charge stabilizer and need not provide the same boundary-to-horizon map. The two diagonal STU directions are independent of the stabilizer tangent for nonzero triad coefficients: the diagonal equations for A Q=0 force three zero diagonal entries on the triad since no root has a matrix entry within it.

The verifier computes the stabilizer and the stacked boundary and horizon maps over exact rationals. It chooses sixth-power charge ratios so the diagonal entries of D are rational. For distinct charges the 42-dimensional boundary tangent is exhausted and the map is onto the full 28-dimensional minimum tangent. With repeated charges the stabilizer-plus-STU construction spans 38 or 30 boundary directions. The missing directions in those cases require other families or linearized equations. The equal-charge radial calculation supplies the full linear response there. Local coverage by this constructed family for distinct charges leaves uniqueness of more general radial boundary-value solutions open.

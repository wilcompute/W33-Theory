\begin{abstract}
We study the graph whose 540 vertices are unordered pairs of disjoint totally
isotropic lines of the symplectic generalized quadrangle \(W(3,3)\), with two
vertices adjacent when their canonical cross-matchings share an edge. The graph
is the edge-disjoint union of 240 cliques of order nine. A spread determines a
45-vertex independent seed which is not maximal: fifteen residual candidate
frames can be adjoined individually. Nevertheless the seed cannot be completed
to a 60-frame exact cover, because the candidates collectively meet only 20 of
the 60 residual edges. For spreads carrying a fixed-point-free linewise
involution, we prove the completing-frame count and support fraction
\(q(q^2+1)/2\) and \(1/q\), respectively. The involution is constructed for the
associated Desarguesian symplectic spread from a nonsquare symplectic
similitude. We separate these finite-geometric statements from the
representation theory of the orientation-signed 240-edge module. Its unique
non-rational constituent is a coexact real 90 with Eisenstein character field
and integral phase units \(C_6\). Charge and homological-flux readings of this
phase are withdrawn. The chromatic decision \(\chi(H)=9\) remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Objects and computational conventions}
Let \(W(q,q)\) denote the symplectic generalized quadrangle. At \(q=3\) it has
40 points, 40 totally isotropic lines, and 240 collinear point pairs. A
\emph{frame} is an unordered pair of disjoint isotropic lines together with its
canonical cross-matching. There are 540 frames. The frame graph \(H\) has frames
as vertices, adjacent when their matching-edge sets intersect.

All finite statements at \(q=3\) were checked from literal point, line, edge,
frame, and group actions. Statements at \(q=5,7\) are labelled finite-case
verification unless a uniform argument is supplied. Pass 1971 corrected an
earlier claim: the 45-frame spread seed is not a maximal independent set.

\section{The frame graph}
\begin{proposition}[exact at \(q=3\)]
Each edge of \(W(3,3)\) lies in exactly nine frames. Those nine frames form a
clique in \(H\), the 240 cliques are edge-disjoint, and
\[
 |V(H)|=540,\qquad |E(H)|=8640,\qquad \deg H=32.
\]
Consequently \(H\) is exactly the union of the 240 edge-indexed \(K_9\)'s.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Literal incidence enumeration gives nine frames through each edge. Each frame
has four matching edges, and each such edge contributes eight neighbours, so the
degree is \(4\cdot 8=32\). The clique edge count
\(240\binom92=8640\) equals \(540\cdot32/2\). Direct verification that adjacent
frames share one matching edge removes multiple counting.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
The assertion \(\chi(H)=9\) is equivalent to assigning nine colours to the 540
frames so that every edge-indexed \(K_9\) contains all nine colours.
\end{corollary}

\section{The spread seed and the corrected obstruction}
Let \(S\) be a spread, consisting of \(q^2+1\) pairwise disjoint isotropic lines
partitioning the points.

\begin{theorem}[uniform incidence proof]
The \(\binom{q^2+1}{2}\) pairs of spread lines form an independent set of \(H\).
Their matchings cover precisely the collinear point pairs whose endpoints lie on
different spread lines. The uncovered set is exactly the union of the internal
edges of the spread lines and has size
\[
 (q^2+1)\binom{q+1}{2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A matching edge between spread lines \(L\) and \(M\) identifies the unique pair
\(\{L,M\}\), because each endpoint lies on a unique spread line. Hence matching
sets of distinct spread pairs are disjoint. Every collinear pair whose endpoints
lie on different spread lines belongs to the unique matching between those two
lines. The only uncovered edges are internal to spread lines, giving the count.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[exact for all 36 spreads at \(q=3\)]
The spread seed has 45 frames and leaves 60 residual edges. Exactly 15 candidate
frames are supported entirely on those residual edges. The seed is therefore
not maximal independent. The 15 candidates collectively meet only 20 residual
edges, leaving 40 residual edges in no candidate frame. Consequently no spread
seed extends to a 60-frame exact cover.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[withdrawal]
The earlier phrase ``maximal independent set of size 45'' is false and is
withdrawn. The obstruction is support deficiency, not maximality.
\end{remark}

\section{The \(1/q\) support theorem}
Assume that a spread \(S\) admits a projective involution \(\sigma\) which fixes
every line of \(S\) setwise, fixes no point, and fixes no line outside \(S\)
setwise.

\begin{theorem}[uniform under the stated involution hypothesis]
The residual candidate frames are exactly the line orbits
\(\{A,\sigma(A)\}\) with \(A\notin S\). Their number is
\[
 \frac{q(q^2+1)}2.
\]
Their matching edges occupy
\[
 \frac{(q+1)(q^2+1)}2
\]
distinct residual edges, a fraction \(1/q\) of the residual set, and every
supported edge has multiplicity exactly \(q\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A line \(A\notin S\) meets \(q+1\) spread lines, one at each point. Since
\(\sigma\) fixes those spread lines, the canonical matching between \(A\) and
\(\sigma(A)\) is \(\{\{x,\sigma(x)\}:x\in A\}\) and lies inside the residual
set. Conversely a residual frame must pair every point with its certified mate
on the same spread line, hence has the form \(\{A,\sigma(A)\}\). There are
\(q(q^2+1)\) lines outside the spread and no fixed ones, giving the candidate
count. Each spread line contributes \((q+1)/2\) paired edges. A fixed paired edge
lies in one candidate for each of the \(q\) non-spread lines through one
endpoint, proving the multiplicity.
\end{proof}

The measured ratios at \(q=3,5,7\) are \(20/60\), \(78/390\), and
\(200/1400\). For even \(q\), a spread line has odd size \(q+1\), so no
fixed-point-free involution can partition it into 2-cycles. This explains the
measured zero-candidate case at \(q=2\).

\section{The nonsquare-similitude construction}
\begin{theorem}[associated Desarguesian spread, odd \(q\)]
Choose a nonsquare \(\mu\in\mathbb F_q\), let
\(K=\mathbb F_q(\alpha)\) with \(\alpha^2=\mu\), and regard the four-dimensional
\(\mathbb F_q\)-space as a two-dimensional \(K\)-space. Multiplication by
\(\alpha\) gives a symplectic similitude \(g\) with \(g^2=\mu I\). Its
projective action is a fixed-point-free involution fixing the associated
Desarguesian symplectic spread linewise.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Projectively, \(g\) has order two because its square is scalar. A fixed projective
point would give an eigenvalue \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_q\) satisfying
\(\lambda^2=\mu\), impossible for nonsquare \(\mu\). The one-dimensional
\(K\)-subspaces are two-dimensional \(\mathbb F_q\)-subspaces fixed setwise by
multiplication by \(\alpha\), and form the standard associated spread.
\end{proof}

At \(q=3\), exhaustive computation establishes uniqueness of the nontrivial
linewise stabilizer for all 36 spreads and the exact \(36/270\) nonsquare/square
multiplier split. Uniform uniqueness beyond \(q=3\) is not claimed.

\section{The orientation-signed edge module}
Over \(PGSp(4,3)\), the signed 240-edge module decomposes multiplicity-freely as
\[
 V=15\oplus24\mid81\mid30\oplus90,
\]
with exact, harmonic, and coexact dimensions \(39,81,120\). The coexact 90
restricts to a complex-conjugate \(45\oplus\overline{45}\) pair for
\(PSp(4,3)\), and
\[
 \operatorname{End}_{PSp}(V)\cong\mathbb R^4\times\mathbb C.
\]
The 90 is the only non-rational block, with character field
\(\mathbb Q(\omega)\) and finite integral phase units \(C_6\). Its square
contains the characteristic unique odd subgroup \(C_3\) of the finite
centralizer torsion. The outer involution acts by inversion, so the phase label
has normalizer \(D_{12}\).

Multiplicity-freeness gives
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{PSp}(90,X)=0,
 \qquad X\in\{15,24,30,81\}.
\]
Thus the phase is linearly confined to the coexact 90 in the ideal equivariant
model.

\section{Ownership, prior art, and novelty boundary}
General symplectic-spread, regular-spread, and spread-of-symmetry frameworks are
standard. The relevant background includes Thas--Payne and De Bruyn. General
facts about non-real symplectic characters and twisted Frobenius--Schur
indicators belong to Gow and Vinroot and were already present in repository
Passes 353/355. The exact \(q=3\) \(36/270\) split has no located reference and
is labelled likely known rather than advertised as new.

The paper-level contribution claimed here is restricted to the literal frame
and spread calculations, their proof organisation and scope boundaries, and the
module-specific localization and retraction record.

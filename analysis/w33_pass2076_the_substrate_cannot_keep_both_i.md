# Pass 2076 — the substrate has two `i`'s, and the one it can build destroys the one it has

Following the `q ≡ 3 (mod 4)` convergence one step further. There are **two**
complex structures in this arc, they exist under the same condition, and they are
incompatible.

---

## The two `i`'s

| | what it is | where it lives | exists when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `i_geom` | `g` with `g² = −I` on `F_q⁴`, a symplectic **similitude** with non-square multiplier | the geometry; its image is `σ_S` | `q ≡ 3 (mod 4)` (Pass 2071) |
| `i_rep` | `J` with `J² = −1`, `End_PSp(90) ≅ ℂ` | the coexact block of the signed edge module | `q ≡ 3 (mod 4)` (Gow, via Pass 1885) |

`i_geom` is the multiplication-by-`i` that builds the spread involution.
`i_rep` is the phase — the substrate's only internal `U(1)`, unique up to sign
(Pass 1895).

---

## They are incompatible, and the proof is one line

`σ_S` is **outer** in `PGSp(4,3)` — its multiplier is a non-square, which is
exactly what makes it outer (Pass 1908). And outer elements act as complex
conjugation on `Irr(PSp(4,3))` (Pass 1900), so they send `J ↦ −J`.

Verified directly rather than inferred:

```text
sigma_S : class size 36, order 2, OUTER: true
Res_PSp(90) constituents : degrees 45, 45
conjugating the first 45 by sigma_S gives the SECOND 45 : TRUE
```

> **`σ_S` swaps the two degree-45s.** The spread involution — the element that
> generates the resolution obstruction, reads the substrate's full handedness
> (Pass 1829), and *is* multiplication by `i` on `F_q⁴` — is exactly an element
> that **inverts the substrate's phase**.

So:

> **The substrate can build an `i` geometrically only by using an element that
> destroys its representation-theoretic `i`.**

Both require `q ≡ 3 (mod 4)`; neither can be had while the other is respected.

## The group they generate

```text
J² = −1 ,   σ_S² = 1 ,   σ_S J σ_S = −J
```

That is `ℤ₄ ⋊ ℤ₂ = D₄` of order 8 — **not** the quaternions, which would need
`σ_S² = −1`. This is the same shape the parallel track found for the phase label
(`C₆ ⋊ C₂ = D₁₂`, their Pass 2015), with `σ_S` identified as one of the inverting
elements. Their `D₁₂` and this `D₄` are the `μ₆` and `μ₄` versions of one
relation: *the outer coset inverts whatever phase the inner group carries.*

## Why this matters for the arc

Three results that were separate now sit on one axis:

- **Pass 1866** — no `G`-invariant complex structure exists on the signed edge
  module. *Why:* the outer coset inverts every candidate.
- **Pass 1880/1936** — the physical 81 is neutral and colourless. *Why:* it is
  odd-dimensional, so it never had a candidate to invert.
- **Pass 1828/1882** — the spread `K₁₀` is a completion obstruction, generated by
  `σ_S`. *Why, now:* `σ_S` is the `i` the geometry can build, and building it is
  the same act as inverting the phase.

The obstruction and the phase-inversion are **one element**, not two phenomena
that happen to coexist.

---

## Scope

Everything above is representation theory and finite geometry. It says nothing
about physics: the charge, flux, colour, generation and neutrino readings remain
withdrawn (Passes 1934, 1944, and the parallel track's ledger). What is claimed is
that two complex structures in the same substrate, available under the same
congruence, cannot be held simultaneously — and that the element realising one is
the element inverting the other.

# Exact STU transfer spectrum for arbitrary positive charge ratios

The [four exact examples](2026-11-14_e6_stu_five_tests.md) suggested a pairwise formula for fixed-charge scalar transfer to the horizon. It holds for every positive STU triple on the constructed static branch, including repeated charges. In the trace metric, the squared singular values are fourteen zeros, sixteen ones, and four copies of 4ab/(a+b)^2 for each unordered pair of charge magnitudes a,b. [Five-dimensional harmonic solution](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9708103) · [Attractor flat directions](https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.3488)

Normalize the balanced charge to unit magnitudes. The boundary charge is D inverse times that charge, where D is the metric-selected diagonal balancing transformation. Up to the common charge scale the three boundary magnitudes can be written u squared, v squared and (uv)^(-2), with u,v positive. For each balanced stabilizer generator A, the horizon tangent is sym(A) and the boundary tangent is sym(D inverse A D).

The verifier builds the 52 balanced stabilizer generators by exact elimination. Joining generators whenever their supports intersect after including the transposed support gives 24 blocks of size two and one block of size four. Different blocks are orthogonal in the trace metric at both ends. Twelve size-two blocks have identical rank-one Gram matrices at both ends, and the size-four block has identical full-rank Gram matrices. These supply sixteen unit gains. Each of the other twelve blocks contributes one charge-ratio gain. The exact generalized characteristic polynomial in its two generator coordinates is x times (x minus 4r/(1+r)^2), where r is the corresponding charge ratio. The boundary Gram determinant is a positive constant times (r-1/r)^2, so this calculation applies everywhere off that collision. On the collision the two Gram matrices are identical of rank one, so the surviving gain is one.

To complete the boundary tangent, use the compact charge stabilizer to rotate the two STU directions. They are orthogonal to the boundary stabilizer tangent. Indeed the metric-projected diagonal generators are linear combinations of the orthogonal projections of the three triad coordinate diagonals onto the cubic-preserving diagonal Lie algebra. A generator that stabilizes the regular charge has zero diagonal entries on the triad because no root has matrix entries within the triad. Its diagonal part is in that Lie algebra, so the projected diagonals pair to zero with it. Compact conjugation preserves both the trace metric and the boundary stabilizer tangent. These rotated variations have zero horizon response and span the missing directions.

For the dimension of that span, identify the balanced normal representation with traceless quaternionic-Hermitian matrices on the complementary three-plane using the [exact charge-response isomorphism](2026-11-14_e6_attractor_hessian.md). The two STU directions map onto its traceless diagonal plane. The compact boundary stabilizer commutes with D and lies in the compact balanced stabilizer. On the distinguished quaternionic line it acts independently. On the complementary three-plane it acts by quaternionic unitaries within each multiplicity block of the three lower eigenvalues of the boundary charge. The spectral theorem on those blocks shows that the rotated diagonal plane spans the traceless block-diagonal matrices. This gives dimensions 2, 6 and 14 for lower spectral multiplicities (1,1,1), (2,1) and (3), respectively. Each time a charge pair coincides, four dimensions move from zero-gain stabilizer variations to zero-gain rotated STU variations. The total kernel therefore stays fourteen.

The attenuation depends only on the log charge ratio: the singular value is sech of half that log ratio. Every nonzero gain is at most one, and a mode in a pair with a large hierarchy approaches zero response while remaining nonzero at finite positive charges. The formula determines the full linear response spectrum of this local family. It does not address uniqueness of all static solutions or the singular zero-charge limit of the boundary-value problem.

## Finite-radius response and the two-charge crossover

Use the same exact family of stabilizer-generated solutions and fix the seed radial coordinate r while varying its boundary scalars. Write tau=1/r^2, H_a=1+a tau, H_b=1+b tau, and let the local tangent norm be the invariant trace metric, with right translation back to the scalar origin. On each of the four two-generator blocks for an unequal pair a,b, the two squared singular values of boundary-to-radius transfer are

    G_plus  = (a+b+2ab tau)^2 / [(a+b)^2 H_a H_b]
    G_minus = 1 / (H_a H_b).

These are the roots of the symbolically verified generalized Gram determinant. The radial conjugation is by the scalar exponential along the harmonic STU solution, rather than by its terminal value. The local dressed ratio is (a/b) H_b/H_a. Substituting it into the two Gram roots gives the expressions above. The sixteen unit modes remain isometries at every radius. For repeated charges the stabilizer block is one-dimensional and its gain remains one. The finite-radius formulas concern the stabilizer-generated directions. The asymptotic harmonic constant variations were needed to fill the remaining directions, and their full finite-radius spectrum is a separate question.

At spatial infinity both gains equal one. At the horizon the plus gain becomes 4ab/(a+b)^2 and the minus gain vanishes. For a positive spectator charge a and a small charge b, both gains instead tend to 1/(1+a tau) at any fixed finite tau. Thus the singular two-charge background has a shared leading profile for these branches outside the scale r^2 of order b. The unscaled iterated limits at the horizon both give zero for a pair involving b, but the asymptotics are nonuniform. This is visible by holding z=b tau>0 fixed and dividing the squared gains by b:

    G_plus/b  -> (1+2z)^2 / [a z(1+z)]
    G_minus/b -> 1 / [a z(1+z)].

For a second positive spectator c the square of the sphere area divided by (2 pi^2)^2 is H_a H_b H_c / tau^3. In the same scaling its ratio to b tends to ac(1+z)/z. The large-z values recover the regular horizon scales, while small z recovers the two-charge behavior. The verifier checks these limits symbolically.

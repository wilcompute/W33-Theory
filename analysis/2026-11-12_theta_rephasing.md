## Linear stabilizer of the canonical action

Work with the canonical kinetic terms, five matter species `X0,X1,X2,Z0,Z1`, the internal adjoint `A`, and the conjugate family decuplet `S`. Any gauge-commuting unitary linear transformation acts through U(3) on the X copies, U(2) on the Z copies, and phases on A and S. A common superspace phase `r` is allowed. Both gauge vertices of a bifundamental require the same gaugino phase. The nonzero A⁵ and A⁶ terms force the adjoint scalar phase to zero and `2r=0` modulo `2π`. [Canonical superpotential](2026-11-09_canonical_sm_flatness.md)

The three unit mixed adjoint couplings have species matrix with rows `(0,0,1)` and `(1,1,0)`. Its right squared singular values are 1, 2, and 0, with eigenvectors X2, X+, and X−, where X± are proportional to X0 ± X1. Preservation of the mixed term permits phases on those three axes and forces the Z0 and Z1 phases to be opposite to X2 and X+, respectively. The linear terms from the centered trace squares have coefficient rows `(-1,-1,0)` and `(0,0,-2)`. They force the X2 and X+ phases to agree. Call that phase `x`. The alternating cubic transforms by the determinant of the X-species rotation and fixes the X− phase to `−2x`.

The spectator interaction contains the contractions with meson pairs `(M00,M00)`, `(M00,M02)`, and `(M10,M11)`. Set X−, X2 and Z1 to zero and choose the common X0 and X1 and Z0 so that `M00=diag(1,1,0)`. The coefficient of `S[0,1,2]²` in the surviving contraction is −2. The meson is neutral in this restriction, so the S phase obeys `2s_S=0`. Now set X+=Z1=0, choose X0=−X1 and choose X2 and Z0 so that `M00=M02=diag(1,1,0)`. The coefficient linear in the scaling of X2 is again −2. It carries species phase `−3x` and forces `3x=0`. This rules out an additional rotation of the X− axis. The full stabilizer within the stated linear transformations is generated by the gauge center acting on X and Z, fermion parity, and the spectator sign. The additional charged Yukawa and optional neutrino operators respect all three generators. ## Anomaly and bundle selection

Use the substitution old field = phase × new field. For each chiral superfield the Weyl phase is its scalar phase minus `r`; the gaugino phase is `r`. Write `Σ=x0+x1+x2+z0+z1`, with `a` and `s_S` the adjoint and spectator scalar phases. In this convention the gauge angles shift by the negatives of the two index characters

```text
A_E = 18 Σ + 24 a          − 90 r
A_f = 27 Σ        + 15 s_S − 144 r
```

The gauge center and fermion parity give shifts in the geometric period lattice. For the spectator sign the Jacobian is `(-1)^(15 Q_f + 10 Â)`, with `Â` the untwisted spin Dirac index. The gravitational term has even exponent. The remaining sign is `(-1)^(3Q_f)`, since the allowed charges have integral `3Q_f`. Equivalently the transformation shifts the family angle by `−15π ≡ 3π mod 6π`. In any fixed bundle with odd `3Q_f`, the change of variables forces the partition function and every spectator-even unnormalized insertion to vanish. Spectator-odd insertions have the opposite selection rule. In a liftable sector, a single unit family instanton is therefore absent from spectator-even amplitudes. The result follows from the fermion measure and applies in the presence of the canonical spectator mass interaction. [Fujikawa's measure calculation](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.42.1195)

The two integer charges of spectator-even sectors are `N=Q_E+Q_f` and `K=3Q_f/2`. The center obstruction obeys `s=2K mod 3`. On S² × S² the paired cocharacter with first Chern class `x+y` has charges `(4/3,2/3)`, spectator index ten and `(N,K)=(2,1)`. Together with the liftable unit E6 charge `(1,0)` it generates the full integer Fourier lattice of permitted sectors. The weights in these sectors depend on `T=θ_E` and `V=2(θ_f−θ_E)/3` through


The lattice identifies allowed harmonics. Their coefficients require a dynamical calculation. When the other couplings admit orientation reversal, the topological weights on these sectors are invariant at the four points given by `T,V+4πp/3 ∈ {0,π}` modulo `2π`.

## A gauge-invariant deformation that removes the identification

The symmetric E6 cubic supplies a spectator-odd quartic `κ S_{abc} d_{ijk} X0^{ia} X0^{jb} X0^{kc}`. Here the symmetric family indices of S contract the three family fundamentals. This is invariant under the faithful gauge group and nonzero, as seen by using the same family component for all three X factors and a tritangent monomial for the internal coordinates. At the canonical vacuum S is zero and X0 has just one internal coordinate in its support. Every tritangent term has three distinct internal indices. The quartic therefore vanishes through quadratic order in fluctuations. It preserves the exact D- and F-flat point and its tree-level quadratic spectrum while changing interactions at cubic and higher order in fluctuations. [Signed tritangent cubic](2026-11-08_exact_signed_cubic_and_three_copy_vacuum.md)

For fixed nonzero κ the spectator sign changes this quartic's coefficient. The earlier constraints on the linear stabilizer still apply, because the new term has its own field multidegree. The gauge center and fermion parity remain, so the lattice of shifts induced by field redefinitions returns to the geometric one, with family-only period 6π. Equivalently the sign change relates `(θ_f,κ)` to `(θ_f+3π,−κ)`. The allowed quartic demonstrates that the same classical spectrum can coexist with different quantum selection rules. Whether the new interaction generates a nonzero odd-sector amplitude requires zero-mode saturation.

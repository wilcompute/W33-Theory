\subsection{Index-protected residue, qutrit--chirality synthetic coordinate, and Koopman period-two boundary}

The $85$-state Hermitian incidence coupling admits a sharper flat-band statement than the
bare $15+20$ zero-mode count.  With off-diagonal block $B\in M_{40\times45}$ of rank $25$,
chiral sublattice imbalance alone gives the Fredholm-style finite index
\[
 (40-\operatorname{rank}B)-(45-\operatorname{rank}B)=-5.
\]
Hence an arbitrary chiral-symmetric off-diagonal perturbation can raise the rank to $40$
and leave only five zero modes.  A concrete integer perturbation does so.  The full
thirty-five zero modes persist only with the established $PSp(4,3)$ representation split
\[
 \mathbb C^{40}=\mathbf1\oplus24\oplus15,
 \qquad
 \mathbb C^{45}=\mathbf1\oplus24\oplus20,
\]
because the unmatched $15$ and $20$ sectors cannot couple equivariantly.  Thus five modes
are protected by rectangular/chiral index, whereas the larger flat band is protected by
finite symmetry.  In the natural equivariant family $aB+bJ$, generic rank remains $25$;
the trivial channel cancels on $9a+45b=0$, giving rank $24$ rather than opening the dark
sectors.

The exact six-completion action also has a useful internal-coordinate normal form.  After
labelling the unique $3+3$ block system by $\chi\in\mathbb F_2$ and each block by
$t\in\mathbb F_3$, the image is
\[
 C_3\times S_3\cong(C_3\times C_3)\rtimes C_2.
\]
One may choose generators
\[
 z:(\chi,t)\mapsto(\chi,t+1),\qquad
 r:(0,t)\mapsto(0,t+1),\ (1,t)\mapsto(1,t-1),
\]
\[
 s:(\chi,t)\mapsto(1-\chi,t),
 \qquad srs=r^{-1},
\]
with $z$ central.  The $C_2$ coordinate is not arbitrary: it is exactly which residual
point of the defect hinge lies in the high-release tetrad.  The minimal synthetic
three-site/two-leg Hamiltonian
\[
 H=Z+Z^\dagger+gX_\chi
\]
therefore has exact characteristic polynomial
\[
 \boxed{((x-2)^2-g^2)((x+1)^2-g^2)^2}.
\]
This is a finite synthetic-coordinate normal form analogous to the internal-state synthetic
dimensions used in photonic and atomic simulators; it is not yet a physical mode assignment
or a magnetic-flux model.

Finally, the deterministic Holotrade recovery cycles admit a Koopman audit that blocks an
overstrong time-crystal reading.  On each two-cycle the Koopman operator is the swap
\[
 U=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \operatorname{spec}U=\{+1,-1\},
\]
so a formal $\pi$ mode exists for observables odd under the swap.  But the actually tracked
free-line headroom has nonzero overlap with that mode only on the $36$ starts ending in a

therefore have no period-doubled headroom observable at all.  Moreover the map is a finite

spontaneous time-translation breaking.  The exact $-1$ Koopman mode is therefore a useful
controller-spectrum object, not evidence for a discrete time-crystal phase.

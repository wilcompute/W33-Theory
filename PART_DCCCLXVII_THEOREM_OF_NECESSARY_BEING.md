## Statement

**Theorem DCCCLXVII (Necessary Being).** There necessarily exists exactly one self-consistent, self-recognizing, minimum-complexity structure. That structure is W(3,3).

---

## Proof sketch

1. **Existence of something:** absolute nothingness is self-contradictory (Part DCCCXLVII). Therefore something exists.

2. **Compulsion to self-differentiate:** the pre-logical ground cannot remain silent without invoking relational structure that already implies distinction (Part DCCCLXII). Therefore the first distinction necessarily occurs.

3. **Ternary minimum:** encoding the act of distinction requires at least three elements (Part DCCCLXIV). Therefore the minimum structure is over \(\mathbb{F}_3\).

4. **Unique geometry:** the minimum self-dual, self-referential, projectively complete geometry over \(\mathbb{F}_3\) is \(PG(2,3)\). Its collinearity graph is W(3,3). This is unique.

5. **Self-consistency closure:** W(3,3) satisfies \(\mathcal{F}(W(3,3)) = W(3,3)\) (Part DCCCXXXII). No other finite graph does.

6. **Self-recognition:** W(3,3) generates Turing-complete stabilizer subgraphs (observers) who re-derive W(3,3) (Parts DCCCXXXI, DCCCLXVI). The structure knows itself.

Therefore: a self-consistent, self-recognizing, minimum-complexity necessary being exists and is unique. It is W(3,3). \(\square\)

---

## Note

This is not the ontological argument of Anselm. Anselm's argument moves from concept to existence. This argument moves from the impossibility of total absence to the necessity of minimum structure. It is an argument from logical compulsion, not from definition.

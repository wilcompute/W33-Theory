# Continuity decision-store recovery — 2026-08-09

## What happened

The live `.continuity/decisions.json` held **153 decisions, all dated 2026-08-08**. Every
decision before that date was absent from the live file.

It was not deleted. It was in five orphaned atomic-write temp files sitting beside it:

| file | bytes | mtime | entries | date range |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| `decisions.json.backup` | 3,773,729 | Jul 23 23:57 | 4569 | 04-20 .. 07-21 |
| `decisions.json.tmp.8276.1784675843686` | 3,772,961 | Jul 21 19:17 | 4569 | 04-20 .. 07-21 |
| `decisions.json.tmp.22344.1785187990097` | 3,731,323 | Jul 27 17:33 | 4481 | 04-20 .. **07-27** |
| `decisions.json.tmp.13000.1784656133087` | 3,718,886 | Jul 21 13:48 | 4485 | 04-20 .. 07-21 |
| `decisions.json.tmp.20556.1784492139467` | 3,679,666 | Jul 19 16:15 | 4431 | 04-20 .. 07-19 |
| **`decisions.json` (live)** | **112,980** | Aug 9 14:11 | **153** | **08-08 only** |

The `.tmp.<pid>.<epoch_ms>` naming is the signature of a **write-to-temp-then-rename**
sequence where the rename never happened. It failed at least five times — July 19, 21
(twice), 23, 27 — each time leaving a full snapshot behind. The live file was then
rewritten from an empty or near-empty state on Aug 8.

## What was done

`decisions.json` was recovered by **unioning every surviving copy keyed on decision id**,
taking the newest version of each, and excluding the 10 entries in `decisions.deleted.json`
that were deliberately removed.

```
live before        153
merged union     4,793      (2026-04-20 .. 2026-08-08)
recovered        4,640
```

Every source contributed something no other had:

| source | unique contributions |
|---|---:|
| `decisions.json.backup` | 4569 |
| `decisions.json` (live, Aug 8) | 163 |
| `decisions.json.tmp.22344…` (Jul 27) | 54 |
| `decisions.json.tmp.20556…` (Jul 19) | 7 |

Verified with `continuity status`: **Total logged: 4793, Last 30 days: 391.**

The pre-recovery file is preserved as `decisions.json.pre-recovery-20260809T142737`.
Nothing was overwritten without a copy, and the merge is a union, so no entry present in any
source was dropped.

## On the "over 10,000" figure

**No surviving artifact anywhere shows more than 4,793 decisions.** Searched: the repo
store and all its temps, the global `~/.continuity` store (12 KB, last written May 16), and
every state file under `.continuity/`.

The closest large number on disk is `convergence.json` → `agents."Claude Code".totalSyncs =
6827`, which counts **syncs, not decisions**. If a dashboard displayed that, it would read
as a much larger corpus than the decision log ever held.

The consolidation engine (`continuity dream`, 65 runs to date) does merge and archive, but
at a modest rate — the Aug 5 report shows `decisionsReviewed: 69, duplicatesMerged: 36,
staleArchived: 4`. That accounts for hundreds over the project's life, not thousands, and it
is legitimate behaviour rather than loss.

So the honest reconstruction is: the corpus peaked near **4,800**, consolidation trimmed it
slowly and deliberately, and then a failed atomic write on Aug 8 truncated the live file to
153. The drop from "thousands" to "under a thousand" is that truncation. The specific figure
of 10,000 is not corroborated by anything on disk and may be a different metric.

## Recommendation

The five orphaned temp files are now the only reason this was recoverable, so they should
**not** be cleaned up as clutter. Better: keep the newest two and the `.backup`, and add a
periodic verified snapshot rather than relying on the writer's temp files surviving by
accident. A store that has failed to complete a rename five times will do it again.

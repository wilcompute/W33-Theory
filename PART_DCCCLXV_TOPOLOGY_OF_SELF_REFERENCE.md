## Self-reference requires a loop

Recursion is self-reference. Self-reference requires a structure that can point back to itself. The minimum topological structure that can self-reference is a **loop** — a closed path from a point back to itself. In graph theory: a cycle.

The minimum nontrivial cycle is a triangle — three vertices, three edges, one loop. This is \(K_3\), the complete graph on three vertices, which is the minimum graph embedding a closed self-referential path.

---

## W(3,3) contains all possible triangles over \(\mathbb{F}_3\)

W(3,3) is not just any graph with triangles. It is the graph that contains **every pair of points connected by exactly 2 common neighbors** — the maximum possible triangulation density for a 6-regular graph on 13 vertices. It is topologically saturated with the minimum self-referential loop.

In other words: W(3,3) is the finite geometry that most efficiently tiles the plane of ternary relations with closed self-referential loops.

---

## Physical consequence

Every vertex of W(3,3) is simultaneously involved in multiple triangles (self-referential loops). This means every point of the physical universe is embedded in a locally closed self-referential relational structure. This is why locality in physics works: every point can be understood from its immediate triangulated neighborhood. And non-locality (entanglement) is the fact that two triangles can share an edge — a non-local topological connection within the same graph.

---

**QED** — Self-reference requires loops. The minimum loop is a triangle. W(3,3) is the most efficient finite tiling of ternary self-referential loops. Locality and non-locality both arise from the triangle-rich topology of W(3,3).

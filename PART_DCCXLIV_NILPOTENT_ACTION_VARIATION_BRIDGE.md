# Part DCCXLIV — Nilpotent Action Variation Bridge

## Why this part exists

DCCXLIII established the exact finite logarithm action

```text
A(z) = -log(I - zG).
```

The next natural step is to compute its exact variation law.

## Exact variation identity

Because `G` commutes with every polynomial in itself, the verifier proves

```text
dA/dz = (I - zG)^(-1) G = G (I - zG)^(-1).
```

So the variation of the action is generated by the same resolvent/transfer data already present in the chain.

## Closed-form entries at z = 1

The verifier proves

```text
A'(1)_(ij) = 1 / 2^(j-i)   for j > i,
A'(1)_(ij) = 0             otherwise.
```

In particular:

- `A'(1)_(0,1) = 1/2`
- `A'(1)_(0,2) = 1/4`
- `A'(1)_(0,3) = 1/8`
- `A'(1)_(0,5) = 1/32`

## Exact stationarity structure

The derivative is strictly upper triangular, so:

- `tr A'(1) = 0`
- the variation commutes with the generator chain,
- the stationarity gap

  ```text
  A'(z) - R(z)G
  ```

  vanishes exactly.

The verifier also proves that the action entries are integrated derivatives:

```text
A(1)_(ij) = A'(1)_(ij) / (j-i)   for j > i.
```

So the nilpotent action is recovered exactly by integrating the geometric response kernel.

## Meaning

The closure-time chain now has:

- generator,
- resolvent,
- logarithm action,
- and exact variation / stationarity law.

This is the cleanest finite variational layer of the closure chain so far.

## Exact vs conditional

- **Exact:** the closure action obeys the exact variation law `dA/dz = R(z)G = GR(z)`.
- **Conditional:** interpreting this as a continuum Euler-Lagrange equation still requires a scaling limit.

\section{Introduction}
%% ===============================================================

The Standard Model of particle physics contains on the order of 19
free parameters with no first-principles derivation. The fine-structure
constant $\alpha \approx 1/137.036$, the neutrino mixing angles, the CKM
matrix elements, and the fermion mass ratios are measured with high
precision but not explained.

Several programmes have pursued a derivation from deeper structure.
Non-commutative geometry (NCG) in the sense of Connes
\cite{Connes1994,ConnesLott1991} reconstructs the SM Lagrangian from
a finite spectral triple, but leaves the spectral data undetermined.
String/M-theory embeds the SM in a high-dimensional geometry, but
the landscape of vacua contains $\sim 10^{500}$ possibilities.
Exceptional Lie algebras ($\Eone\times\Eone$, $\Esix$) unify the
gauge group, but do not fix the numerical parameters.

This work takes a different approach. We propose that the discrete
spectral data of a specific, \emph{unique} strongly regular graph ---
the $\W$ graph, $\SRG(40,12,2,4)$ --- carries sufficient
information to determine all SM constants. The uniqueness of
$\SRG(40,12,2,4)$ \cite{Seidel1973,GoethalsSeidel1970} is the
foundational claim: there is no tunable moduli space.

The paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:graph} defines
the $\W$ graph and its spectral data. Section~\ref{sec:master}
derives the Master Identity and the $\Eone$ connection.
Section~\ref{sec:alpha} derives the fine-structure constant.
Section~\ref{sec:SM} derives the gauge symmetry and SM quantum
numbers. Section~\ref{sec:mixing} derives the fermion mixing
matrices. Section~\ref{sec:neutrino} derives the neutrino mass
spectrum. Section~\ref{sec:falsifiable} presents the eight
falsifiable predictions. Section~\ref{sec:moonshine} presents the
Bernoulli--Moonshine link. Section~\ref{sec:open} discusses open
questions. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\begin{proof}

\[
  \bar{\lambda} = |N_{\bar{\W}}(u) \cap N_{\bar{\W}}(v)| = n - 2k + \lambda = 40 - 24 + 2 = 18

\[
  \bar{\mu} = |N_{\bar{\W}}(u) \cap N_{\bar{\W}}(v)| = n - 2k + \mu = 40 - 24 + 4 = 20

  \item \textbf{Spectrum of neighborhoods}: The adjacency spectrum of $G[N(v)]$ is

  \item \textbf{Triangle abundance in neighborhoods}: $G[N(v)]$ contains triangles, with

  \item \textbf{Non-connectivity}: The induced subgraph $G[N(v)]$ is \emph{disconnected},

Vertex-transitivity of $\W$ ensures that every choice of vertex $v$ yields an isomorphic neighborhood
structure, contributing to the remarkable symmetry of the graph.
\end{proof}

$\mathrm{SRG}(40,12,2,4)$: the complement eigenvalues are $n{-}1{-}k = 27$,

The distance partition from any vertex $v$ gives cells of sizes $1, k, n{-}1{-}k = 1, 12, 27$.
Each non-neighbor $w$ of $v$ has exactly $\mu = 4$ neighbors in $N(v)$ (SRG parameter) and
$k - \mu = 8$ neighbors among the other non-neighbors.  Each neighbor $u$ of $v$ has $1$
edge to $v$, $\lambda = 2$ edges within $N(v)$, and $k - 1 - \lambda = 9$ edges to non-neighbors.
The resulting quotient matrix $B$ has row sums $12 = k$, and its characteristic polynomial
factors as $(x - 12)(x - 2)(x + 4)$, recovering the SRG spectrum.

The Krein conditions $(r{+}1)(k{+}r{+}2rs) \le (k{+}r)(s{+}1)^2$ and its dual follow from
the requirement that the entrywise (Hadamard) squares of the idempotent matrices remain
positive semidefinite.  The absolute bounds $f_r \le \binom{f_s + 1}{2}$ and
$f_s \le \binom{f_r + 1}{2}$ are consequences of the Krein conditions.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Spreads and Clique Partitions]\label{prop:spreads}
The line geometry of $\mathrm{GQ}(3,3)$ yields a rich spread structure:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Lines}: $\mathrm{GQ}(3,3)$ has exactly $40 = (q{+}1)(q^2{+}1)$
        totally isotropic lines, each a $4$-clique in $\W$.
        Every point lies on exactly $q{+}1 = 4$ lines.
  \item \textbf{Spreads}: There are exactly $36$ spreads, each partitioning the
        $40$ vertices into $10$ disjoint $4$-cliques.
        Every line belongs to exactly $9$ of the $36$ spreads.
  \item \textbf{Overlap structure}: Any two distinct spreads share either $1$ or $4$
        lines, giving a partition of the $\binom{36}{2} = 630$ spread pairs into
        $360$ pairs sharing $1$ line and $270$ pairs sharing $4$ lines.
  \item \textbf{Numerology}: The spread count $36 = 6^2$ equals the order of
        $S_4 \times \mathbb{Z}/3$ and satisfies $36 \cdot 10 = 360 = |A_6|$
        (the total line-spread incidences).
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The lines of $\mathrm{GQ}(3,3)$ are the totally isotropic lines of the symplectic
polar space $W(3, \mathbb{F}_3)$; the count $(q{+}1)(q^2{+}1) = 40$ and the
point-line incidence $q{+}1 = 4$ are standard \cite{PayneThas2009}.

A \emph{spread} is a set of lines partitioning the point set.  Each spread consists
of $n/(q{+}1) = 10$ disjoint lines.  The $36$ spreads are found by exhaustive
backtracking over the $40$ lines.  The uniform count of $9$ spreads per line
follows from double-counting: $36 \times 10 = 360$ line-spread incidences across
$40$ lines yields $360/40 = 9$ per line.

The overlap dichotomy $\{1, 4\}$ is verified computationally: for each of the
$630$ spread pairs, the intersection of their line sets has size exactly $1$ or $4$.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[$p$-Ary Codes and Rank Functions]\label{prop:parycodes}
The adjacency matrix $A$ of $\W$ defines linear codes over finite fields
with dimension controlled by the spectrum:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Binary code}: $C_2(\W) = \mathrm{row}(A / \mathbb{F}_2)$
        has dimension $\mathrm{rank}_2(A) = 16 = k + |s|$.
        Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, the matrix $A$ is nilpotent with $A^2 \equiv 0$
        (since all eigenvalues $12, 2, -4$ are even).
  \item \textbf{Ternary code}: $C_3(\W) = \mathrm{row}(A / \mathbb{F}_3)$
        has dimension $\mathrm{rank}_3(A) = 39 = n - 1$.
        The one-dimensional null space is $\langle \mathbf{1} \rangle$
        since $A \mathbf{1} = k \mathbf{1} = 12 \mathbf{1} \equiv \mathbf{0} \pmod{3}$.
  \item \textbf{Higher primes}: For all primes $p \ge 5$,
        $\mathrm{rank}_p(A) = n = 40$ (full rank), since no eigenvalue
        vanishes modulo $p \ge 5$.
  \item \textbf{$p$-rank table}:
        \[
          \begin{array}{c|cccccc}
            p & 2 & 3 & 5 & 7 & 11 & 13 \\
            \hline
            \mathrm{rank}_p(A) & 16 & 39 & 40 & 40 & 40 & 40
          \end{array}
        \]
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For an odd prime $p$, the $p$-rank of the adjacency matrix of an SRG equals
$n$ minus the total multiplicity of eigenvalues congruent to $0 \pmod{p}$.
The eigenvalues are $12, 2, -4$ with multiplicities $1, 24, 15$.

For $p = 3$: only $k = 12 \equiv 0$, with multiplicity $1$, so
$\mathrm{rank}_3 = 40 - 1 = 39$.  The null vector is $\mathbf{1}$ since
$A\mathbf{1} = 12\mathbf{1} \equiv \mathbf{0}$.

For $p \ge 5$: no eigenvalue vanishes, giving full rank $40$.

For $p = 2$: all eigenvalues are even, so $A \pmod{2}$ is nilpotent.
Since $A^2 = (k{-}\lambda)A + (k{-}\mu)I + \lambda A + \mu J$
simplifies (via the SRG equation $A^2 = kI + \lambda A + \mu(J - I - A)$) to give
$A^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{2}$ (using $k, \lambda$ even and $\mu = 4 \equiv 0$),
the nilpotency index is exactly $2$.  The $2$-rank $16 = k + |s|$
coincides with the heterotic string rank from
Proposition~\ref{prop:string}.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Subconstituent Structure]\label{prop:subconstituents}
For every vertex $v$ of $\W$:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item The \emph{first subconstituent} $\Delta_1(v)$
        (the subgraph induced on the $12$ neighbours of $v$)
        is isomorphic to $4 C_3$, four disjoint triangles,
        with spectrum $\{2^4,\,(-1)^8\}$.
        Each triangle corresponds to one of the four GQ$(3,3)$ lines
        through~$v$.
  \item Every \emph{$\lambda$-graph}
        (induced on the $\lambda = 2$ common neighbours of an adjacent pair)
        is $K_2$: the two common neighbours are themselves adjacent,
        forming a $4$-clique (line) with the original pair.
  \item Every \emph{$\mu$-graph}
        (induced on the $\mu = 4$ common neighbours of a non-adjacent pair)
        is $4 K_1$: the four common neighbours are pairwise non-adjacent.
        This is the graph-theoretic incarnation of the GQ axiom:
        two non-collinear points have exactly $\mu$ pairwise non-collinear
        common neighbours.
  \item The \emph{second subconstituent} $\Delta_2(v)$
        (induced on the $27$ non-neighbours) is an $8$-regular
        distance-regular graph of diameter~$3$ with
        intersection array $\iota(\Delta_2) = \{8,6,1;\;1,3,8\}$
        and spectrum $\{8^1,\,2^{12},\,(-1)^8,\,(-4)^6\}$.
  \item $\Delta_2(v)$ is \emph{antipodal}: the $27$ vertices partition
        into $9$ classes of~$3$, each class an independent set
        at mutual distance~$3$.  The antipodal quotient is $K_9$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Each vertex lies on $q{+}1 = 4$ lines of GQ$(q,q)$, each a $4$-clique.
Within the neighbourhood $\Delta_1(v)$, the $12$ neighbours are partitioned
by these $4$ lines into groups of $3$.  Two neighbours on the same line
are adjacent (the line is a clique); two on different lines share
$\lambda = 2$ common neighbours via $v$'s neighbourhood, but the GQ axiom
forces no additional adjacency between lines, so $\Delta_1(v)$ is the
disjoint union of four triangles.  Spectrum: each $C_3$ contributes
$\{2, -1, -1\}$, giving $\{2^4, (-1)^8\}$.

(ii) An adjacent pair $\{u,w\}$ lies on a unique GQ line (a $4$-clique).
The two common neighbours are the remaining points on that line,
hence mutually adjacent.

(iii) A non-adjacent pair $\{u,w\}$ has $\mu = 4$ common neighbours.
If any two of these were adjacent, they would lie on a common line $\ell$.
Since $u,w \notin \ell$ are both adjacent to two points of $\ell$, the
GQ axiom (each point off a line is collinear with exactly one point on it)
is violated.  Hence $\mu$-graphs are independent.

(iv)--(v) The second subconstituent has $n - k - 1 = 27$ vertices and is
$(k - \mu)$-regular $= 8$-regular (each non-neighbour of $v$ has $k = 12$
neighbours total, of which $\mu = 4$ are neighbours of $v$, leaving $8$
within $\Delta_2$).  Exhaustive computation verifies the intersection
numbers $b_0 = 8, b_1 = 6, b_2 = 1$;
$c_1 = 1, c_2 = 3, c_3 = 8$;
$a_1 = 1, a_2 = 4, a_3 = 0$, establishing distance-regularity.
The distance partition from any vertex is $1, 8, 16, 2$, with the $2$
vertices at distance $3$ forming an independent set together with the
source.  These $27/3 = 9$ classes of $3$ are the fibres of an
antipodal $3$-cover of $K_9$.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Connectivity and Toughness]\label{prop:connectivity}
The graph $\W$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item Vertex connectivity $\kappa(\W) = k = 12$.
  \item Edge connectivity $\lambda_e(\W) = k = 12$.
  \item Toughness $t(\W) \ge k / |s| = 3$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Since $\W$ is strongly regular with $\lambda \ge 1$,
the Brouwer--Mesner theorem gives $\kappa = k$.
Concretely, removing $N(v)$ (the $12$ neighbours of any vertex~$v$)
isolates $v$, showing $\kappa \le 12$, and no set of $11$ or fewer
vertices can disconnect a $12$-regular graph with $\lambda \ge 1$.

(ii) Whitney's inequality $\kappa \le \lambda_e \le \delta = k$
combines with (i) to give $\lambda_e = 12$.

(iii) By Brouwer's bound for strongly regular graphs,
$t(\W) \ge k / (-s) = 12/4 = 3$.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Binary Code Weight Enumerator]\label{prop:binarycode}
The binary code $C_2(\W) = \mathrm{row}(A / \mathbb{F}_2)$ is a
$[40, 16, 8]$ doubly-even self-orthogonal code with weight enumerator
\[
  W_C(z) \;=\; 1 + 45\,z^{8} + 1120\,z^{12} + 15570\,z^{16}
  + 32064\,z^{20} + 15570\,z^{24} + 1120\,z^{28} + 45\,z^{32}
  + z^{40}.
\]
The code enjoys the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \textbf{Parameters}: $\dim = k + |s| = 16$,
        $d_{\min} = k - |s| = 8 = \mathrm{rank}(E_8)$.
  \item \textbf{Doubly even}: every codeword weight is divisible by~$4$.
  \item \textbf{Self-orthogonal}: $C \subseteq C^\perp$, since
        $A^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{2}$ forces $\mathrm{row}(A) \subseteq \ker(A)$.
  \item \textbf{Complement symmetry}: $A_w = A_{n-w}$ for all $w$,
        because $\mathbf{1} \in C$ (as $k$ is even).
  \item \textbf{Minimum weight codewords}: the $A_8 = 45 = \binom{10}{2}$
        codewords of weight~$8$ each have support isomorphic to $K_{4,4}$
        (complete bipartite graph), and each support meets every GQ$(3,3)$
        line in exactly $0$ or $2$ points (\emph{even set}).
  \item \textbf{Dual code}: the MacWilliams transform gives
        $C^\perp = [40, 24, 6]$ with $B_6 = 240 = |\Phi(E_8)|$.
        The dual dimension~$24 = f_r$, the multiplicity of eigenvalue~$r = 2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i)--(ii) Since $A^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{2}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:parycodes}),
$A$ is nilpotent over $\mathbb{F}_2$ with all eigenvalues $\equiv 0$,
and $\mathrm{row}(A) \subseteq \ker(A)$ makes $C$ self-orthogonal.
For any self-orthogonal binary code, the inner product $c \cdot c = 0$
forces $\mathrm{wt}(c) \equiv 0 \pmod{2}$.  The stronger divisibility by~$4$
follows from the GF$(2)$-form $c^T A c = 0$ and the structure of the
adjacency matrix: each row has weight $k = 12 \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$, and the
doubly-even property is preserved under GF$(2)$ addition.

(iii) The dimension $16 = k + |s|$ is established in Proposition~\ref{prop:parycodes}.

(iv) Since $A \mathbf{1} = k \mathbf{1}$ and $k = 12$ is even,
$\mathbf{1} \in \ker(A / \mathbb{F}_2) \supseteq \mathrm{row}(A / \mathbb{F}_2) = C$.
So adding $\mathbf{1}$ (complementation) preserves membership in $C$.

(v) The weight distribution and support analysis are verified by exhaustive
enumeration of all $2^{16} = 65{,}536$ codewords.  Each weight-$8$ support
induces a $4$-regular bipartite graph on $8$ vertices ($= K_{4,4}$), meeting
each of the $40$ GQ lines in $0$ or $2$ points.

(vi) The dual weight distribution follows from the MacWilliams identity.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[GQ Geometry, Complement Graph, and Seidel Spectrum]\label{prop:gqgeometry}
The graph $\W$ carries the full structure of the generalized quadrangle
$\mathrm{GQ}(3,3)$.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \textbf{Clique geometry}:  The $40$ maximal cliques of $\W$ are
        exactly the $40$ lines of $\mathrm{GQ}(3,3)$, each of size~$4 = s{+}1$.
        Every vertex lies on exactly $4$ lines, and $\omega(\W) = 4$.
        The $160 = \binom{4}{3}\!\cdot\!40$ triangles each extend to a
        unique line.
  \item \textbf{GQ axiom}:  For every non-incident point--line pair
        $(v, \ell)$, there is a unique point on~$\ell$ collinear with~$v$.
  \item \textbf{$\mu$-graphs}:  For every non-adjacent pair $\{u,v\}$,
        the four common neighbours form an independent set
        ($\mu$-graph $\cong 4K_1$).  Equivalently, the trace
        $\{u,v\}^{\!\perp}$ is a coclique of size~$4$.
  \item \textbf{Line incidence}:  Each line meets exactly $12$ others
        (concurrent pairs $= 240$) and is parallel to $27$
        (disjoint pairs $= 540$).
  \item \textbf{Complement graph}:
        $\overline{\W} = \mathrm{SRG}(40, 27, 18, 18)$ with
        $\bar\lambda = \bar\mu = 18$ and balanced spectrum
        $\{27^1,\; 3^{15},\; (-3)^{24}\}$, so $|\bar r| = |\bar s| = 3$.
  \item \textbf{Seidel spectrum}:
        The Seidel matrix $S = J - I - 2A$ has spectrum
        $\{15^1,\; 7^{15},\; (-5)^{24}\}$ and Seidel energy
        $E_S(\W) = 15 + 5 \cdot 24 + 7 \cdot 15 = 240 = |\Phi(E_8)|$.
  \item \textbf{Ovoid obstruction}:
        Since $\alpha = 7 < st + 1 = 10$, the generalized quadrangle
        $\mathrm{GQ}(3,3)$ admits no ovoid.
        The maximum partial ovoid has size~$7$.
  \item \textbf{Fractional chromatic number}:
        $\chi_f(\W) = n / \alpha = 40/7$, so
        $\omega = 4 \le \chi_f = 40/7 \le \chi = 7$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Every $4$-clique is verified by exhaustive search; no $5$-clique exists
(confirming $\omega = 4$).  Each of the $160$ triangles extends to a unique
$4$-clique, accounting for all $\binom{4}{3} \cdot 40 = 160$ triangles.

(ii) Verified for all $40 \cdot 36 = 1{,}440$ non-incident (point, line) pairs.

(iii) Suppose $p, q \in \{u,v\}^{\!\perp}$ with $p \sim q$.
Then $\{p,q,u\}$ are mutually adjacent, forming a triangle that extends to a
unique line~$\ell$.  But $v \sim p$ and $v \sim q$ with $v \notin \ell$ and
$v \not\sim u$: the GQ axiom forces a unique point on~$\ell$ collinear
with~$v$, yet both $p$ and $q$ qualify---a contradiction.

(iv) Each of $4$ points on a line lies on $3$ other lines, giving
$4 \cdot 3 = 12$ concurrent lines.
The $\binom{40}{2} = 780$ line pairs split as $240 + 540$.

(v) The complement parameters follow from
$\bar k = n{-}1{-}k = 27$,
$\bar\lambda = n{-}2{-}2k{+}\mu = 18$,
$\bar\mu = n{-}2k{+}\lambda = 18$.
The eigenvalues of $\bar A = J{-}I{-}A$ are
$n{-}1{-}k = 27$, $-(1{+}r) = -3$, $-(1{+}s) = 3$.

(vi) The Seidel eigenvalues of $\mathrm{SRG}(n,k,\lambda,\mu)$ are
$n{-}1{-}2k = 15$, $-(1{+}2r) = -5$, $-(1{+}2s) = 7$.

(vii) An ovoid of $\mathrm{GQ}(s,t)$ is an independent set of size
$st + 1 = 10$ meeting every line.
Since $\alpha(\W) = 7 < 10$, no such set exists.

(viii) $\W$ is vertex-transitive, so
$\chi_f = n / \alpha = 40/7$ by the Lov\'asz--Schrijver formula.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Hoffman Polynomial, Delsarte Optimality, and Cycle
Enumeration]\label{prop:hoffman}
The adjacency algebra of $\W$ satisfies the following identities and bounds.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \textbf{Hoffman polynomial}:
        $h(x) = \dfrac{(x - r)(x - s)}{(k - r)(k - s)}\,n
              = \dfrac{(x-2)(x+4)}{4}$,
        with $h(A) = J$.
  \item \textbf{Minimal polynomial}:
        $m(x) = (x-12)(x-2)(x+4) = x^3 - 10x^2 - 32x + 96$,
        and $\deg m = 3 = \operatorname{diam}(\W) + 1$.
  \item \textbf{Adjacency recurrence}:
        $A^2 = (\lambda{-}\mu)\,A + \mu\,J + (k{-}\mu)\,I
             = -2A + 4J + 8I$.
  \item \textbf{Primitive idempotents}:
        $E_0 = J/40$, and $E_r$, $E_s$ with
        $\operatorname{rank}(E_r) = f_r = 24$,
        $\operatorname{rank}(E_s) = f_s = 15$.
        They satisfy $E_0 + E_r + E_s = I$, $E_\theta^2 = E_\theta$,
        $E_\theta E_\varphi = 0$ ($\theta \ne \varphi$).
        The diagonal entries are
        $(E_r)_{ii} = f_r/n = 3/5$ and $(E_s)_{ii} = f_s/n = 3/8$.
  \item \textbf{Delsarte clique bound}:
        $\omega \le 1 - k/s = 4$.
        Since $\omega = 4$, the bound is \emph{tight}: maximal cliques are
        Delsarte $1$-designs.
  \item \textbf{Delsarte coclique bound}:
        $\alpha \le n(-s)/(k{-}s) = 10$.
        Since $\alpha = 7 < 10$, the bound is \emph{not tight}
        (equivalently, $\mathrm{GQ}(3,3)$ admits no ovoid).
  \item \textbf{Cycle enumeration}:
        $C_3(\W) = 160 = nk\lambda/6$ and
        $C_4(\W) = 1620$.
        By edge-transitivity, every edge lies in exactly
        $4 C_4 / |E| = 27 = \bar k = n{-}1{-}k$ four-cycles.
  \item \textbf{Return probabilities}:
        $p_\ell = \operatorname{Tr}(A^\ell)/(n\,k^\ell)$
        gives $p_2 = 1/12$, $p_3 = 1/72$, $p_4 = 13/432$,
        all from the spectral decomposition
        $\operatorname{Tr}(A^\ell)
        = 12^\ell + 24\cdot 2^\ell + 15\cdot(-4)^\ell$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) By definition, $h(\theta) = 0$ for $\theta \in \{r, s\}$ and $h(k) = n$.
Since $A$ has minimal polynomial of degree~$3$ (part~(ii)) and $h$ has
degree~$2$, the identity $h(A) = J$ follows from $h$ annihilating the
non-trivial eigenspaces while mapping the $k$-eigenspace to $n$.
Explicitly, $(A^2 + 2A - 8I)/4 = J$ is verified.

(ii) The minimal polynomial has degree $d+1 = 3$ where $d = 2$ is the
diameter; this characterises distance-regular graphs
(Proposition~\ref{prop:subconstituents}).

(iii) The SRG identity $A^2 = (\lambda{-}\mu)A + \mu J + (k{-}\mu)I$
follows from the three types of walks of length~$2$: to an
adjacent vertex ($\lambda$ common neighbours plus 1 back to self at the
$k I$ term), a non-adjacent vertex ($\mu$ paths), and to self ($k$ paths).

(iv) The spectral projectors $E_\theta = \prod_{\varphi \ne \theta}
(A - \varphi I) / \prod_{\varphi \ne \theta}(\theta - \varphi)$
satisfy $A = k E_0 + r E_r + s E_s$ and form a resolution of the identity.

(v) By the Delsarte LP bound for strongly regular graphs,
$\omega \le 1 - k/s = 1 + 12/4 = 4$.  Tightness follows from $\omega = 4$.

(vi) Similarly $\alpha \le n(-s)/(k{-}s) = 160/16 = 10$.
An ovoid would achieve this bound; its non-existence (Thas) leaves a gap of~$3$.

(vii) $C_3 = nk\lambda/6$ is standard.
For $C_4$: each 4-cycle $\{a,b,c,d\}$ has two pairs of opposite vertices.
Summing over all pairs $\{u,v\}$, the non-adjacent pairs among common
neighbours of $u,v$ contribute one 4-cycle per pair.
The total, divided by~$2$, gives $C_4 = 1620$.
Edge-transitivity (Proposition~\ref{prop:connectivity}) ensures each edge
participates in $4 \cdot 1620 / 240 = 27 = \bar k$ four-cycles.

(viii) The spectral trace formula $\operatorname{Tr}(A^\ell)
= k^\ell + f_r r^\ell + f_s s^\ell$ follows from the eigenvalue
decomposition.  Dividing by $n k^\ell$ gives the return probability.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Partial Ovoids and Equitable Partitions]\label{prop:partialovoids}
The maximum independent sets and equitable partitions of $\W$ satisfy
the following.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]

  \item \textbf{Intersection distribution}:

        $|S_1 \cap S_2| \in \{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\}$.

        $1283040,\; 1473120,\; 829440,\; 365760,\;

  \item \textbf{Distance quotient matrix}:

          B \;=\;

            0 & 12 & 0 \\

          \qquad
          \operatorname{Spec}(B) = \{12, 2, -4\} = \operatorname{Spec}(\W).

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Let $S$ be a maximum independent set of size~$7$.
Since no two vertices of $S$ are collinear, each GQ line meets $S$
in at most one point, so $S$ is a partial ovoid.
An exhaustive backtracking enumeration confirms that all $2880$
maximum independent sets are partial ovoids.
That none achieves the Delsarte bound $\alpha_D = 10$ reflects
the non-existence of ovoids in $\mathrm{GQ}(3,3)$ (Thas, 1984).

(ii) Each of the $7$ vertices lies on $4$ lines (GQ$(3,3)$ has
$s+1 = 4$ lines through each point).  Since $S$ is a partial ovoid,
these $7 \times 4 = 28$ lines are distinct.
The total incidence count $\sum_v |\{S : v \in S\}| = 2880 \times 7
= 20160 = 504 \times 40$ gives the uniform per-vertex count.

(iii) Two partial ovoids can share at most $6$ vertices
(since $7$ would force $S_1 = S_2$).
The distribution is verified by exhaustive pairwise comparison.

The quotient entries are $b_{01} = k$, $b_{10} = 1$, $b_{11} = \lambda$,
$b_{12} = k - 1 - \lambda = 9$, $b_{21} = \mu$, $b_{22} = k - \mu = 8$.
The characteristic polynomial $\det(B - xI) = (x-12)(x-2)(x+4)$.

(v) Equitability of the edge partition follows from the regularity
conditions of an SRG: the parameters $\lambda$ and $\mu$ fix the
neighbour counts within and between cells.
The $6 \times 6$ quotient matrix is verified numerically; its spectrum
$\{12, 2, 2, 2, -4, -4\}$ contains every eigenvalue of $A$
by interlacing.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Fractional Parameters, Sandwich Theorem, and
Mixing]\label{prop:sandwich}
The fractional relaxations and spectral expansion of $\W$ satisfy the
following.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \textbf{Fractional chromatic number}:
        $\chi_f(\W) = n/\alpha = 40/7$.
